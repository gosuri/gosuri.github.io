## Summary

Community Twitter Space hosted by Nadia Bajuelo (Overclock Labs), introducing new head of product Anil Murty, followed by Greg's weekly updates and an AMA. Greg reports all-time adoption highs (6.5 TB of leased memory, 827 AKT daily spend), attributed largely to Chia miners plotting on Akash at ~10 cents vs. 40 cents on traditional clouds. He highlights ecosystem wins: Sifchain running its frontend on Akash (a DAO-decentralization use case he expects to replicate), Praetor simplifying provider onboarding — including a community member writing a guide on turning idle compute into passive income — and his own July 4th weekend guide to deploying a React app on Akash via buildpacks.io. Anil previews the product roadmap: IP leases (publicly routable IPv4), a provider-service refactor to microservices, a sandbox environment, GPU support spec work, and a low-friction web UI for deployments. Greg then lays out Akash's phased adoption plan: (1) Cosmos-native users, since Akash requires major behavioral change (no email/password/credit card); (2) data-intensive/GPU workloads, notably by sourcing underutilized GPUs freed by the Ethereum merge; (3) performance/edge — putting servers where users are, envisioning "a thousand regions" vs. Amazon's 16-17, since Akash doesn't build data centers but onboards existing ones; and finally the mainstream web2 "trillion dollar market." In the AMA he covers the Overclock Labs/Akash Network relationship, funding history (~$6M seed-stage, Galaxy Digital as biggest investor, Fertitta family), ~30 employees, ~4 years of runway, AKT tokenomics (389M max supply, ~14.7% inflation), and argues mass adoption will come from higher-level abstractions (e.g., DAO DAO) where users don't know they're using Akash.

### [gpu-economics] Ethereum merge GPUs will be Akash's unique value proposition
> "Us solving the GPU problem, especially by sourcing the underutilized GPUs of the Ethereum merge, is going to be a very important unique value proposition Akash can provide to the world."

### [local-compute] Servers must move to where the user is
> "How do you serve that data in a high-performance way, in a low-latency way? You do that by reducing the network latency between the end user and the server, and that means you need to go where the user is — that means the servers need to be in the same location of the user."
> — [00:32:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=egfFJqkSCLw&t=1972s)

**Context:** Phase three of the adoption plan — after cost, solve performance via Akash's geographically distributed provider model (edge compute thesis).

Akash — we don't build a data center, people with data centers come to us. It's a fundamentally different model."
