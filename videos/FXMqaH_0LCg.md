## Summary

Steven Waterhouse (Orchid founder, Nazare Ventures) moderates a panel with Greg Osuri (Akash), Ben Fielding (Gensyn), a co-founder of Exabits, and Shannon (head of growth at Livepeer) on whether decentralized computing is a viable business. Waterhouse frames the discussion around David Cahn's Sequoia article on AI's "$600 billion hole" — infrastructure spend vastly exceeding projected revenue — and asks how decentralization can shrink it.

In his intro, Greg says he founded Akash to democratize cloud and compute, describes Akash as "the world's first supercloud" (crediting Cornell for the concept), and claims Akash figured out a "tokenized open source software" business model that solves contributor churn without going closed-source like Docker or open-core companies. On costs, he argues removing the cloud middleman removes Amazon's ~43% compute margin, and lays out a three-part cost thesis: chip costs, middleman margins, and isolated islands of unshared chips — proposing pooled, shared clusters where "compute should be a common good." He attacks Nvidia's monopoly margins and reveals George Hotz's tinygrad work rewriting AMD drivers, with "the first ever Tiny Box" set to run on Akash.

On verification and trust, Greg explains Akash's design: no incentives until verification exists (contrasting with io.net, which he says enabled incentives without verification and got Sybil-attacked), plus a reputation system, optimistic quorum checks across providers, and a live proposal from a UT Austin professor for TEE-based verifiable hardware. The panel closes with each guest answering "decentralized AGI, yes or no, and when" — answers range from "in 60 years" to Ben's four-to-five years; one panelist (attribution ambiguous) laments AGI should be decentralized but current trends point the other way.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Compute controlled by unelected few is not the future
> "Really founded Akash to democratize cloud and compute, because compute is the fabric that connects the modern society, and that controlled by a few people that are unelected, that take tax from us, is not a future that I want for me."
> — [00:02:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FXMqaH_0LCg&t=173s)

**Context:** Greg's self-introduction; he also credits Cornell for the supercloud idea and calls Akash "the world's first supercloud."

### [other] Tokenized open source as the business model for open source software
> "A big challenge today with open software... is contributor churn... we saw companies like Docker just run out of business after raising at billions of dollars of valuation... the challenge is how do we really create open source, stay open source, without taking shortcuts... that was one of the big things that we figured out at Akash."
> — [00:03:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FXMqaH_0LCg&t=216s)

**Context:** Greg claims Akash pioneered a "tokenized open source software" model; says crypto's money premium gives open source new sustainability properties.

### [gpu-economics] Cutting the cloud middleman removes Amazon's ~43% margin
> "To specifically address a question about the $600 billion hole: if you think about it, Amazon makes about 43% margin on compute on average. So if you remove Amazon, you remove 43% at least."
> — [00:12:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FXMqaH_0LCg&t=737s)

**Context:** Responding to the moderator's question of how decentralized compute shrinks Sequoia's $600B AI expenditure hole.

### [decentralized-ai] Compute should be a common good; shared clusters will drive cost down
> "OpenAI has chips and Anthropic has chips and we have chips, you have chips, everybody has chips. If we all share the chips amongst ourselves we can create a much cheaper world, because now we don't have isolated clusters but rather a shared cluster. I think compute should be a common good. When that happens the cost will be significantly lower, because now we don't need new chips coming from Nvidia as much as we are needing now."
> — [00:13:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FXMqaH_0LCg&t=826s)

**Context:** His pooling thesis for solving AI's cost problem: today's isolated islands of chips are the access bottleneck, not raw supply.

### [gpu-economics] Breaking Nvidia's monopoly via AMD — first tinybox will run on Akash
> "Nvidia is a king obviously and their profit margins are incredibly high — the actual cost of the chip is not as much what Nvidia sells them for... well, it's a monopoly. Break the monopoly — you've got to get better or cheaper chips from competitors like AMD... George Hotz is rewriting the drivers for AMD to make it usable... the first ever Tiny Box is going to be on Akash. So things like that are coming, and that's going to reduce the cost significantly."
> — [00:14:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FXMqaH_0LCg&t=872s)

**Context:** Concludes "ultimately decentralization, open source is a solution to the cost problem and we'll get there — right now centralization is too inefficient and that's creating the hole bigger and bigger."

### [crypto-depin] No incentives without verification — io.net as the cautionary tale
> "Akash doesn't have verifiability, it has a reputation system, and Akash doesn't have incentives because, well, we don't have verification. What io.net did is they enabled incentives without verification — then you get Sybil attacks... that's why we chose not to do incentives until we have verification."
> — [00:19:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FXMqaH_0LCg&t=1181s)

**Context:** Answering how to trust you're getting the GPU you paid for; describes Akash's optimistic quorum verification, reputation with fraud proofs, and a UT Austin TEE proposal for verifiable hardware.

### [decentralized-ai] AGI should be decentralized — but current trajectory says otherwise
> "I think AGI should be decentralized. I hope it will be, but the way the things are going right now it doesn't look like it's going to be."
> — [00:31:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FXMqaH_0LCg&t=1880s)

**Context:** Closing lightning round ("decentralized AGI, yes or no, and when?"); another answer in the round was "yes, in 60 years." **Speaker:** possibly not Greg — the rapid-fire round is not diarized.
