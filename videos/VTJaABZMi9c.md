## Summary

Podcast ("Everything Bagel") with hosts Alex Kehaya and Bidhan Roy (founder of Bagel Network), featuring Greg as guest. Greg recounts Akash's origins in 2017 — the legal constraints of the era (no credit cards, no Google ads for crypto), raising only $1.8M to launch, and bootstrapping a technical community via "Testnet" challenge events before the token existed. He lays out his thesis that early-stage funding is inversely correlated with progress, citing Solana (~$30M), Ethereum ($18M ICO), and Cosmos as lean successes, and invokes Bezos's two-pizza-team rule. He explains Akash's incentive philosophy: incentivize growth after product-market fit rather than bootstrapping supply, using utilization data — H200s (best for running DeepSeek) at 100% utilization, network-wide utilization up from ~10% to ~70% in 12 months, per-GPU earnings roughly doubling from $10 to $20/day. He discusses open-source licensing (leaning toward GPL/AGPL after competitors with near-nine-figure funding copied Akash code without credit — some demos even still showed AKT as the payment token), community-building patterns (Shaw Walters/Eliza, Bonsai's Discord, live coding streams), and admits that if starting today he would likely launch Akash on Solana or a shared-state chain rather than a sovereign Cosmos chain. The closing segment is his strongest recurring thesis: open-source AGI "has to happen" because every sufficiently important mass-adoption technology has been open source; OpenAI will be "the new AOL"; and crypto is the only sustainable framework to monetize open machine intelligence, which must be permissionless and unstoppable.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [open-source-ai] OpenAI will be the new AOL
> "Similarly, like OpenAI, I think, will be the new AOL of the world. Yes, they were the first to get to market with ChatGPT... that kicked off an entire industry, but I think the true intelligence through AI will be open source."

**Context:** Answering whether open-source AGI ("open intelligence") is achievable; he compares closed AI pioneers to AOL shipping CDs before the open internet won.

### [open-source-ai] Open-source AGI has to happen
> "It's something that we absolutely need... it has to happen, because historically speaking, any sufficiently important technology that reached mass adoption is open source — Linux, worldwide web, you name it... Transformers, open source, right?"
> — [00:44:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VTJaABZMi9c&t=2686s)

**Context:** Host asks if getting entrepreneurs to build open-source AGI on crypto rails for humanity is crazy; Greg says it is a necessity, citing DeepSeek unseating closed state-of-the-art models.

### [decentralized-ai] Crypto is the only framework to monetize open AI
> "There's no doubt in my mind that crypto is the only framework to have monetizable machine intelligence... it's going to be multiple people, multiple organizations, multiple protocols interoperating with each other, permissionless."
> — [00:46:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VTJaABZMi9c&t=2814s)

**Context:** On how open-source AI sustains itself; he argues decentralization/crypto is the only proven mechanism to fund open source, and intelligence "has to be unstoppable."

**Context:** Explaining how utilization data (not upfront token emissions) drives Akash's incentive design; dated snapshot of GPU demand in early 2025.

### [gpu-economics] Akash utilization grew 10% to 70% in 12 months

### [other] Overfunded startups don't innovate
> "There historically hasn't been a single success story... that's overfunded, that actually innovated... Solana is a classic story, Ethereum is a classic story... most successful teams always have significantly lower funding because... once you have money you have distractions."

**Context:** His 2018/2019 viral thesis that seed-stage funding is inversely correlated with progress; Akash raised $1.8M to launch its chain.

### [other] Would launch Akash on Solana today, not a sovereign chain
> "If I were to do it today, I would most likely do it as Solana or one of these shared-state chain systems... when you do sovereign, yes, you have the benefits of control, but if you ask yourself deeply, do you really need that control in the early stages?"

**Context:** Reflecting on lessons from launching Akash as the first Cosmos chain in an era when Ethereum's gas fees made it unusable; a notable revision of his own architecture choice.
