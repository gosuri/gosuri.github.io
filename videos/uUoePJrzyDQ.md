## Summary

Proof of Data 2024 panel on decentralized compute, featuring Greg alongside Ben (co-founder, Gensyn) and Sofia (co-founder, Tromero), with a moderator connected to Protocol Labs. Greg introduces Akash as "the world's first supercloud that's decentralized," jokes that per Elon Musk GPUs are "harder to get than drugs," and claims Akash is the only platform offering A100s/H100s on demand for under $2/hr ("we sell spice"). His central argument: cloud concentration is trending toward a feudal society — an invisible "cloud tax" where ~50% of online services' income goes to unelected cloud providers — and "if we don't decentralize, we will die as a society." He advocates full-stack decentralization (general-purpose compute complementing specialized protocols like Gensyn's verification layer), recounts earning ~$500k from a $300 Helium miner as a cautionary tale for incentive design (Akash deliberately launched "pre-incentives"), and muses on unstoppable networks where AI agents self-replicate and fund their own compute. Notably, he endorses proof-of-personhood as a prerequisite for network use, says free speech "should be limited to humans and not machines," and argues proof-of-stake/crypto networks — unlike "five unknown board members" controlling the most powerful AI (an OpenAI-board jab) — are best poised to prevent a real Skynet. On privacy/verification questions he discloses that SolveCare stores HIPAA-compliant patient data on Akash and that the US Department of Defense uses a fork of Akash internally, and he explains why general-purpose compute verification remains unsolved (ZK proofs of a Stable Diffusion run took ~40 minutes). Sofia (not Greg) predicts the GPU shortage bottleneck eases around 2026.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash is the world's first decentralized supercloud
> "Akash is world's first supercloud that's decentralized. A supercloud is essentially a cloud that sits on private, public, hybrid, single computer, and effectively provides you capabilities and features that would look like a normal cloud, and with a marketplace where you can source resources from places that you normally can't find in your typical cloud marketplaces."
> — [00:01:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=87s)

**Context:** His self-introduction on the panel; canonical statement of the supercloud framing.

### [gpu-economics] GPUs "harder to get than drugs"; sub-$2 H100s on Akash
> "GPUs are very hard to get, and according to Elon Musk, harder than drugs. But there are GPUs available in the wild, and Akash is effectively bringing a lot of that GPU supply to the market for people that need them. Right now Akash, I bet, is the only platform you can get A100 and H100s..."
> — [00:02:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=132s)

**Context:** He finishes "...on demand for sub $2 — so we sell spice" in the next caption block (00:02:55); a dated market-condition claim from the March 2024 GPU crunch.

### [cloud-decentralization] The cloud tax is creating a feudal society
> "What that is trending towards is a feudalistic society that we work so hard as a humanity to escape — few people with the resource that everyone needs have control over those resources and charge a tax. All of us pay a cloud tax without even realizing we are paying a cloud tax: an average 50% of all the income that online services make go to a cloud provider, and these providers are unelected companies with shareholders, profits... as a primary driver."
> — [00:07:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=431s)

**Context:** Answering why decentralized compute matters now; he adds that with hyperscalers as GPU gatekeepers, "try getting a GPU from Amazon — it's impossible."

### [cloud-decentralization] If we don't decentralize, we die as a society
> "We are in a very crazy political and social-economical environment. If we don't decentralize, we will die as a society, and feudalism over freedom is going to prevail — and that's the reality we are faced with right now."
> — [00:08:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=518s)

**Context:** After arguing cloud innovation has stalled (the promised edge computing and 10ms latency never arrived because hyperscale was more profitable) and censorship is "getting out of control."

### [decentralized-ai] Full-stack decentralization, not one layer
> "All the way from GPU access to the layers in the middle — when you have verification and fault tolerance and caching and load balancing and all the good stuff — to the UIs and UXs of the world."
> — [00:13:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=818s)

**Context:** His answer opens "you need to have full stack decentralization... from resource acquisition to delivering the application" (00:12:56); Akash stays general-purpose and minimal at the protocol layer so specialized protocols like Gensyn can compose on top — "open protocols building on open protocols" (00:15:03).

### [crypto-depin] Helium paid him ~$500k for a $300 box — why Akash is pre-incentives
> "I was the early Helium miner in 2019 — I bought a device for about $300 and I minted enough tokens to be worth about half a million dollars in about a year's time. I'm the wrong type of person to be incentivized... that's why it's very challenging to design incentives. Akash is pre-incentives — the reason we have not launched incentives even though we have a huge budget is because incentive design is extremely, extremely hard."
> — [00:21:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=1288s)

**Context:** Agreeing with Ben's warning that proxy incentives get gamed; crypto is "phenomenal for incentives, at the same time very, very wasteful."

### [ai-agents] Unstoppable networks where agents self-replicate
> "What happens [when] you have an unstoppable network where agents can self-replicate by training themselves and no one's going to stop them, and if you perfect the incentive structure, they can create incentives on the network which will further scale up the network and no one can stop them? I don't know the answer to the question — I'm bringing a child out next month and she's going to grow up in this crazy world."
> — [00:22:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=1330s)

**Context:** Riffing on the accidental "Akash Network = Sky Net(work) = Skynet" translation; the moderator had asked about giving AI agents wallets and infrastructure access.

### [ai-agents] Proof of personhood; free speech for humans, not machines
> "I don't see a reason why humans would not have some degree of proof of personhood as a prerequisite to use the network... as much as I'm a free market fundamentalist and a free speech fundamentalist, I do believe that free speech should be limited to humans and not machines, because as a humanist... maybe in 30 years down the line this talk will be used as a way to suppress robotic speech."
> — [00:23:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=1416s)

**Context:** He notes Akash is a proof-of-stake network "where humanity has a stake — as far as I know, no bots own Akash."

### [decentralized-ai] Crypto networks are better positioned to prevent Skynet than corporate boards
> "I believe humans will do the right thing when given that they have agency to do the right thing — and not some five unknown board members that take over the most powerful AI for a few days and we have no idea what's going to happen. That's why I believe proof of stake networks, or crypto networks, are a lot more poised to prevent such a Skynet in the future."
> — [00:24:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=1458s)

**Context:** A clear reference to the November 2023 OpenAI board crisis; decentralized governance as AI-safety mechanism.

### [other] The US Department of Defense runs a fork of Akash
> "Also, US Department of Defense use Akash internally to secure their servers — but they use a fork of the Akash Network, they don't connect to the network."
> — [00:31:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=1888s)

**Context:** Answering a privacy question; he also cites SolveCare storing HIPAA-compliant US patient data on Akash (00:30:43).

### [decentralized-ai] General-purpose compute verification is unsolved; ZK is 40 minutes per image
> "If you look at ZK... last I checked, for something like a Stable Diffusion verification, a ZK proof will take 40 minutes to verify that the result came back exact. So if you're waiting 40 minutes before you can get your image generated..."
> — [00:37:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUoePJrzyDQ&t=2238s)

**Context:** He states flatly "verification for general purpose compute is not possible yet" (00:35:51) — the only path is higher-order benchmarking of results plus reputation.
