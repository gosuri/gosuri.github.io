## Summary

Official Akash livestream two weeks after the Mainnet 2 launch ("the world's first open source cloud"), hosted by community lead Michael with Greg, co-founder/CTO Adam Bozanich, and guest Sunny Aggarwal (Sikka/Osmosis). Greg reports first-week numbers: 150+ deployments, ~27 active, 53 active developers (vs. Polkadot's ~420 and Ethereum's ~2,500), ~68 AKT GMV (~$130k annualized), a surprise Gate.io listing, and users seeing 8x cost savings vs. Amazon Fargate when the team predicted 2x. Adam recounts the two-hour chain upgrade and coordination quirks of stopping a permissionless network; Sunny compares it to slower Cosmos Hub upgrades. A long segment explains audited attributes — a decentralized auditor/reputation layer letting deployers target providers by security tier, region, or trust set — which Greg frames as the "unbounded" customization that makes Akash "Airbnb for compute" vs. Amazon's Hilton, potentially reaching 8.2 million data centers. Priorities post-launch: usability ("make a race car a Prius"), auditor ecosystem growth, and Greg's side tools (Mitra self-healing deployments, DDoS/DNS/KMS/Sia integrations, pub-sub event triggers). Sunny explains Osmosis (customizable AMMs, monthly-iteration app chain), threshold decryption against front-running, and why he backed Akash: everyone else was building decentralized web 1.0 primitives (storage, DNS) while Akash is the only compute network — the missing piece for a decentralized web 2.0. Greg sketches "ownerless services" (DAO-run, threshold-signature-controlled frontends), predicts inflationary rewards give way to take income within a decade, and pitches the peer-to-peer cloud use case where ML companies' idle in-house clusters earn AKT. Sunny and Adam both anticipate the post-proof-of-work GPU glut landing on Akash. Ends with a WordPress pricing bug mea culpa ($1.60/month corrected, ~8x cheaper than Fargate), geography trivia from Sunny, and prize drawings.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Predicted 2x savings vs. Amazon, saw 8x
> "Some of the users have gained or noticed 8x cost savings in comparison to Amazon Fargate... realistically our prediction was like 2x, but seeing 8x was... a really cool thing."
> — [00:04:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiTE_qXoFvM&t=267s)

**Context:** First-week Mainnet 2 metrics; he adds savings vary with supply/demand and "I'm pretty sure next week is going to be a double or more" of the 53 active developers.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash as Airbnb for compute across 8.2 million data centers
> "Akash is positioned to have this hyper customizable cloud, and that potentially can be reached, installed in 8.2 million data centers in the world. So the way you want to picture Akash is really: if Akash is Airbnb [for] compute, Amazon is like Hilton."
> — [00:22:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiTE_qXoFvM&t=1337s)

**Context:** Explaining audited attributes — an unbounded, free-market attribute system versus the "homogeneous" experience of big chains and the bounded customization of centralized clouds.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash is the gateway to the decentralized cloud
> "Akash is the only network that does compute... when you have to deploy something, you got to deploy compute and then you attach all the services to compute... so that puts us in a very amazing position of being this gateway for the decentralized cloud."
> — [00:56:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiTE_qXoFvM&t=3385s)

**Context:** On integrating with Handshake/ENS (DNS) and Sia/Filecoin/Arweave (storage); he cautions "do not believe the hype is adoption" and predicts a product-market-fit shakeout of decentralized networks "in the next few years" ([00:57:06]).

### [crypto-depin] "Ownerless services": no human should control a web service
> "The idea that I frame called ownerless services: there shouldn't be a single owner, there shouldn't be an identifiable person or a human being that is controlling the website. I think it should be on a threshold signature scheme; it could be... a DAO controlling the website."
> — [01:00:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiTE_qXoFvM&t=3638s)

**Context:** Follows Sunny's point about self-hosting DeFi frontends for privacy; Greg cites THORChain's interest in Akash for exactly this.

### [cloud-decentralization] In 10 years Akash will be fully self-sustaining; 1% of cloud = $3B
> "In 10 years I would imagine it's going to be a fully self-sovereign... self-sustaining system, and if you think about the cloud, it's what, 300 billion dollars, right? So even a reasonable one percent market share is three billion dollars of... economic activity coming into the network."
> — [01:12:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiTE_qXoFvM&t=4322s)

**Context:** Answering how the network stays secure after 2030 as inflation decays: staking rewards get replaced by take income from network activity ("very hypothetically forward-looking numbers").

### [gpu-economics] ~20% of AI-company margins go to cloud bills
> "There's an article by [Andreessen] Horowitz that stated about 20% of margins for a lot of AI companies go to paying the cloud bills, and... it's rampant, it's very very expensive."
> — [01:17:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiTE_qXoFvM&t=4664s)

**Context:** Asked which use cases have the most potential; sets up the ML/peer-to-peer cloud pitch below.

### [gpu-economics] The peer-to-peer cloud: ML clusters earn AKT in downtime
> "They can install Akash and earn compute credits in AKT tokens in their downtime, and when they want to peak they can use this AKT tokens to actually use for compute. So you essentially create a very amazing mechanism where the provider becomes a tenant and vice versa. It's a self-serving ecosystem of a peer-to-peer cloud... that's only possible on Akash."
> — [01:19:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiTE_qXoFvM&t=4750s)

**Context:** ML companies' in-house clusters sit idle most of the day; he adds "we have machine learning companies knocking on our door" and the key technical unlock is GPU multi-tenancy ([01:19:52]).

### [gpu-economics] Proof-of-stake transitions will flood the market with GPUs for Akash
> "Especially as we see a lot of these proof-of-work coins start to switch to proof-of-stake... you're gonna have a lot of GPUs on the market that... can do something better, and so I'm hoping to see that Akash will be there and ready to fill in those boots and take in all that capacity."
> — [01:21:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiTE_qXoFvM&t=4880s)

**Context:** **Speaker:** Sunny Aggarwal, not Greg — anticipating the Ethereum merge GPU glut (a year and a half early); Adam agrees there will be "giant excess capacity of GPUs" Akash must capitalize on ([01:22:02]).
