## Summary

A community (Akash Alpha) interview days before the Mainnet 14 "refactor" upgrade, explaining the bombshell announcement that Akash will deprecate its sovereign Cosmos L1. Greg frames it as shared-state vs. sovereign-state, using a mansion-in-the-woods vs. downtown-apartment analogy: sovereignty brings freedom but crushing upkeep. History: Akash first chose Ethereum in 2017, but CryptoKitties crashing the network convinced him a serious infrastructure play needed its own chain, and 2017–18 alternatives (EOS) were "borderline scammy" — whereas 2025 offers mature modular architectures. Core reasons for leaving: validator profitability can't be sustained by AKT emissions without massive transaction volume ("economics at a certain stage becomes unsustainable"), crypto-economic security cost isn't justified, Akash never actually used its sovereign freedoms in 5 years, and Cosmos has stagnated because its founders went passive ("when you don't have the founder mode, the fire usually dies" — Satoshi being the only exception). He details the 18-month refactor: 8 years of accreted code made any change break the chain (stability was chosen over progress; zero security incidents and only planned downtime across 13 mainnet upgrades), and the rebuilt stack will let Akash ship BME (burn mechanism), incentives, verifiable compute, and virtual machines ("90% of AI uses virtual machines") fast — backed by a public roadmap. On migration: 15–18 foundations reached out (Solana and Ethereum mentioned approvingly), timeline is ~1 more year, stakers need do nothing — stake migrates automatically — and staking may evolve from paying for security to direct exposure to AI compute. He pledges to keep his and his family's AKT staked through migration.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [crypto-depin] A blockchain without decentralization "is just a really bad database"
> "If you remove all the bells and whistles of decentralization, from a technology standpoint it's just a really bad database — that's what it is, end of the day. But decentralization on top of it makes it extremely powerful. It's the only database where you do not need permission to write... In SQL you need permission to write, you need a password, username; in blockchains you don't need permission to write. That's the most attractive part of a blockchain, but that comes with a lot of trade-offs."
> — [00:05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HmRIa6PhO8Q&t=300s)

**Context:** Explaining the true cost of running a chain — validator hardware, redundancy, and profitability tied to token emissions and price.

### [crypto-depin] Sovereign-chain economics are unsustainable without massive volume
> "Only way to offset this risk of unknown cost is have massive amounts of transactions being written. Well, it's very unlikely to have that many amount of transactions for a non-financial product... you need a lot of volume to be profitable. So economics at a certain stage becomes unsustainable without the volume... To summarize: crypto-economic value was not justifiable in terms of security, and second, validator profitability cannot be sustained with the current economics... and the market conditions don't seem to be that favorable considering what's happening geopolitically."
> — [00:07:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HmRIa6PhO8Q&t=426s)

**Context:** The core economic argument for leaving the sovereign L1: "no one knows the answer" to the right emission level in proof-of-stake chains. Summary portion at [00:09:18].

### [other] "When you don't have the founder mode, the fire usually dies"
> "The founders of Cosmos [aren't] active anymore, and when you don't have the founder mode, the fire usually dies. Very rarely you can see an example — I think the only example I can think of where the founder is not present is probably Satoshi in Bitcoin... You can have the founders retire or go when you have a level of maturity, and Cosmos doesn't have the level of maturity for the founders to go silent."
> — [00:10:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HmRIa6PhO8Q&t=644s)

**Context:** Why the Cosmos ecosystem stagnated (no growth in members, liquidity, or technical complexity; no roadmap or direction) despite Akash funding shared-security efforts.

### [decentralized-ai] "We're not Bitcoin, we're AI" — the AI market demands rapid change
> "Akash is like that very strong blockchain but very hard to make changes. It's great if you're Bitcoin — no changes to the blockchain — but we're not Bitcoin, we're AI. AI market demands a lot of change. We wanted new incentives, we wanted the burn mechanism... we want all these new capabilities that we said we're going to do on the roadmap, but we discovered that we can't really do much without upgrading the blockchain."
> — [00:17:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HmRIa6PhO8Q&t=1032s)

**Context:** Justifying the 18-month refactor via a 100-year-old-house analogy; he admits the slowdown "did hurt us quite a bit" as the AI market demanded VMs and verification faster than a decentralized project could ship.

### [decentralized-ai] Post-upgrade roadmap: verifiable compute and VMs, with demand to follow
> "I feel like it's going to be a new chapter post Mainnet 14 upgrade... that is going to set a new cadence in terms of how fast we'll be shipping features. And that made me comfortable enough to commit to a public roadmap that I published on my Twitter... Verifiable compute I think is huge... virtual machines — again, 90% of AI uses virtual machines. So I'm really excited for these two capabilities, and I think the demand will catch up."
> — [00:22:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HmRIa6PhO8Q&t=1336s)

**Context:** BME (burn mechanism) targeted ~1 month post-upgrade; JWT support shipping so providers needn't stay online for application operations.

### [crypto-depin] Staking will evolve from security to direct exposure to AI compute
> "We're looking at potentially giving direct exposure for stakers into the compute itself, instead of just security right now — because the stakers are staking for security but they're not directly engaging with the AI compute, which is the bigger sort of opportunity... I'm particularly excited for staking on Akash evolving to a new paradigm."
> — [00:23:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HmRIa6PhO8Q&t=1421s)

**Context:** Reassuring stakers: migration is ~1 year out, stake migrates automatically, and he's keeping his own and his family's AKT staked throughout — "my entire family's future depends on the AKT" ([00:26:33]).
