## Summary

An April 2018 fireside chat at the Node meetup where Greg Osuri is the interviewer and Anatoly Yakovenko explains what would become Solana — then still called "Loom," with a rebrand teased at the end. Greg introduces the project as embracing time as a foundational layer, "about 700,000 transactions per second," and — asked if he's an investor — responds "excited to be the investor... they take my money," an early on-record Osuri bet on Solana. Anatoly walks through his Qualcomm embedded-systems background (Brew OS), proof of history (a sequential SHA-256 hash chain as a verifiable clock, verifiable in parallel on a 1080 Ti), proof of stake versus proof of work, his skepticism of delegated proof of stake ("tragedy of the commons... I'm not sold"), Byzantine faults, long-range attacks, and why a NASDAQ-speed chain (700k TPS vs. NASDAQ's 500k messages/sec) enables a front-running-free fully on-chain decentralized exchange as the first killer app. He offers the memorable line that in a thousand years proof of history yields "a data structure that represents every event in the world... at a millisecond resolution."

Greg steers the Bitcoin energy discussion — a single Bitcoin transaction taking ~77 kWh, "what it takes to power one average American house for a week" — and the exchange includes a striking early energy-compute convergence line ("I'm honestly bullish on proof of work — I can imagine the future when there's solar panels that are powering the heating of my water heater and all those things are doing is hashing"), though attribution between Greg and Anatoly is unclear in the captions. Greg closes by endorsing the whitepaper as "phenomenal" and saying the team has solved fundamental scalability and interoperability problems.
