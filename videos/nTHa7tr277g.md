## Summary

A 2018 panel on decentralized storage at Block 2thefuture, moderated by Casey from Google, with Liz Steininger (Least Authority), a Storj Labs engineer, James Prestwich (Storj co-founder, then Summa CEO), and Greg ("co-founder and CEO of Overclock Labs, the company behind Akash Network" — a very early public appearance, pre-mainnet). Panelists discuss what decentralizing storage means (control, security, privacy, decentralizing the "treasure map" of data locations), why data shouldn't live on-chain (cost, GDPR right-to-be-forgotten), and usability. Greg explains Akash's three-part design — procurement, deployment, settlement — using blockchain only for procurement as an open marketplace with deployment off-chain, and notes Akash does block storage rather than decentralized storage. On usability he argues the near future is a mix of centralized and decentralized systems (Coinbase vs. Bitcoin). Asked what he wants within a year, he reveals he testified before the California Assembly two days prior on the first blockchain law introduced in the state, and calls for legislation, highly scalable/high-performance storage, and better usability. Panel closes with "own your data" and "it's not ready yet — but you should still want it."

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash's early architecture: on-chain procurement, off-chain deployment
> "For us, we're a decentralized cloud infrastructure marketplace... Akash is where you procure your infrastructure and deploy and you settle — so that's three pieces of the puzzle: procurement, deployment, and settlement. We use blockchain for procurement as an open marketplace... whereas deployment is off chain; we can't really use blockchain per se because of performance, security... reasons."
> — [00:05:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTHa7tr277g&t=307s)

**Context:** 2018 explanation of the Akash design, three years before mainnet — the marketplace-on-chain/workloads-off-chain split that shipped.

### [cloud-decentralization] The future is a combination of centralized and decentralized systems
> "User interfaces in decentralized systems, they're hard because you're writing and reading from the chain, [and] depending on what chain, it's really slow. So I think it's gonna be a combination of centralized systems and decentralized systems — you look at Coinbase, centralized, whereas Bitcoin is not... we can do much better, but it's really really hard. I think that's gonna be a big huge topic for the next few years."
> — [00:14:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTHa7tr277g&t=867s)

**Context:** On usability trade-offs. **Speaker:** likely Greg (flows directly into his answer about California legislation), but captions are not diarized.

### [other] Legislation first — then the US is a cradle of innovation
> "I like to see regulation, legislation — in fact regulators can't do anything without legislation... I testified in front of assembly two days ago on the first blockchain law ever introduced in state of California. I really like to see some legislation around blockchain and this new economy — it's very very important. That's my number one priority, because if you get that out of the way, I think US is a cradle of innovation."
> — [00:15:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTHa7tr277g&t=911s)

**Context:** His one-year wish list, followed by wanting highly scalable/high-performance storage systems and better usability.

### [cloud-decentralization] Decentralization is key for future society
> "Well, security and ownership — we all know what happens when your data [is] centralized, with Facebook there's no auditability, there's nothing going on but your data. So I think decentralization is key here for future society."
> — [00:04:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTHa7tr277g&t=265s)

**Context:** Answer to why decentralizing storage matters, weeks after the Cambridge Analytica story broke. **Speaker:** possibly not Greg — captions are not diarized and several panelists answered in sequence.
