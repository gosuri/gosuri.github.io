## Summary

An Akash Network community livestream hosted by Adam Wozney (Akash community lead) featuring Colin Lowenberg (Akash partnerships lead), Caleb DeLisle (author of the PacketCrypt algorithm behind PKT), Josh and Jesse Berger (PKT community founders / PacketPal), and Chalabi (Chandra Station). Greg Osuri is not a speaker in this video.

The conversation covers what PKT is — a founderless, Bitcoin-style blockchain using a "bandwidth-hard" proof of work aimed at building a decentralized bandwidth marketplace ("decentralize Comcast" the way Akash aims to decentralize AWS) — and how to mine PKT announcements profitably on Akash. Colin shares test results (roughly 140 PKT/day on a 10-CPU Akash deployment at ~$60/month, about 2x return at then-current prices), demos deploying a miner through the Akash GUI, and explains lease mechanics: prices lock in when a lease is struck, deployments can be closed anytime, and the `signed_by` attribute restricts bids to Akash-audited providers. The guests discuss ISPs and centralized clouds blocking miners (Hetzner blocking PKT pool IPs), the case for multi-cloud/decentralized hosting as robustness against single points of failure, PKT's 100-day 10% emission "decimation" schedule, and PacketPal's "packet cube" edge device intended to let people share internet with neighbors. Colin notes Akash had no GPU support yet ("we certainly are going to have GPU support in the future but we don't have it today" — dated, as GPU support later became Akash's flagship) and that pricing pegged to USD/stable pricing was being considered. Useful as a snapshot of Akash's 2021 CPU-mining era positioning, but contains no statements from Greg Osuri.
