## Summary

Andy Pickering interviews Greg Osuri in a structured explainer format. Greg recaps AngelHack (200,000 developers at peak, launching Firebase) and his open-source contributions (Kubernetes, Docker, Terraform), then makes the case against cloud concentration: three companies control ~80% of "someone else's computer," which he likens to feudalism; cloud providers sell scarce GPUs to the highest bidder and thereby "dictate how AI should be trained"; ~50% of what consumers pay online services flows invisibly to cloud providers; and hyperscaler moats stifle innovation (he mentions an active FTC/"FCC" investigation into Google and Amazon).

The Airbnb-of-server-hosting analogy is developed at length: ~8.1 million data centers used only ~15% of the time (Intuit, Walmart seasonality; Amazon itself turned its own idle retail capacity into AWS — "what Amazon did to themselves is what Akash is doing for the rest of them"). Airbnb-style variety versus Amazon's Hilton-style consistency; reverse-auction pricing with CPU compute 50-80% cheaper (up to "80 times cheaper" for idle supply) but GPUs only 10-20% cheaper. Differentiators: developer UX (an NVIDIA engineer amazed at launching an LLM in 10 minutes), permissionless neutrality for platforms like Brev, Prime Intellect and NodeShift (Amazon is known to "pull the plug" on successful dependents), open-source reliability (four years without downtime versus Amazon's "200 times a year"; the South Korean security firm that caught a bug), 500+ contributors, and on-chain funding as an open-source sustainability model (contrasted with Docker's fate and HashiCorp's license change). He explains AKT staking (~10-14% APR), on-chain governance, and a take-rate on hosting fees.

Roadmap: "trial wallets" to fix crypto onboarding (95% of users are lost at the buy-AKT step, which he blames on US regulatory ambiguity), then a managed-services marketplace over the next 12 months to reach AWS parity and give open-source creators a business model — turning Akash into a "network of networks." The hot-take round yields his firmest-conviction prediction: decentralized AI, with even your fridge contributing to a "global machine learning machine," and a 10-year vision of 2,000-3,000 regions with self-coordinating agents transacting in crypto.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Three companies controlling 80% of the cloud is feudalism
> "It turns out there are three companies that control these computers, 80% of it, at least. Now, that, to me, is a little troublesome. Why? Because so much power in the hands of a few. You know, we had something like that. It was called feudalism, right? If you have so much power in the hands of a few, that few dictate the future of this public utility."
> — [00:03:54](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/akash-network-the-decentralized-compute-marketplace/id1471243424?i=1000669078512)

**Context:** Opening framing of why decentralized cloud matters; "cloud is just someone else's computer."

### [gpu-economics] Cloud providers dictate who gets to train AI
> "Who powers these AI machines? You need something called GPUs. How do you get them? You have to go to this cloud provider. And it's nearly impossible for a company to get these GPUs because they're scarce. These cloud providers sell the GPUs to the highest bidder. So they literally dictate today how AI should be trained and who should use AI by choosing who they want to give their GPUs."
> — [00:04:35](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/akash-network-the-decentralized-compute-marketplace/id1471243424?i=1000669078512)

**Context:** Extending the feudalism argument to AI; followed by the invisible-tax claim: "50% of what we pay to online services goes to a cloud provider."

### [gpu-economics] Data centers sit unused 85% of the time
> "There are about 8.1 million data centers in the world... in these data centers, they only use 15% of the time. That means 85% of the time they're not being used... [Intuit] during tax season, their compute is used 95% of their capacity or higher... Off tax season, they only use 5% or less."
> — [00:09:13](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/akash-network-the-decentralized-compute-marketplace/id1471243424?i=1000669078512)

**Context:** The Airbnb analogy's supply side (quote spans into the 00:09:58 block); he notes AWS itself was born from Amazon's idle retail capacity — "what Amazon did to themselves is what Akash is doing for the rest of them" (00:10:40).

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash has run four years without a single outage
> "It's been online for about four years, has not gone down a single time, like not a single time. Amazon goes down 200 times a year, right?... because Akash is open source, because you have so many eyeballs watching this code base 24/7, the chances of bad code getting in thereby causing instability is very, very low."
