## Summary

A panel hosted by Olas on the decentralized AI stack — training, fine-tuning, and inference — with speakers from Gensyn (decentralized training compute), Flock (federated learning + blockchain), Ritual (AI co-processor/inference), Manifold (Bittensor subnet 4 verification, corcel.io search), and Greg Osuri for Akash. The moderator frames the stack as infrastructure (compute/storage), data, and intelligence/app layers.

Greg positions Akash as "the world's first supercloud" — a layer sitting atop private, public, and hybrid compute with a marketplace and orchestration to schedule workloads — and claims Akash was then the only place to get on-demand H100s/A100s, calling GPUs the most important chipsets for humanity (the "spice must flow" Dune analogy). He argues for horizontal, do-one-thing-well protocols: more protocol surface means a bigger attack surface, and permissionless censorship resistance is the core primitive ("when you build on Akash... you own it, no one's going to stop you"). He cites adoption claims (healthcare companies storing HIPAA-compliant data, an NSA/Department of Defense project, one of the largest IP-address marketplaces) and open-governance stats (about 480 contributors, only about 20 from Overclock Labs). He makes a contrarian argument that crypto solves open-source sustainability, pointing to Docker's fire sale and Kubernetes' contributor crisis. On value accrual he's emphatic the app layer captures the most value ("a company that's going to use Nvidia is going to be a lot more valuable than Nvidia"), with infrastructure competing on price ($2/hr H100s). On supply strategy he describes seven-figure-per-month crypto incentive experiments, 3x quarter-over-quarter workload growth (~2,000 active leases), and predicts the GPU supply crunch lasts until about 2026 — whoever has the biggest war chest wins. Other panelists debate verification of off-chain training, federated learning, and consumer-GPU supply.
