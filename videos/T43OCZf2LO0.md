## Summary

A solo Greg talk (posted by Founder School) on why Akash exists, framed as first principles rather than AI hype. He traces computing history: mainframes (borrowing time from the system) → personal computing → the internet, "born out of decentralization" — then re-centralization via hyperscalers, with close to 70% of IT spend going to four companies whose hardware vendors optimize for them, not the public. He catalogs the harms: gated GPU access (the Semafor story; Musk's "GPUs are harder to get than drugs" line), a hidden "cloud tax" — over 50% of every penny spent on online services goes to three cloud providers (citing a16z's "trillion dollar paradox") — the CrowdStrike outage as proof of opaque fragility (Delta canceling 7,000 flights over one bad code push), and cloud moats stifling innovation like late-1800s railroads, now under antitrust investigation. The hyper-concentration risk he names "digital feudalism": corporations dictating who gets to do AI and rationing compute ("the spice must flow"). His answer: compute as a public utility "as free as the air we breathe," restoring sovereignty over data and compute, empowering communities over corporations. He contrasts AWS's ~trillion-dollar unit and 60%+ margins with Akash at 80% cheaper, notes 4 years of zero network downtime, describes the DePIN category (Akash as "category creator" alongside Filecoin, Bittensor), the reverse-auction mechanism, Nvidia as Akash's biggest user and the only crypto protocol it uses, Nous Research, and Venice AI. Growth: 50% quarter-over-quarter lease growth. Closing: society stands at a crossroads between centralization and decentralization.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [decentralized-ai] Digital feudalism: corporations will dictate who gets to do AI
> "The real risk is us as a society going back to feudalism — I call this digital feudalism. There was a time in history that few people had most power in Europe and peasants had to depend on these people for accessing basic things like food and water, and that is a future we're facing right now. The future is bleak where a corporation or a business dictates who gets to do AI, dictates a rationing of resources to use AI — and we're kind of seeing that right now, Amazon and Google of the world rationing computing power... rationing, or we call it, the spice. But the spice must flow."
> — [00:08:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T43OCZf2LO0&t=485s)

**Context:** The stakes section of the talk — the risk of hyper-concentration of cloud/AI power.

### [cloud-decentralization] Compute should be as free as the air we breathe
> "We believe compute should be a public utility. Compute should be as free as the air we breathe and water we drink, and that's the future we're all building towards. What that means is restoration [of] sovereignty: we need to have control over our data, we need to have control of our compute, and we need to empower communities rather than corporations to drive this technological progress."
> — [00:08:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T43OCZf2LO0&t=528s)

**Context:** His recurring public-utility thesis, presented as the industry's "new era of decentralization."

### [gpu-economics] A 60%-margin layer cannot be a public utility — everyone pays a cloud tax
> "Over 50% of every single penny we spend on online services goes to one of the three cloud providers. All of us pay a cloud tax without knowing who we're paying the tax to... [AWS] is alone a trillion dollar business for Amazon, and profit margins are over 60%, and that's not okay — you can't have an [ab]normally profitable layer and you call it a public utility... decentralized systems like Akash [are] 80% cheaper than what you would otherwise pay on Amazon."
> — [00:05:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T43OCZf2LO0&t=312s)

**Context:** Citing Andreessen Horowitz's "Trillion Dollar Paradox" study; margin/utility line is at [00:09:32].

### [cloud-decentralization] The internet was born decentralized — the cloud reversed it
> "[Personal computing] created the internet on the foundation of decentralization, connecting these individual devices in a hive network, sharing intelligence and removing the need for a centralized coordinated system to communicate... historically, decentralization is what really spun the innovation and the accessibility that we have today. But eventually somehow the centralization came, with large hyperscalers... close to 70% of all the IT spend is spent by four companies."
> — [00:02:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T43OCZf2LO0&t=135s)

**Context:** His mainframe → PC → internet → cloud re-centralization narrative; the 70% figure is at [00:02:59].

### [cloud-decentralization] Four years live with zero downtime — open source reliability
> "In a case like Akash, we have been live for about 4 years and there hasn't been a single time the network went down or slowed down. It's always available, it's always live, and that's possible because it's open source — people are looking at the source code all the time, people are pushing updates all the time — and it's decentralized: there's not a single system that controls the entire network."
> — [00:10:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T43OCZf2LO0&t=614s)

**Context:** Contrasted with the CrowdStrike incident — "somebody pushed bad code to production" taking down airlines — as proof of centralized opacity and fragility.

### [decentralized-ai] Nvidia is Akash's biggest user — the only crypto protocol it uses
> "Nvidia happens to be a biggest user of Akash — in fact, Akash is the only crypto protocol Nvidia uses. Akash is integrated into Nvidia products, and incredible companies like Nous Research, which happens to be a top tier AI team that's working on decentralized training, to University of Texas to Rochester [Institute] of Technology — Akash has a wide ecosystem in the traditional AI world and also in the decentralized world."
> — [00:12:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T43OCZf2LO0&t=742s)

**Context:** Ecosystem section; he also highlights Venice AI (Erik Voorhees's privacy-optimized ChatGPT replacement) and 50% quarter-over-quarter lease growth.
