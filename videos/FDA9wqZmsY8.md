## Summary

A relaxed live interview three weeks after Akash's GPU marketplace launch. Greg retells the origin story — edge computing and the 100ms latency obsession, accidentally inventing a blockchain-like system (BitTorrent replication plus Git/Merkle verification), the Ethereum prototype killed by CryptoKitties, writing Tendermint modules before Cosmos SDK existed, and throwing away 70k lines of code to migrate. On shared-vs-sovereign chains he's nuanced: Cosmos has "the best sovereign stack, hands down," EVM insecurity is "the root of all the exploits," but he'd consider Solana in five-to-ten years and even says Akash would move there if unsuccessful after five years. The meatiest section maps the 2023 GPU supply crunch: H100s at ~$40k/chip (minimum 8 per server, ~$300k), two-year order queues "harder than a Rolex," hyperscalers deprioritized by Nvidia because they built competing chips (TPUs, Trainium), and startups locked into year-long CoreWeave contracts — making Akash "the only place for secondary markets" in unused GPUs, with Foundry as a major supplier and all A100s sold out at launch (one listed at $0.13/hr vs. the ~$1/hr market rate). He describes onboarding non-crypto AI devs daily with a claimed 98% conversion rate, the Keplr/AKT friction problem, USDC payments with a 20% non-AKT usage fee, and Akashml as a credit-card-friendly AI layer. Notable dated segments: he calls the SEC "a rogue agency acting illegally," predicts the Democrats won't win the 2024 election and the next administration will be less crypto-hostile, mentions a Marine officer confirming DoD experimentation with Akash, and reveals he moved from San Francisco to Austin. The closing vision is his clearest sovereign-AI statement: personal financial and health data should never leave the house, AI feudalism (a few chip-rich companies selling "you") must be avoided, ~1% of global GDP will soon go to machine learning, and success means his wife and kids using Akash with sovereignty over their data.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [crypto-depin] Cosmos-style sovereign chains will surpass Ethereum in 5-10 years
> "Is it as secure as Ethereum? Not yet. But given a chance, for next five, ten years, will [it] get better than Ethereum? Absolutely."
> — [00:18:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDA9wqZmsY8&t=1116s)

**Context:** Comparing shared-state vs. sovereign stacks; he calls Cosmos "the best sovereign stack, hands down" and dismisses L2s as "a caching layer in the middle."

### [other] If Akash isn't successful in five years, it would move to Solana
> "If Akash is not successful in, let's say, five years — if there's no traffic, we haven't met any thresholds that we need to meet in order to consider to be successful — we'll move to Solana."
> — [00:19:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDA9wqZmsY8&t=1158s)

**Context:** A conditional trade-off of sovereignty for cost: a native token adds security but users pay validators; he's long shared-state tech on a 5-10 year horizon despite staying sovereign today.

### [decentralized-ai] Akash was built for machine learning from the 2017 white paper
> "It's funny that Akash seems like GPUs overnight, but if you look at our white paper, the first section that talks about motivation — we talk about Akash, it's primarily built for machine learning. This is published in 2017, 2018."
> — [00:24:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDA9wqZmsY8&t=1461s)

**Context:** Pushing back on the idea that Akash pivoted opportunistically to AI when the ChatGPT wave hit.

### [gpu-economics] H100s take two years to get — "harder than a Rolex"
> "H100s... [have] the highest demand. In fact they're so hard to get, it takes about two years if you order now. It's harder than a Rolex."
> — [00:29:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDA9wqZmsY8&t=1768s)

**Context:** Laying out 2023 GPU scarcity; he later adds an H100 costs ~$40k per chip, sold in minimum sets of eight (~$300k per server) ([00:31:38]), and cites Musk's "harder to get than drugs" line.

### [gpu-economics] Nvidia deprioritized the hyperscalers as competitors
> "Turns out Nvidia deprioritized the big cloud when it comes to giving them chips, because Nvidia considers this big cloud companies as competition... they prioritize... smaller providers like CoreWeave and DataCrunch and Lambda Labs."
> — [00:32:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDA9wqZmsY8&t=1942s)

**Context:** Explains why AWS/Google/Azure lacked on-demand H100s: they built competing AI chips (TPUs etc.), so Nvidia routes supply to neoclouds it invested in (CoreWeave at ~$8B valuation).

### [gpu-economics] Akash is the secondary market for the GPU supply crunch
> "Now imagine you paid for something for the whole year which you may or may not know if you have use for it. What do you do? You put them in Akash as a secondary market... if you want on-demand chips, will you come [to] Akash? Because Akash is the only place for secondary markets — essentially an unused GPU market. That's what the whole thesis is all about. So Akash is in a very unique position to take advantage of this supply crunch."
> — [00:36:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDA9wqZmsY8&t=2202s)

**Context:** The Airbnb-for-idle-GPUs argument: AI startups locked into year-long CoreWeave-style contracts resell unused capacity; Foundry named as a large H100/A100 supplier to Akash.

### [gpu-economics] All A100s sold out within three weeks of GPU launch
> "The moment we launched GPUs three weeks ago, all the A100s are sold out."
> — [00:40:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDA9wqZmsY8&t=2422s)

**Context:** Early demand signal from the August 2023 GPU mainnet; he later notes demand skews to A100s over H100s because "H100s are so expensive and so prohibitive people are not building for them" ([00:42:34]), and cites an A100 listed at $0.13/hr vs. ~$1/hr market standard.
