## Summary

Long-form Smart Economy podcast interview with Greg Osuri covering his career arc and the Akash thesis, then pivoting hard into local compute and energy. Greg recounts moving to the US in 2004, early Bitcoin use (~2010), getting hooked on Ethereum's permissionless database model (~2015-16), and publishing the Akash white paper in 2017 after concluding an open-source cloud needs both open software and an incentive network. He stresses Akash raised only ~$2M and frames the project as a series of experiments, claiming a track record of correct calls: predicting the GPU/chip shortage in 2020 and the energy shortage two years before this interview, plus early bets on Cosmos and AI.

The core thesis: clouds are built on control and can never tap compute they don't control — compute inside homes, small data centers, vehicles — and demand would inevitably outpace supply, proven when clouds ran out of GPUs in 2024. He describes Akash supply as mostly sub-10MW "edge" data centers, and the newly announced "home node," positioning homes as dual-purpose mini power plants (solar/wind/geothermal) for AI inference and eventually training. He details his own solar-powered home cluster running a Llama 70B model and agents for security/cameras, insisting on local inference for privacy. He argues heterogeneous, distributed training (Pluralis; a Bittensor subnet's 72B-parameter distributed run) plus data-center moratoriums and energy constraints will force decentralized training. Closing predictions for ~2030: weaker dollar (vs gold), scarcer resources, real AI job displacement (front-end/back-end devs out of work in 2-3 years), double-digit GDP growth from AI with lower employment, stablecoins as de facto dollar rails, an energy-rich US, and decentralization taking center stage as the only way to scale.

### [energy] Homes as power plants for AI

**Context:** Explaining Akash's newly announced "home node"; he says he testified in Congress "last May" about solving the energy crisis by using homes, since homes are dual-purpose with excess energy for AI training or inference.

### [gpu-economics] Claimed track record: called the GPU and energy shortages
> "We predicted in 2020 that there's going to be a chip shortage, GPU shortage... we predicted two years ago there's going to be energy shortage... making the bet on Cosmos, right bet; making the bet on AI is right bet; making a bet on energy now is I think it's going to be the right bet as well."
> — [00:20:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0fBYZPHOQds&t=1225s)

**Context:** Recounting Akash's history of technology picks; quote spans into the [00:22:33] block for the "bet on energy" line.

### [cloud-decentralization] Cloud demand will outpace compute supply
> "There will come a point where cloud consumption outpaces the supply for the compute... at the rate at which the data was growing, there is no single company in the world that can catch up and scale. So the obvious answer is a grid model... in a peer-to-peer manner where there is compute everywhere."
> — [00:26:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0fBYZPHOQds&t=1573s)

**Context:** His founding conviction for Akash; he says the thesis was first proven in 2024 when clouds literally ran out of GPUs while people had GPUs in their data centers and homes.

### [local-compute] Electric vehicles as data centers on wheels
> "You look at a lot of things I've been calling out about electric vehicles being data centers on wheels. I tweeted this about I don't know 5 years ago and you see Elon Musk talking about [Teslas] becoming a potential data centers on wheels because they do have GPUs."
> — [00:28:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0fBYZPHOQds&t=1705s)

**Context:** Arguing latent compute is everywhere — TVs, refrigerators, gaming machines, vehicles — and its constraints are solvable.

### [local-compute] Compute shortage means borrowing from your neighbors
> "The shortage is going to get worse. And what happens when you have shortage? You borrow from peers, you borrow from your neighbors. What Akash enables is borrowing compute power from your neighbors that are not in physically geographical accessible distance for you — basically making anyone your neighbor that you can borrow from."
> — [00:31:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0fBYZPHOQds&t=1882s)

**Context:** On agents consuming ever more tokens (cites Anthropic at ~$30B annual revenue) and compute scarcity worsening; "that's the thesis and I still continue to believe."

### [local-ai] Local inference because cloud can't be trusted with private data
> "My home is entirely run by agents at this point... all my cameras are hooked up to my AI agent. I need local inference because I would never trust cloud on terms of privacy. General rule of thumb is if anything that leaves your network, doesn't matter how secure it is, it is potentially vulnerable to attack vectors that you have no idea and no control over."
> — [00:37:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0fBYZPHOQds&t=2272s)

**Context:** Describing his own solar-powered home AI setup (net-zero energy bill, local clusters, models with no internet access) which is now part of the Akash home node network.

### [local-compute] Home computers will beat data centers as models optimize
