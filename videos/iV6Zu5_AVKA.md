## Summary

A DeFi Summit panel with Greg Osuri and Eric Ma (Deeper Network CMO), moderated by Mona (Nuri). Greg argues centralization's efficiency advantages are "just a factor of time": "centralized is yesterday... the future is decentralized." His three advantages of decentralized systems: openness (extensibility with guarantees — unlike Twitter/Facebook APIs that "kill your company"), censorship resistance (deplatforming is being "weaponized by state actors"; a platform that can censor a sitting US president shows how much power they hold), and cost (Akash is one-third to one-fourth the price of centralized clouds because market pricing beats administered pricing). He's candid about cons: usability ("not for everyone" yet), self-custody risk, and unresolved tragedy-of-the-commons abuse. On security he claims ~70% of blockchain nodes run on Amazon with their keys stored there ("not Jeff Bezos is going to save your keys... they probably have access to your keys"), versus Akash's enforced "resiliency through redundancy" (spread keys across five boxes, remote signers). The highlight is his tear-down of DeFi's "adorable" cognitive dissonance: Uniswap's smart contracts are decentralized but its indexer (Graph nodes), web UI, and DNS run on Amazon/Cloudflare — "unless three-fourths of your infrastructure is decentralized, you're not DeFi." He predicts serious regulatory risk for founders whose names are on DNS records of trade-facilitating frontends ("something is going to happen — I'm extremely scared of this"), says users should run their own UIs, cites ~$25K/year to run an Ethereum full node via Infura, and coins the refrain "open finance needs open cloud." Closes with Rosa Luxemburg: "people that don't move don't notice their chains."

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] "Centralized is yesterday... the future is decentralized — it's an evolution"
> "For me, I don't see... centralized is yesterday, and then the future is decentralized. It's an evolution... that means all the things that we have in terms of efficiency and whatnot will come in decentralized networks itself, and that is just a factor of time."
> — [00:05:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iV6Zu5_AVKA&t=344s)

**Context:** Responding to Eric Ma's pros/cons of centralization; Greg concedes efficiency but frames every centralized advantage as temporary.

### [other] Platforms that can censor a sitting president prove the danger; society must take power back
> "If you tell me nothing bad happens, I'll tell you you're full of [it]. Something bad will happen — and when a centralized platform can censor a sitting US president, that tells you the amount of power these platforms have... We are seeing quite a bit of weaponizing of this deplatforming by state actors looking to hurt companies or organizations that have disagreement with these states... We as a society owe it to take back the power to ourselves."
> — [00:07:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iV6Zu5_AVKA&t=469s)

**Context:** His censorship-resistance argument, referencing Trump's 2021 deplatforming as proof of concentrated platform power.

### [gpu-economics] Akash costs one-third to one-fourth of centralized cloud
> "When it comes to Akash, the cost advantage of using Akash versus a centralized cloud is one-third... sometimes it is one-fourth, like when compared to Google. And the reason for that is because open marketplaces generally tend to optimize for the forces of demand-supply for pricing, versus an individual setting the price."
> — [00:08:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iV6Zu5_AVKA&t=512s)

**Context:** Third pillar of his decentralization pitch: market-based pricing structurally beats administered hyperscaler pricing.

### [cloud-decentralization] ~70% of blockchain nodes run on Amazon — with their keys
> "I see a lot of nodes being deployed on Amazon — I think arguably about 70% of the nodes running are running on Amazon today, and they all have their keys in Amazon itself. If the server on the Amazon gets compromised, you're screwed... no one is going to save your keys, not Jeff Bezos — in fact, they probably have access to your keys and you don't even know about it. It's that bad. Current cloud is so insecure that people don't talk about it unless something really bad happens."
> — [00:12:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iV6Zu5_AVKA&t=765s)

**Context:** Arguing d-cloud is more secure because Akash enforces "resiliency through redundancy" (keys spread across five providers, remote signers); concludes "hands down, decentralized cloud is the future" ([00:14:11]).

### [crypto-depin] DeFi's "adorable" cognitive dissonance — Uniswap runs on Amazon and Cloudflare
> "The cognitive dissonance we have in crypto, especially DeFi, is adorable... Look at the Uniswap architecture: you have smart contracts running on Ethereum, the indexer running on Graph — where are these [Graph] servers running? Amazon. The JavaScript application — where is this running? Amazon. The DNS — probably Amazon or probably Cloudflare. You're not decentralized — just own it... Unless three-fourths of your infrastructure is decentralized, you're not DeFi."
> — [00:18:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iV6Zu5_AVKA&t=1109s)

**Context:** Asked how cloud/web3 powers DeFi; he wonders why Uniswap isn't on Handshake DNS and why Graph nodes and UIs aren't on Akash/Sia/Skynet. Refrain at [00:23:27]: "Open finance needs open cloud... being on Amazon, being on Cloudflare, you're not decentralized — prove me wrong."

### [crypto-depin] Regulators will come for DeFi frontends registered in founders' names
> "There's a huge regulatory risk when you're hosting your web UI on a domain name that's registered under your name... the authorities could be like: no, you run uniswap.org, it's registered [to] Hayden Adams... you're facilitating exchange-type of relationship or money transfer on your domain, hosted in your name — hence you are responsible. Something is going to happen; I'm extremely scared of this notion... The solution to the problem is you should let your own clients, your own users, run their own UIs."
> — [00:21:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iV6Zu5_AVKA&t=1278s)

**Context:** A prescient 2021 warning (predating later DeFi frontend enforcement actions); he analogizes writing BitTorrent (legal) vs. hosting infringing files (not). Also claims running an Ethereum full node via Infura costs ~$25,000 ([00:23:27]).
