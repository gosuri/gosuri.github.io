## Summary

Greg's October 2021 Yahoo Finance Live interview (same segment re-uploaded by Akash as 9MCrtBKQYkw). Against the backdrop of the Trump SPAC news and the Parler/AWS de-platforming, Greg argues the internet was born decentralized but ~80% of cloud computing is now controlled by four companies, making de-platforming a civil-liberties threat independent of politics. He describes Akash as working "like a traditional cloud" but sourced from a distributed network of cloud-grade data centers worldwide (Equinix, which "actually provides compute to Amazon," is a big Akash provider), claims open-source auditability gives similar or better security guarantees than the big clouds, and shares early growth numbers: ~500 live applications and 30,000+ deployments in the six months since launch, with workloads doubling monthly. On abuse, he explains Akash's provider-level self-moderation model: providers choose whom to serve, so contested-but-legal apps can find hosts while universally rejected content cannot.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] 80% of cloud controlled by four companies
> "Internet was created as a decentralized network to fight against centralization... but over time it got increasingly centralized, and now the landscape looks like 80 percent or so of the cloud computing, which is a backbone for the internet, is controlled by four companies — Amazon being the biggest one."
> — [00:01:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ESTCd9nb5CM&t=89s)

**Context:** His recurring cloud-centralization thesis, answering how sites get de-platformed.

### [other] De-platforming is a threat to civil liberties
> "We're saying increasingly de-platforming becoming a threat to our civil liberties... it's not so much about what side of the political spectrum you fall under, but the fact that there's a single company or a single individual or entity that has so much power on civil liberties is what's concerning."
> — [00:02:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ESTCd9nb5CM&t=130s)

**Context:** Referencing the Parler takedown as the latest example.

### [cloud-decentralization] Open source makes Akash as secure as or more secure than AWS
> "It's probably more secure than your traditional Amazon because Akash being open source, all our source code is actually open for a public audit, so it offers similar levels if not better levels of security guarantees Amazon's or the big cloud offers."
> — [00:03:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ESTCd9nb5CM&t=216s)

**Context:** Rebutting the host's security concern about choosing Akash over AWS.

### [cloud-decentralization] Early growth: 500 apps, 30k deployments, doubling monthly
> "We launched the platform about six months ago and so far we have about 500 applications live... over 30,000 deployments that happened over the last six months... until last month we were actually doubling in terms of workloads."
> — [00:03:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ESTCd9nb5CM&t=216s)

**Context:** Quantified adoption snapshot ~6 months after the Akash marketplace launch.

### [cloud-decentralization] Provider self-moderation instead of central takedowns
> "The way Akash network operates is self-moderation. That means providers on the Akash network choose who they want to serve... as long as there's a provider that's willing to serve Parler, Parler can run on Akash. But if there are no providers... that website won't be served on Akash platform."
> — [00:05:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ESTCd9nb5CM&t=345s)

**Context:** His standard answer to how a censorship-resistant network handles harmful content.
