## Summary

CoinFund's "Mined" podcast (hosts Jake Brukhman and Evan Feng) recorded the day DeepSeek R1 tanked Nvidia and the markets, with Greg Osuri and Prime Intellect co-founder Vincent Weisser. Greg frames DeepSeek as vindication of a long-held thesis: OpenAI is "the modern AOL," closed source is not a moat, and any sufficiently successful technology must go open source — chip export controls simply forced Chinese free-market innovation. He argues synthetic data (R1's reasoning-model-generates-training-data loop) broke AI's data ceiling and "accelerated our AGI timeline significantly," leaving energy as the binding constraint: US data centers cap around 8-10K H100s (not training grade), nuclear takes 14 years and ~$34B to build, so single-data-center training caps out "in two years" and distributed training is "the only way out," giving decentralization "a good five-year shot" to go mainstream. He gets concretely personal about local compute: his new house has 60kW of solar and a 100kWh battery and he is building a home data center holding 32 H100s to host inference and his own private local AI, predicting privacy plus the energy bottleneck will drive AI into homes on a distributed grid. On "DeAI summer," he predicts a Uniswap-like breakout for decentralized AI/agents accelerated by DeepSeek, tokenized shared-ownership models with royalties for open models, and — "another prediction" — that AGI is now possible on-chain rather than inside privately funded labs, denting Nvidia's networking-first model. He closes with a libertarian take: remove export controls ("if we didn't have export controls DeepSeek wouldn't exist"), no subsidies, and skepticism that $500B-style state-adjacent investment plans will work. Vincent covers distributed-training mechanics (low-communication training, fault tolerance, DiLoCo lineage).

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [open-source-ai] OpenAI is the modern AOL
> "If you look at similar patterns, OpenAI is the modern AOL of the world. In fact I tweeted about it two years ago on this very exact fact: that any sufficiently successful technology has to be open source."
> — [00:07:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eb-XhauNLNE&t=425s)

**Context:** Reacting to DeepSeek R1 crossing the frontier; he adds that chip export controls to China let the free market innovate its way to cheaper training — "a classic textbook definition of a free market fundamentally working."

### [open-source-ai] Open technologies will exceed closed; communities overpower companies
> "For the first time it's so clear and so obvious and so evident that open technologies will exceed closed source technologies, and being closed source is not a moat at all, and communities will overpower companies."
> — [00:08:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eb-XhauNLNE&t=511s)

**Context:** On DeepSeek breaking big tech's presumed compute moat; he compares the moment to the open browser killing AOL's model.

### [decentralized-ai] Synthetic data broke the data ceiling and accelerated AGI
> "This paradigm shift is a game changer for training, and I think accelerated our AGI timeline significantly, because now we broke the data challenge."
> — [00:12:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eb-XhauNLNE&t=770s)

**Context:** On R1's two-model setup (a reasoning model generating synthetic data to train the actual model); he compares it to protein folding and self-driving simulation data.

### [energy] Single-data-center training caps out in two years; distributed training is the only way out
> "You look at what technologies can enable model training advancement, and if you look around, only decentralization — distributed training — is the only way out. That's why, in two years I think we're going to cap out as to how much you can do in a single data center."
> — [00:19:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eb-XhauNLNE&t=1152s)

**Context:** After citing the Memphis 250MW data center, planned 1.2GW sites, ~96 US nuclear reactors already at full use, the last reactor taking 14 years/$34B, and the ~8-10K H100 per-data-center ceiling in the US.

### [decentralized-ai] Five-year window for decentralization to go mainstream
> "We're not as fast as China to build out nuclear reactors — they can do it in three years. So I feel like we have a good five-year shot for decentralization to take mainstream."
> — [00:19:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eb-XhauNLNE&t=1196s)

**Context:** US grid stuck near 4.5TW for a decade while EVs and other loads come online; inference especially, he argues, can run anywhere on locally generated power.
