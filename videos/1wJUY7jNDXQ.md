## Summary

A 45-minute panel (April 26, 2023) moderated by Matt Beck, Director of Investments at DCG, with Greg Osuri (Akash/Overclock Labs), Robert "Caro" Myers (OpenTensor/Bittensor), and Harry Grieve (Gensyn). Greg introduces Akash as an open compute marketplace for cloud-grade resources, framing it as a secondary market for unused data-center capacity, and declares "GPUs are the new oil" — noting H100s were nearly impossible to obtain (citing Elon Musk buying ~10,000 GPUs) and predicting peer-to-peer markets will be the only way to access them. He recounts his open-source origins (growing up in India priced out of closed software, Kubernetes work since 2013) and argues that because OpenAI is "sucking the air out of the open source AI movement," decentralizing access to compute is essential for open-source AI to exist. He predicts open source will "eventually win out" over closed models, since products lag frontier models long enough for open source to catch up, and companies can't move as far as communities. The panel discusses regulatory capture as the biggest threat to open-source AI (Caro), verification of decentralized training (Harry), and onboarding web2 ML engineers — where Greg argues the draw is offering what centralized clouds can't: Akash launching H100s on mainnet ("the only place where machine learning devs get H100s"). He also warns about concentrated AGI: OpenAI API credits already being "more valuable than money" at SF hackathons, and whoever builds AGI having no incentive to release it. Dated markers: GPT-4/alpaca-era references, Akash GPU testnet, upcoming H100 mainnet launch.
