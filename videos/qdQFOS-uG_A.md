## Summary

A one-hour-plus one-on-one between Cryptocito and Greg Osuri, recorded the day Akash announced its "radical open source" community-groups model (SIGs, working groups, user groups modeled on Kubernetes) — publicly funded specs anyone can build, which Greg frames as "hiring talent by not hiring." He dismisses that week's Avalanche-AWS partnership as a marketing gimmick (just AWS Activate credits) and lays out his definition of decentralization as removing attack vectors layer by layer, arguing Cosmos is more decentralized than Ethereum because no foundation holds the keys.

The core of the interview is the GPU thesis, weeks before Akash shipped GPU support: society runs on a cloud where four companies control ~80% of the market and "half of every dollar" paid to online services goes to them; AI is pushing that system to breaking point since compute demand doubles every six months, A100s take six weeks from Nvidia and are reserved for Amazon's high-paying customers; meanwhile ~$19B of Ethereum-mining GPUs sit idle post-merge as natural Akash supply. He gives a ~3-month timeline for GPUs, cites Lensa-style AI avatar apps ($1M/day revenue, $10-20 per training that Akash could cut to $4-5) as the killer demand, and concedes users care about access, not decentralization. Other segments: why he built on Tendermint/Cosmos instead of Ethereum (gas spikes during the CryptoKitties era), Akash as the first IBC transaction, app-chain migration (dYdX, possibly Compound) favoring Cosmos over Polkadot/Avalanche, planned USDC stable settlement with a "take-and-burn" AKT tax, predictions that composability/shared security get solved in 2023 and ATOM goes "top 10 easy," plus founder-life advice (build in bear markets, guard health, don't start a crypto project unless you believe).

## Topics

decentralized cloud, gpu marketplace, ai compute, gpu shortage, ethereum merge, cosmos, ibc, app chains, open source governance, tokenomics, stable payments, akt, avalanche, regulation

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [ai-agents] Computing is shifting from humans to machines as its users
> "Now we're entering a new phase of computational usage where we're transitioning from humans like you and me using computers to machines using computers with artificial intelligence. So now we are seeing the limits of the existing cloud infrastructure. Why? Because the complexity of computation for AI is nearly doubling every six months."
> — [00:18:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qdQFOS-uG_A&t=1121s)

**Context:** Asked what people underestimate about Akash; two months after ChatGPT's launch he frames AI, not humans, as the coming dominant consumer of compute.

### [gpu-economics] The cloud is breaking down under AI GPU scarcity
> "It takes about six weeks from Nvidia to get you A100 chips. You can't even get them on Amazon anymore because Amazon exclusively reserves those chips for high-paying customers. So if you're a startup company, you want to do AI, it's impossible to get that on Amazon. So we're starting to see the system break down... the only way to reverse that is to take the control back from these cloud companies."
> — [00:19:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qdQFOS-uG_A&t=1168s)

**Context:** His argument that centralized clouds optimizing for profit ration GPU access, making decentralized supply inevitable.

### [gpu-economics] ~$19B of Ethereum-mining GPUs sit idle post-merge — Akash will target them
> "There are about, as far as I estimate in our own interactions, about 80,000 GPUs that were purchased for about 19 billion dollars to mine Ethereum. 19 billion dollars worth of GPUs are sitting idle post-merge... and Akash with GPU capability will be targeting those GPUs."
> — [00:20:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qdQFOS-uG_A&t=1210s)

**Context:** Quantified supply-side thesis, from home mining rigs to data centers "sitting on 20,000 idle GPUs" (he names Hut 8 / web3 cloud operators). The 80,000 figure is as-captioned and sits oddly against $19B.

### [gpu-economics] GPUs on Akash within three months; AI avatar apps as first killer demand
> "We're looking at next three months... we're expecting next three months, but then all our progress is going to be fully public."
> — [00:23:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qdQFOS-uG_A&t=1382s)

**Context:** Timeline for the first GPUs on Akash (GPU testnet ultimately ran mid-2023). Earlier he sizes the demand: each AI avatar training "takes about 30 minutes on a GPU... costs about 10 to 20 [dollars]; with Akash should be able to reduce the price point like five to four dollars," noting "Lensa alone was making a million dollars a day."

### [cloud-decentralization] Users care about access, not decentralization — GPUs are the wedge
> "People don't care about decentralization, people don't care about control as much as you and I like to think. But what they care is access... even though Akash is like 80% cheaper, it's not that hard to get a regular computer on Amazon — there's abundance of it. But I think GPUs are going to be one of those killer use cases for Akash."
> — [00:21:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qdQFOS-uG_A&t=1293s)

**Context:** Candid strategy statement: Akash wins where demand outstrips centralized supply (GPUs), not on ideology or price for commodity compute.

### [cloud-decentralization] The invisible Amazon tax on modern society
> "Half of every dollar we pay to online services like Netflix and Asana goes to a cloud provider, either Amazon, Google or Microsoft. 80% of that market is controlled by four companies... we pay an invisible Amazon tax. When Amazon goes down, half the internet goes down — that's how powerful it is."
> — [00:17:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qdQFOS-uG_A&t=1034s)

**Context:** He continues: "these companies are not elected, they're not regulated, they're not governed by the people, they're completely controlled by their figureheads" — the civic argument for an open cloud.

### [crypto-depin] Composability and shared security get solved in 2023; ATOM top 10 "easy"
> "I think end of the year composability will get solved, I want to see shared security get solved... it'll also make Atom the center in terms of value accrual... none of this financial advice, but I think Atom is going to go top 10 easy."
