## Summary

Akash Weekly Twitter Space (hosted by Nadia Bajuelo) dominated by the week's OFAC sanctioning of Tornado Cash — the first time open-source software landed on the sanctions list. Greg's monologue argues the Slope wallet hack (closed-source code uploading private keys) shows why open source matters, while the Tornado sanction is "analogous to banning electricity because North Koreans use electricity" — though he concedes 80-90% of Tornado activity was illicit, that the team was approached by Treasury first, and that reduced liquidity does make transactions traceable. He warns Treasury (IRS, OFAC, FinCEN) is far more powerful than the SEC. Updates: a community member deployed Zcash for $4/month, Mainnet NYC and Medellin conference appearances, and bounty-program ecosystem discoveries. Guests: Todd Garrison (Blockpane; validator tooling like Tenderduty; got FIO archive nodes running on Akash) discusses why web3 teams still run on AWS/Hetzner and the risk of node-hosting concentration (Greg notes Akash inherently solves provider discovery and remote-signer architectures preserve sovereignty at low cost); Oscars Jepsis (Alter, private messaging on Secret Network) on GDPR, encryption, and becoming an Akash provider — arguing "every single application should host at least their website on Akash." In the predictions round, Greg bets on privacy tech as the next big thing under a coming regulatory hammer, forecasts USDC being KYC'd within two years and crypto splitting into two worlds (KYC'd/regulated vs. anonymous), and endorses community-curated on-chain ban lists. AMA close: no liquidity incentives in a bear market; upcoming provider incentives; and his contrarian thesis that protocols should save incentives for growth post-product-market-fit (citing Filecoin's "reverse incentive" as a cautionary tale).

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [other] Sanctioning Tornado Cash is like banning electricity because North Koreans use it
> "The fact that a tool was blocked because of illicit usage is very surprising, and this is very analogous to, like, I don't know, you know, if North Koreans use electricity — banning electricity. So it's very similar to banning a tool because it was used by bad people."
> — [00:05:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eqwoP7iMh7g&t=301s)

**Context:** First-ever OFAC sanction of open-source software; he still estimates "eighty to ninety percent of the activity on Tornado Cash is illicit" and notes banning it did make transactions more traceable.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash is the foundational decentralized layer to rebuild web3's centralized dependencies
> "Using that as a foundation, as a heavily distributed, decentralized system as a foundation — imagine the kind of tools you can build on top of it, all the way from source code hosting to node hosting to shared storage solutions on top of Akash. I think Akash gives you the foundational layer for folks to build additional abstractions on top that is founded on a decentralized base."
> — [00:41:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eqwoP7iMh7g&t=2501s)

**Context:** Responding to Todd's points that web3 is dangerously reliant on GitHub and Cloudflare (Tornado devs' repos vanished) and that Cosmos validators concentrate in Hetzner data centers.

### [crypto-depin] Privacy tech will be the battleground of the next two years
> "A big thing — my bet would be privacy tech. I think next 18 months to two years we're going to have heavy regulatory hammer throwing down on crypto. I think this is going to be the test of time. I think privacy tech will be the battleground... usable privacy... my bet is privacy-driven every other area — privacy-driven DAOs... I can see deployments being private... money markets being private."
> — [00:46:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eqwoP7iMh7g&t=2801s)

**Context:** Panel question on "the next big thing" in web3; he name-checks Secret Network as a platform powerful enough for usable privacy tools.

### [crypto-depin] USDC will be KYC'd within two years; crypto splits into two worlds
> "I also think USDC will be KYC'd within the next two years. I think we're going to see two clear paths in crypto: one, the KYC'd, heavily regulated side of things and a whole ecosystem on top of that... and then you have this super private, super anonymized crypto world that's going to remain on this side. I think there's going to be two cryptos in the next year to two — it's my take."
> — [00:47:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eqwoP7iMh7g&t=2843s)

**Context:** Extension of the privacy-tech bet; he also expects decentralized identity to cluster with the regulated side, and ZK proofs to enable a "Tornado Cash done better."

### [other] Open, community-curated on-chain ban lists
> "They are on chain and highly visible, and identifiable to a certain degree. So I think these open systems' ban lists are going to be very interesting, because they're going to be curated — especially if they're curated by a community."
> — [00:51:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eqwoP7iMh7g&t=3106s)

**Context:** Arguing compliance (SARs) is easier in open systems — "internet sleuths" researched Treasury's 85 sanctioned addresses; sentence begins "on-chain highly visible ban lists are okay as long as..." in the prior caption block.

### [crypto-depin] Save incentives for growth — don't incentivize product-market fit
> "I'm particularly of a camp where I think it's important for protocols to save incentives for growth and not incentivize product market fit, because then you lose the core sort of like weapon that you have in the journey to product market fit, which is data."
> — [00:56:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eqwoP7iMh7g&t=3365s)

**Context:** AMA on AKT liquidity incentives ("no point of liquidity in a bear market"); he cites Filecoin's pay-to-store as a "reverse incentive" that muffles product feedback, and Uber as the post-PMF success model.
