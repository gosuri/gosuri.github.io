## Summary

Note on dating: the upload date is March 2025 (channel "Founder School"), but the content is unmistakably an early-2023 Akash Insiders livestream re-upload — Greg references "the first two months of 2023," GPT-4's imminent launch, Hong Kong's June crypto legalization, and GPU testnet "within weeks."

Greg walks an Akash Insider host through five early-2023 milestones. First, the "radically open" reorganization: moving roadmap and development from inside Overclock Labs (20 employees vs. ~230 community contributors) into public Special Interest Groups and working groups, with every meeting recorded to Arweave — "public is the boss." He cites Stanford professor David Tse (rendered "David Shay" in captions), who helped design Ethereum's proof-of-stake transition, proposing that Akash borrow security from Bitcoin. Second, the economics overhaul: stable payments (USDC/IST) with take/make fees set by governance, a public goods pool, provider incentives, market-buys of AKT from non-AKT fees, and burns. Third, Akash AI — a verticalized, UX-first product ("three lines of code" to run Stable Diffusion) promised within 45 days, aiming to be "the best AI developer product" because "AI developers are going to use Akash because they get cheap GPUs and good developer experience," not because it's decentralized. He details GPU economics (A100s at ~$0.50/hr vs. ~$4 on cloud, GPU demand doubling every 3.5 months, GPT-4's trillion parameters, OpenAI's $250k/year dedicated-instance pricing) and reveals ~900,000 inventoried GPUs stranded by the Ethereum merge — including top public mining data centers and mining-OS embed deals — expected online during 2023. He predicts China's ChatGPT moment within three months, warns that "labs"/studio-driven chains like Polygon risk losing decentralization, and closes with his investment filters (technical founders, visible work over promises).

### [gpu-economics] A100s at $0.50/hour vs. $4 on traditional cloud
> "We're anticipating from our estimations that an A100 will cost you somewhere around 50 cents, compared to a traditional cloud offering which is about $4 an hour... If you ask any AI dev and offer them an A100 for 50 cents, they will kidnap you."

**Context:** Pre-GPU-launch pricing forecast tied to provider incentives designed to attract high-end GPUs rather than miners' leftover 4090s.

### [gpu-economics] GPU demand doubling every 3.5 months; GPT-4 at a trillion parameters
> "GPUs are the fuel for AI. The need for GPUs is doubling every 3.5 months as these LLM models get more advanced... GPT-3.5 is 175 billion parameters and GPT-4 is going to be a trillion parameters — that means you need that much of compute."

**Context:** Quantifying the AI compute curve just before GPT-4's release; adds that OpenAI was proposing ~$250,000/year for a dedicated ChatGPT instance and "90% of the cost goes to GPU" ([00:34:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=328BxcY4ec4&t=2090s)).

### [cloud-decentralization] Decentralization means removing systemic failure risk
> "It's really about removing the risk of a system failure — removing systemic failure risk. Today you have centralized systems like Amazon and Google of the world: if the company collapses, the product exits, no matter how important it is to you or to the society. If the company dies, the product dies."
> — [00:12:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=328BxcY4ec4&t=748s)

**Context:** His onion model of decentralization: consensus, governance-attack economics, company influence (citing Terra), and — the deepest layer — whether development survives the founding lab, which motivated the radically-open reorganization.

### [crypto-depin] Borrowing security from Bitcoin
> "A person of his nature and stature helping Akash design economics is just the tip of the iceberg... his proposal essentially: let Akash borrow security from Bitcoin — that way we don't need to allocate that many tokens for security, but rather actually borrow from a better security, from a proof-of-work model."
> — [00:11:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=328BxcY4ec4&t=660s)

**Context:** Referring to Stanford's David Tse (captioned "David Shay"), described as having helped Ethereum's move to proof of stake, now contributing to Akash economics through the open SIG process.

### [crypto-depin] Studio-driven chains risk losing decentralization
> "There is a chance the studio will go so big that they determine the protocol completely... I think there's a heavy risk now that it may lose its decentralization nature... and if that model is going to be exported for the rest of the crypto ecosystem — which most likely it looks like it is — then we're in big trouble."
> — [00:41:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=328BxcY4ec4&t=2484s)

**Context:** Naming Polygon (with stated respect for its BD team) as the example of labs/studios accruing power over protocols; contrasts with Akash's radically open model — "make better mistakes tomorrow." Quote spans into [00:42:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=328BxcY4ec4&t=2526s).
