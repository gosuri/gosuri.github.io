## Summary

A Comdex mainnet launch panel on DeFi in Cosmos with Greg Osuri, Tushar Aggarwal (Persistence), and Adriana (Tendermint), moderated by Liam. Greg introduces Akash as a permissionless marketplace for cloud-grade compute at market-driven prices, "usually anywhere from one-fifth to one-third" of market rates. On Cosmos's evolution: Akash worked with Tendermint from 2017 (pre-SDK), diverged building its own modules until switching to the Cosmos SDK in 2019 ("one of the best decisions we ever made"); he credits modular, reusable open-source modules and sovereignty for the ecosystem's growth — arguing shared-state chains like Ethereum and Solana are "phenomenal for developing rapidly" but past a scalability threshold you need Cosmos-style sovereignty (notable in light of Akash's 2025 decision to leave its sovereign chain). Riffing on Persistence's liquid-staking self-repaying loans, he improvises a new idea: DAOs that collateralize AKT to take self-paying loans and buy actual hardware — "you essentially get free infrastructure" that pays itself off. His main DeFi-infrastructure argument: "decentralized finance needs decentralized infrastructure"; with a hostile regulatory climate, protocols must offload liability "from the corporate to the common" (Osmosis running RPC servers, and eventually frontends, on Akash; Strange Clan NFT game), predicting censorship of corporate DeFi entities "is already starting to happen." Adriana covers IBC growth stats (22 chains in 8 months, 260+ apps, ~$165B market cap) and UX-driven adoption; Tushar covers liquid staking, NFT standards beyond ERC-721, and app-chain gamification.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [gpu-economics] Akash compute at one-fifth to one-third of market price
> "Akash is a decentralized cloud infrastructure network, essentially a marketplace for cloud-grade compute — offers compute in an open, in a permissionless way, at a price point that's market driven, usually anywhere from one-fifth to one-third to what you would pay on the market."
> — [00:01:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c4jPJjJz0vo&t=117s)

**Context:** Self-introduction; the standing 2021–22 era cost-advantage claim.

### [crypto-depin] DAOs owning physical infrastructure through self-repaying AKT loans
> "I actually got a brilliant idea after listening here... you can create DAOs that will own infrastructure, that purchase infrastructure — actual hardware — using, let's say you have AKT and you collateralize AKT in a self-paying loan using AKT. So you essentially get free infrastructure that [the DAO] now can own and will be paid off eventually, because [it's] a loan, and then you're just making profit running infrastructure."
> — [00:17:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c4jPJjJz0vo&t=1066s)

**Context:** Improvised on-stage after Tushar Aggarwal described liquid-staking self-repaying loans; an early sketch of DeFi-financed physical compute (DePIN financialization).

### [crypto-depin] DeFi will shift liability "from the corporate to the common" — censorship is already starting
> "Decentralized finance needs decentralized infrastructure... The notion that you're using a centralized infrastructure is just horrible... Today we have an uncertain regulatory climate that's very hostile to anything DeFi and crypto, and the only way to really battle that is to offload liability from the protocol developers to the community — I call it offloading liability from the corporate to the common, because there is no escape from being a corporate: you will get approached, you will get censored, and you will get silenced. And it's already starting to happen. I think we're going to see quite a bit of that transition from the corporate to the common."
> — [00:19:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c4jPJjJz0vo&t=1153s)

**Context:** He cites Osmosis moving RPC servers (and eventually its frontend) to Akash, with DAO governance controlling listings/moderation — continuing his 2021 warnings about DeFi frontend regulatory risk.

### [crypto-depin] Shared-state chains work until a scalability threshold forces sovereignty
> "One of the things that stands out about Cosmos is the sovereignty that we get, unlike other shared-state chains. Shared-state chains like Ethereum and Solana are phenomenal for developing rapidly, but once you reach a certain scalability threshold you certainly need that sovereignty that Cosmos provides."
> — [00:06:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c4jPJjJz0vo&t=417s)

**Context:** Praising Cosmos's appchain model in 2022 — a position he substantially reversed in 2025 when announcing Akash would leave its sovereign L1 for a shared-state ecosystem.
