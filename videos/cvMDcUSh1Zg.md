## Summary

Recorded ~90 days after Akash's GPU mainnet launch, this podcast covers the network's hyper-growth phase. Greg introduces Akash as "the world's first decentralized supercloud" — "Airbnb for GPUs without the cleaning fee" — pooling unused capacity across 8.5 million data centers. He details AkashML, Overclock Labs' for-profit SaaS front end that lets non-crypto ML developers pay by credit card ("we need that Stripe-level onboarding"), and explains why raw Akash is a "race car" for people who know what they're doing. A long section critiques DePIN incentive design: Filecoin/Helium over-incentivized supply and under-optimized demand (Filecoin at sub-0.5% utilization), while Akash deliberately launched pre-incentives to prove product-market fit — claiming 95% utilization on A100s, 250% cumulative monthly revenue growth, and more on-chain fees than Helium, Livepeer, Ripple, Dogecoin, and Cardano. He previews 2024 token incentives (LP-style provider pools designed to push GPU prices down) and cites pricing of ~$2/hr on Akash vs ~$12/hr on AWS for H100s. He shares his "cognitive coefficiency" thesis (value must exceed cognitive load — Vim/film-camera analogy), name-drops the Semafor article "AI chip shortage has an unlikely winner: blockchain," and describes customers (Thumper foundation-model training, medical imaging, in-game character design via Stable Diffusion, Foundry as provider). Notable forward-looking material: AI agents will need permissionless, non-custodial compute (the "Akash Net = Skynet" joke), on-chain AI is 5-6 years out, post-parity means 10,000 regions and 10ms-latency apps, home devices (PlayStation, Xbox, Tesla's ~200 onboard GPUs) becoming providers, and a cited stat that ~5% of global GDP will go to AI compute by ~2030. He closes with his bull-market thesis: Bitcoin ETF trigger + Fed pivot + returning "degeneracy."

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [crypto-depin] DePINs over-incentivized supply and under-optimized demand
> "Looking at the demand side of DePIN networks I came to a conclusion — maybe I was wrong and maybe a lot of these DePINs are wrong — because you're over-incentivizing to a point you're under-optimizing for demand."
> — [00:15:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=956s)

**Context:** He wrote a 2019 paper on bootstrapping free markets with tokens, then reversed; cites Filecoin sub-0.5% utilization and Helium's low utilization as cautionary examples.

### [gpu-economics] 95% utilization on A100s, no incentives needed
> "Now Akash has probably more than almost all the DePIN networks in terms of revenue... doing that without any incentives, and our utilization on GPUs — for high-end GPUs like A100s — [is] about 95%, and overall GPU utilization is somewhere around 50%."
> — [00:18:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=1090s)

**Context:** Argues marketplace health is measured by percentage of goods sold; Akash launched GPUs only after proving organic demand.

### [gpu-economics] Incentives will make Akash the cheapest place for A100s
> "That'll create incentive for providers to bid lower on the prices, so that means you're going to find A100s significantly cheaper on Akash than any other network, decentralized or centralized."
> — [00:19:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=1178s)

**Context:** Describing the planned 2024 LP-pool-style provider incentive design where providers keep bid revenue on top of pool emissions.

### [gpu-economics] GPUs are 36% of all-time revenue after 90 days
> "GPUs launched 90 days ago, and they now account for about 36% of all the revenue that has been ever made in the last two and a half years."
> — [00:29:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=1783s)

**Context:** Answer to what Akash's biggest moneymaker is; GPUs put Akash on a "hyper growth path."

### [gpu-economics] H100s: ~$12/hr on Amazon vs ~$2/hr on Akash
> "For H100s, which is very very high demand, Amazon charges about $12 an hour and Akash charges about $2 an hour — so that's literally the cost difference."
> — [00:42:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=2559s)

**Context:** Pricing comparison; also cites Akash A100s at ~$1.20 vs ~$6.80 on services like Replicate.

### [ai-agents] Bots and agents will come to permissionless compute
> "Akash Net, if you shorten that [and] translate [to] English, it's Skynet — that was not by design... And guess what: when the bots and the agents want compute, they're going to come to a permissionless and censorship-resistant layer that is going to prevent anyone from taking away the compute... they're not going to use credit cards because credit card needs permission — someone's authorization from a bank."
> — [00:54:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=3249s)

**Context:** Prompted by host's on-chain-AGI musing. He adds (00:54:51) that agents are already running on Akash — "there's no way to tell who's an agent who's not because there's no KYC" — and floats Worldcoin-style proof-of-personhood as interesting.

### [decentralized-ai] On-chain AI is at least 5-6 years away
> "If you ask me, I think it's going to take a while by the time you can see something practical in this area... open source AI is doing well and open source AI is not on-chain AI — Akash is great for open source AI. We're at least like five to six years away from on-chain AI."
> — [00:56:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=3418s)

**Context:** On-chain inference is impractical due to verification overhead and latency; mentions ZKML research, Gensyn, and Bittensor (which he says will use Akash for pre-training).

### [cloud-decentralization] Post-parity: 10,000 regions instead of Amazon's 30
> "Once we get to parity we get to post-parity, which I call innovation beyond imagination. You and I cannot sit here and imagine what kind of innovation [there] will be... Amazon has about 30 regions right now; instead of 30 regions you're going to have 10,000 regions."
> — [00:59:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=3549s)

**Context:** Roadmap framing (pre-parity vs post-parity with cloud). He later explains 10ms latency enables applications impossible today (cloud gaming, distributed ML) since "Akash essentially makes any computer a data center" (01:04:05).

### [local-compute] Go into the home: PlayStations, Xboxes, and Teslas as providers
> "Ultimate goal is to get beyond the cloud parity, go into the home. There's so much unused capacity in the home, where you have my PlayStation, my PS2, my Xbox which I use maybe once a year — you're sitting on this massive compute capacity, GPUs — but Tesla, that's not running in the night, is sitting and charging... it has an onboard data center, has about 200 GPUs."
> — [00:59:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=3591s)

**Context:** Core local-compute prediction: idle consumer hardware connects to the supercloud once Akash is reliable enough to "go inside a house" (01:01:17).

### [energy] ~5% of global GDP will be spent on AI compute by ~2030
> "I read a stat somewhere that said about 5% of global GDP will be spent on AI compute... I think in 2030 or something, and it's believable the way AI is going. It's going to need a lot more chips and we don't have capacity as humanity to produce [that] level of chips — the only way we can achieve that level of usage is by looking inward, [at] what we have right now that's not being utilized."
> — [01:00:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvMDcUSh1Zg&t=3634s)

**Context:** Cited (secondhand) stat used to justify tapping idle compute rather than manufacturing new chips; chip supply as the binding constraint.
