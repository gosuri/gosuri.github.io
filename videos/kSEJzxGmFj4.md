## Summary

"Ask Akash" community-call segment (July 2022) where a community member presses Greg on why Akash isn't courting big Web2 companies directly. Greg's answer is a candid walk-before-you-run argument: Akash in mid-2022 was a basic platform (~30 providers, ephemeral workloads plus new persistent storage), so priorities were product-market fit, provider profitability (providers were already making money with hardware tuning), and retention — "if you can't walk you can't run." Enterprises evaluate ease of use, capability coverage, and cost model, not just price; Akash's unique selling proposition against Amazon is heavy geographic distribution from a profitable provider base, open source ("world's first open cloud"), and ~90% lower cost. He envisions scaling to 300–500 providers for geo-presence beyond Amazon and pitches "better, cheaper, faster" with low-latency delivery from hundreds of thousands of nodes. He's honest that UX "is not that great" — built for developers, not "Joe at a bicycle shop" — and explains the legal workaround for credit-card payments: AuthZ-based hosted wallets decoupling payer from user, since selling AKT directly for cards isn't legal in the US. Strategy: web3 users first (they can handle tokens), then small businesses once one-click UX exists, then enterprises.
