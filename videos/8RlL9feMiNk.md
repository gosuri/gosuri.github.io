## Summary

A virtual WCEF panel on web3 infrastructure with John Wu (Gryphon, an ML-powered mesh router launching the "MetaSafe" blockchain that runs on home routers), Jason Morton (Lit Protocol), and Greg Osuri, who joins mid-session. Greg describes Akash as "Airbnb for cloud compute" founded on the fact that ~95% of data-center capacity sits unused, and cites growth stats: ~25 million gigabyte-hours processed in total, 3.5 million in the prior month alone, with top workloads being Osmosis and Juno, plus Chia plotting at ~10 cents versus 40-50 cents on traditional cloud. He coins the trend of "DWS" — decentralized web services (Akash, Livepeer, Arweave, Graph, Storj, Filecoin, Sia) — with each network specializing into niches in a post-product-market-fit growth phase.

Candid moments: he admits Akash's retention "is not as impressive as traditional web2," arguing cost is a hook, not a keeper (the Airbnb analogy), and that decentralization's benefits only prevail behind good UX. On censorship, he warns ~60% of Ethereum nodes run on AWS ("if Amazon pulls the plug, it's gone") and criticizes New York's mining ban as effectively banning SHA-256. He argues one-chain-fits-all failed (Ethereum, Solana congestion), bridges are insecure, and IBC-style interoperability is the way forward. He discloses Akash hit a $3B fully-diluted valuation, that he and his community lost heavily in the Terra collapse, and that Akash has treasury runway for three to four years. He predicts the next one-to-two bear years — a "build market" — will produce tremendous progress and a completely different next bull cycle. Notable prescient adjacent content: Gryphon's pitch that underutilized home routers should earn tokens by providing compute/bandwidth from people's homes.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Airbnb for cloud compute — 95% of data center capacity sits unused
> "Akash was founded about four years ago with an idea of creating an Airbnb for cloud compute. What that means is there's enormous amount of cloud-grade compute sitting in data centers across the world that is underutilized — about 95% of which is not used — and on the other hand we have this crazy demand for cloud with prices only going up."
> — [00:07:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8RlL9feMiNk&t=472s)

**Context:** His panel introduction; adds Akash is "one of the fastest growing clouds in the world" at 25M total gigabyte-hours, 3.5M in the last month.

### [cloud-decentralization] The rise of DWS — decentralized web services
> "We're seeing the rise of DWS, or decentralized web services, I call it... Akash definitely is the small global spot market that never existed before. So I think each of the platforms are finding the niches and specializing to those niches, and I think we're going to continue seeing that solidification, product market fits."
> — [00:10:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8RlL9feMiNk&t=601s)

**Context:** Trend question; he groups Akash, Livepeer, Arweave, Graph, Storj, Filecoin, and Sia under the DWS umbrella, each specializing (e.g., Arweave in permanent storage).

### [other] Cost is a hook, not a keeper — UX decides whether decentralization wins
> "In all honesty, cost is not a keeper. Cost is a hook, but it doesn't retain, because cost is generally a function of demand-supply: once the demand increases, supply reduces, the price goes up, so people start jumping ship... like Airbnb — initially cost was a hook, $20 rooms, but then you eventually got into a mode where you have diversity and variety available to you."
> — [00:14:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8RlL9feMiNk&t=858s)

**Context:** Explaining disappointing decentralized-storage uptake; he candidly admits Akash retention lags web2 because token onboarding and non-custodial UX repel mainstream users.

### [cloud-decentralization] 60% of Ethereum nodes run on AWS — the foundation must decentralize
> "Somewhere around sixty percent of Eth nodes are running on Amazon Web Services. So if Amazon tries to pull the plug, it's gone... The foundation needs to be decentralized, because the companies like Amazon and Google, they have no choice except to comply."
> — [00:21:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8RlL9feMiNk&t=1292s)

**Context:** On New York's just-passed mining ban ("banning SHA-256... a horrible law, I'm pretty sure it's going to reverse in a few years") and hostile regulatory action as a systemic threat.

### [crypto-depin] One chain fits all is dead; IBC-style interoperability is the way
> "The one chain fits all is not a solution — we saw that with Ethereum, and other chains that are trying to have the shared state model are not scaling as they were expected, like Solana or Avalanche... there's consensus that bridges are very insecure and interoperability is the way to go, like the IBC style."
> — [00:24:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8RlL9feMiNk&t=1462s)

**Context:** On what infrastructure components must mature next; he flags cross-chain identity and composability as unsolved.

### [crypto-depin] The bear market is a build market — next bull will be a different world
> "I think the next one to two years of a bear market is going to be tremendous progress with a lot of these problems, and that's going to be exciting. I mean, we see the next bull cycle is just going to be a whole different mode when you see all these problems solved."
> — [00:25:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8RlL9feMiNk&t=1503s)

**Context:** He later calls it "a build market, I call it, not a bear market"; says VC drying up makes people resourceful, and post-Terra "nobody has money, people can actually start focusing on building."

### [energy] Decentralized systems look inward for efficiency, not outward expansion
> "This whole promise of decentralized systems is: it looks inward and it looks to be more efficient with usage, instead of outward and expansionary. I think that's important."
> — [00:20:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8RlL9feMiNk&t=1248s)

**Context:** Recurring Osuri argument (also made at Dcentral 2022) — monetize existing underutilized resources rather than build more; here endorsing Gryphon's router-compute pitch.
