## Summary

A newcomer-friendly Zima Red episode where Greg walks host Andrew (a self-described decentralized-compute newbie) from first principles: why data centers exist, why ~8 million of them run at ~15% utilization (seasonal peaks like tax season), how four companies (Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Alibaba) hold 80%+ of cloud, and why regulation historically kills industries — so the fix is a free market "regulated by math." He recounts Akash's origin: first code in 2013, the Cornell "supercloud" paper, Ethereum smart contracts inspiring the design, CryptoKitties taking down their Ethereum-based prototype, and choosing Cosmos for an appchain that "always works."

Mechanics covered: SDL job definitions, reverse auctions where tenants set price and providers bid, escrow-funded leases, peer-to-peer execution, audited providers for privacy/compliance, and TEEs. He reports 92% GPU utilization ("highest of any cloud, decentralized or not"), supply from mid-size operators (4,000-8,000 H100 facilities constrained by 10MW+ power availability, ~1.2kW per H100), the Brev/Agora acquisition path that made Nvidia a customer, and credit-card payments without losing self-custody lifting activation from 0.5% to 75%.

Forward-looking material is dense: Nous Research and Prime Intellect training the first 1B and 10B parameter models over the internet on Akash, with 100B and trillion-parameter runs to follow; a prediction that decentralized AI "shines" by mid-2025; Wall Street interest in tokenized GPU compute with futures within a year; quantum risk within three years (MIT collaboration to quantum-proof Akash); closed-source AI lobbying on Capitol Hill; 1700% year-over-year growth targeting 1% cloud market share in ~5 years; and a $200k Bitcoin call for late 2025.
