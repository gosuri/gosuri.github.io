## Summary

Episode of Orchid Labs' Priv8 podcast with host Derek Silva. Greg recounts the Akash origin story: AngelHack, the deployment-friction problem, Linux containers in 2013, Kubernetes contributions from 2014, and discovering that 85-90%+ of data center capacity sits unused — leading to a reverse-auction marketplace where "some users experience anywhere from 8x cost savings" vs. Amazon. He frames the market problem in numbers: four companies control ~81% of cloud share, the market heads to $1 trillion by 2025, and "everyone pays an Amazon tax." His signature analogy debuts here at length: "if Akash is Airbnb, Amazon is Hilton" — the network spans mission-critical Equinix racks to devices living in homes, including Akash's in-development Supermini home supercomputer (shown on camera), whose home use case is machine learning. On AI economics, he cites ~20% of AI companies' margins going to Amazon, describes ML startups keeping GPU racks in their offices that idle ~20 hours a day, and proposes those machines earn tokens on Akash when idle — "the tenant becomes a provider... a true peer-to-peer cloud for AI... fundamentally a game changer." He predicts Ethereum's move to proof-of-stake will unlock enormous GPU supply for the network. Web3 predictions: open marketplaces as the defining theme, a shift from password/account systems to wallet-first, and the full cloud stack decentralizing (Handshake, Orchid, Sia, NuCypher). On sustainability, he argues cooling is the biggest data-center cost and decentralization spreads out energy consumption. Closing topics: data sovereignty ("the future is consent"), his personal privacy stack (VPN, Brave, ProtonMail, YubiKey, monthly computer reformats, no phone-number-linked identity).

### [cloud-decentralization] Users see up to 8x cost savings over Amazon

**Context:** Describing the reverse-auction marketplace that "lets the user set the price" — "truly setting a market driven pricing model for the cloud."

### [cloud-decentralization] Four companies control 81% of a trillion-dollar-by-2025 market — the "Amazon tax"
> "Right now about 81 percent of market share is controlled by four companies, and it is about to be a trillion dollar market by 2025. It is a massive market... cloud... is a fabric for today's society... everyone pays an Amazon tax, an invisible tax."
> — [00:09:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2AE257OCMA&t=552s)

**Context:** Argument for why matching idle supply with demand is "a dumb problem to correct" — with cost reduction as the natural economic result.

> "A16z wrote an article recently that basically said 20% of margins for AI companies goes to Amazon. So it is so big that it is actually hurting their viability to exist as a business. It's no longer a secondary cost, it is a primary cost, sometimes much more than payroll... I'm particularly excited about AI workloads."
> — [00:09:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2AE257OCMA&t=595s)

**Context:** Setting up his AI-as-the-future-workload thesis; the attribution to the article's author is trimmed from garbled captions ("recent wrote a article").

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash is Airbnb, Amazon is Hilton — from Equinix racks to devices in the home
> "If Akash is Airbnb, Amazon is Hilton. Akash is a very versatile system, so much so that you can have both very mission critical infrastructure that's running on Equinix, which is the largest interconnected data center network in the world, to devices that can live in the home, with varying degrees of reliability."
> — [00:12:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2AE257OCMA&t=768s)

**Context:** His signature marketplace analogy; he goes on to cite 8.2M data centers, sub-10ms cell-tower colocation, underwater data centers, and future "data centers in space."

### [local-ai] The home Supermini box is for machine learning
> "Something like a box like a Supermini, which is in the home, which is... a low trust environment it lives in — the use cases for this is more machine learning."
> — [00:15:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2AE257OCMA&t=939s)

**Context:** Greg shows the Akash Supermini home supercomputer prototype on camera (pre-orders open, hoped to ship later in 2021 barring chip shortage); users choose trust level — home boxes for ML number-crunching, Equinix tier-4 for financial data.

### [local-compute] Idle office GPUs earning tokens — a peer-to-peer cloud for AI
> "The big problem there is you're only using [the GPUs] maybe two to three hours a day; 20 hours you're sitting there idle. In a case like that they can install Akash and essentially earn tokens for this 20 hours, and when they really want to use them they can use the Akash tokens to effectively get the cost low. So this is a scenario where the tenant becomes a provider and vice versa, really creating a true peer-to-peer cloud for AI, and I think that is fundamentally a game changer."
> — [00:27:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2AE257OCMA&t=1622s)

**Context:** ML companies keep GPU racks in their offices to dodge cloud costs; he adds Akash gets "one or two requests a week" for GPU capacity from leads with "millions of dollars worth of spend."

### [gpu-economics] Ethereum's proof-of-stake shift will unlock enormous GPU supply

> — [00:28:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2AE257OCMA&t=1709s)

**Context:** Announcing the GPU marketplace plans and multi-tenancy work; he contrasts Akash ("run anything you can run on Amazon," e.g. TensorFlow unchanged) with GPU projects requiring code rewrites.
