## Summary

Pre-launch livestream three days before Akash Mainnet 2 went live (March 8, 2021), hosted by Kelsey Ruiz with Greg Osuri, CTO Adam Bozanich, Tendermint VP Engineering Alessio Treglia, and Jack Zampolin (Peggy JV/Cosmos). Greg's company update centers on the Equinix Metal integration — "by far the largest interconnected data center network in the world" (220+ data centers in ~25 countries) — which will bootstrap the network's compute capacity as the first provider. In the fireside chat Greg retells the Akash origin story: Overclock Labs built an edge-optimized Kubernetes deployment platform, found ~8.2 million data centers that could use it, and discovered ~85% of compute capacity is underutilized because companies provision for peak (Intuit at tax season, Walmart at Christmas), while a ~$370B cloud market is dominated by middlemen who exploit demand without solving supply — hence an open, decentralized marketplace. He recaps the year-long Akashian Challenge testnets (consensus, DEX/reverse-auction, platform phases), citing ~480 active developers at peak vs. Polkadot's ~420 and Ethereum's ~2,500 monthly actives. Adam explains mainnet 2 benefits (lower cost via container density on bare metal, censorship resistance, no logins or PII, on-chain TLS certificate distribution) and warns against running validators on the MVP yet (1TB ephemeral storage limits, audited-attributes reputation system still bootstrapping via Overclock as first auditor). Jack covers Gravity Bridge (paying for Akash compute with Ethereum assets like DAI), chain name service, and a future "spin me up a node" wallet-infrastructure use case; Alessio says Tendermint intends to move its own infrastructure off AWS onto Akash. Ends with giveaways and community shoutouts.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] 85% of the world's compute capacity sits underutilized
> "As we started deploying we discovered there's a huge problem that happened to be underutilization of compute... around 85 percent of the compute capacity out there is underutilized."
> — [00:09:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KAOqIm4Mfu8&t=555s)

**Context:** Greg's origin story: companies provision data centers for peak load (Intuit tax season, Walmart holidays), leaving massive idle capacity — the supply side of Akash's marketplace thesis. He also cites ~8.2 million data centers that could leverage Overclock's software.

### [cloud-decentralization] Cloud providers are middlemen exploiting demand without solving supply
> "We have this increased demand for the cloud which currently is about 370 billion dollars and you have this increased supply that's not used, and you have this middlemen called cloud providers that they take advantage of the increased demand but not really solving the supply problems."
> — [00:10:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KAOqIm4Mfu8&t=640s)

**Context:** Core recurring Akash argument — the founding inefficiency thesis that led to an open, decentralized compute marketplace.

### [cloud-decentralization] Equinix Metal will bootstrap Akash's compute capacity
> "The biggest of course is our integration with Equinix. Equinix Metal is by far the largest interconnected data center network in the world — we're talking about over 220 data centers in about 25 countries."
> — [00:02:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KAOqIm4Mfu8&t=172s)

**Context:** Company update ahead of Mainnet 2; Greg adds ([00:04:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KAOqIm4Mfu8&t=256s)) that "as we scale and as we get adoption we will likely see more and more high quality data centers like Equinix."
