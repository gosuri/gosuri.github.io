## Summary

Official livestream of the first Akash Accelerate conference (Austin, TX, on Greg Osuri's birthday). Greg opens with a founding-history keynote: Overclock Labs started in 2015 to make Kubernetes production-ready globally, pivoted to a decentralized control plane in 2016-17, and published the 2018 whitepaper that framed the "supercloud" and named machine learning as Akash's primary use case years before the GPU crunch. He recaps milestones (2020 mainnet, persistent storage, IP leases, 2023 GPU marketplace, private containers, "radical open source" governance with ~500 contributors), claims sub-$2 on-demand H100s and better GPU availability than Amazon, and lays out Console 2.0, managed/custodial options, and a "cloud parity" roadmap aiming at moving mainstream enterprises off AWS. He then hosts a coffee chat with Eric Voorhees (Venice AI) covering Voorhees' Bitcoin history and why AI must be decentralized; asked about Akash's biggest risk, Greg says regulation is the only thing that could stop it. The day includes panels on decentralized AI (Venice, Bittensor, Aden), a Morpheus keynote by David Johnston, a decentralized-compute CEO panel (Flux, Cato Digital, Aethir/Avail), builder demos from Passage, Brev.dev (Nvidia's launchables run on Akash), and Flock.io, and a UT Austin professor's talk arguing decentralized AI is the only viable architecture for healthcare and 6G. Greg closes with an audience Q&A on acceleration, university programs, supply/demand balancing, copycat networks (his tell: closed-source forks, fake numbers, and "we solved distributed training" claims), and the hardest engineering moments (first Cosmos SDK/IBC chain, deleting 90k lines of code). Notable dated specifics: 58M GB-hours served, ~180k total deployments, ~40% lifetime GPU utilization, one integration per month, and an Nvidia AI account tweet about Akash the day of the event.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] The 2018 supercloud thesis
> "We wrote a paper... the subsequent version, 2018, that introduced a notion of a supercloud — the Akash supercloud platform — where we said, well, you can decouple the control plane from the resource plane, where the resources could effectively be any cloud-capable compute."
> — [00:08:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=60mmj3bNzB0&t=501s)

**Context:** Keynote history segment; Greg credits Cornell with formalizing the "supercloud" term and claims Akash's whitepaper articulated it in 2018.

### [gpu-economics] Claimed 2018 call that ML would drive GPU demand
> "In 2018 in our white paper we talked about primary use case being machine learning. We anticipated the need for GPUs... the cost of GPUs and cost of compute was only going to go up for machine learning because the data sets are just getting larger and larger... we specified the primary use case for Akash is machine learning — it just took seven years to realize that."
> — [00:09:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=60mmj3bNzB0&t=543s)

**Context:** Greg retrospectively cites the whitepaper as predicting the GPU shortage/AI compute boom that materialized in 2023-24.

### [other] IP addresses scarcer than gold
> "I think IPs are more scarce than gold — think about it. It'd be cool to have an IP coin someday that's backed by IP addresses."
> — [00:12:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=60mmj3bNzB0&t=764s)

**Context:** Aside while noting Akash is quietly one of the largest IP-address marketplaces; floats a future IP-backed token.

### [cloud-decentralization] Cloud parity: moving Walmart-scale workloads off AWS
> "The goal really is to get to cloud parity, in the sense in a few years from now we should be able to go to Walmart or some large company and be like, hey, you can move all your stuff from Amazon to Akash. We're not there yet — we need to build a lot of capabilities."
> — [00:27:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=60mmj3bNzB0&t=1665s)

**Context:** Roadmap section of the keynote (reserved instances, managed backends, access control, secrets management as the missing pieces).

### [gpu-economics] Akash as the first place to get frontier chips
> "Akash should always be the first place to get the most advanced compute, so we're going to get the Blackwell processors very soon, or GH200s very soon... we're also looking at ASICs. I want Akash to be the first place to get advanced compute before you can go to the cloud."
> — [00:32:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=60mmj3bNzB0&t=1927s)

**Context:** Audience Q&A on what he hopes the community achieves before the next Accelerate.

### [gpu-economics] More available GPUs than Amazon

### [decentralized-ai] Decentralized training is starting to work

### [cloud-decentralization] Regulation is the only thing that can stop Akash
