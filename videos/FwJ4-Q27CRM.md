## Summary

A long, candid interview recorded early in the 2023 AI wave. Greg recounts his background (AngelHack founder, Firebase's launch venue, popular Kubernetes/Go libraries, learning to code in India without owning a computer) and describes his CEO role as a "support function." A major thread is Akash's move to "radically open source" — all meetings, specs, and progress public on GitHub — which he says solved hiring: outside contributors from Google/Facebook-caliber backgrounds now outnumber Overclock Labs employees. He frames his core economic thesis: marketplaces (value exchange) should be decentralized because they optimize for perfect competition, while companies need not be; Akash is "Airbnb for data centers," a spot market for compute. On the Supermini home-mining device: it was halted because Akash wasn't ready for low-power supply and 60,000 prepaid signups created too much reputational risk for a hardware effort during COVID chip shortages — but he says home miners will "absolutely" have a place soon, since Akash's focus is now demand, not supply. He gives detailed GPU-economics views: A100s cost ~$20k with six-week lead times, AI complexity is "doubling every six months," US-China semiconductor protectionism and TSMC's US move will raise chip costs "five times," and Akash's GPU market will launch with at least 20,000 GPUs sourced from post-Ethereum-merge miners. He critiques Flux's hype-driven model and inflationary "pumpernomics," argues the next narrative is "real yield," predicts the bull market returns in 2025, and declares his 12-month mission: make Akash the best platform for AI workloads because "AI is too important to be centralized." He closes arguing there is no decentralization without open source, blaming closed-source FTX/Celsius for the industry's collapse.
