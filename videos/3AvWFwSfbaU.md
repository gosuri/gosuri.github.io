## Summary

Casual sit-down interview with retail-crypto YouTuber Adrian Stewart ("Louisiana Swamp Rat"), recorded roughly two weeks before publication (the first ~8 minutes are a solo host addendum; the interview proper begins at [00:08:38]). Greg retells the Akash origin story: learning to code in pre-internet India, shipping software on floppy disks, the dot-com data-center glut, the rise of AWS ("half a trillion dollars in value... 90% margins for certain products"), and cloud deplatforming/censorship as the motivation for decentralization. He traces the supercloud lineage — the container-native revolution, a 2015 Cornell supercloud paper, and Akash's own 2018 paper ("the world's first decentralized supercloud") — noting the term later became an enterprise buzzword. Timely specifics: the GPU testnet was live (A100s, RTX 3090s), GPU mainnet was "43.5 days" away, ~90% of Akash contributors were outside Overclock Labs, third-party clients (e.g., Cloudmos) were being funded, and USDC payments via Noble/IBC were expected in June. Greg claims Akash launches from "a position of strength" as the only marketplace with A100s during the GPU shortage. Extended riffs cover chip geopolitics (his claim that China stole ASML fab designs and US export controls were retaliation; TSMC's US fabs costing ~5x; "chips are the new oil"), AI usage (GPT-4 makes him ~10x faster at coding), AGI arriving sooner than expected, why machine learning is inherently centralized (praising Gensyn and Bittensor while warning about "crypto GPT"-style scams), and a pitch for Ethereum miners to join the upcoming GPU testnet. Captions are heavily garbled throughout ("Akash" often renders as "a cash"/"a college"/"a cause").
