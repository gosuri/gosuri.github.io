## Summary

An IOSG Ventures panel in Singapore on decentralized GPU compute with Greg Osuri (Akash), Jasper Zhang (Hyperbolic), Shiv (RISC Zero), Ben Fielding (Gensyn), and Trevor Harries-Jones (Render Network). Greg introduces Akash as "the world's first decentralized supercloud" — decoupling the control plane from a resource plane of "any cloud-capable computer" — and claims Nvidia is Akash's biggest user, integrated directly into Nvidia products. Asked about decentralized compute's killer feature, Greg argues it is distribution of supply: enormous untapped compute exists in homes, devices, and appliances (his gaming console is "used maybe once a year"), citing a US Air Force experiment that built a supercomputer from PlayStations; the blockers are distributed training/inference (which is "getting solved") and verifiability. Other panelists cover ZK verification of off-chain compute (RISC Zero), privacy as embedded technology rather than product (Gensyn), creator-focused UX (Render), and tokenizing AI models (Hyperbolic). On GPU supply/demand dynamics, Greg says Nvidia demand is already declining as open-source models run on AMD MI300s, Groq ASICs, Exo Labs Mac clusters, and upcoming thermodynamic chips (Extropic) — Nvidia "has to" lose dominance because "I don't want to live in a digital feudalism." Others add Gartner's projection of 5% of global GDP on AI compute by 2030 (Shiv) and Jevons paradox / Bitcoin-mining-style ASIC evolution (Fielding).

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [local-compute] The killer feature is untapped supply in homes, devices, and appliances
> "Distribution of supply — there is enormous amount of supply all the way in data centers, but also in homes, in devices and appliances. We haven't touched even close to what we can touch when you have truly decentralized systems... The limit — it's limitless, pretty much, because every device has a downtime. For example, the gaming console in my house is used maybe once a year, and it's got really powerful chips."
> — [00:05:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_UG21_OaZLo&t=342s)

**Context:** Asked what decentralized compute's killer feature is versus centralized cloud. He cites a US Air Force experiment that networked PlayStations into a supercomputer as proof home hardware is usable.

### [local-ai] Home compute is usable once distributed training/inference is cracked
> "Yes, we do have a lot of compute at home that's very usable, but we haven't quite cracked the code in distributed training and inference that can leverage various sets of devices."
> — [00:07:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_UG21_OaZLo&t=428s)

**Context:** Naming the remaining technical blockers — distributed training/inference ("getting solved") plus verifiability for permissionless, trustless access to home devices.

### [gpu-economics] Nvidia demand is already declining as open models run on non-Nvidia chips
> "We are already seeing a decline in Nvidia's demand, especially as we start cracking being able to deploy or infer on non-Nvidia chips... With MI300s from AMD you're able to run Llama 3.1 405B pretty well... there's a company called Exo Labs which seems very promising — they do cluster inference on even Macs. So as the models are getting open-sourced and as we see a lot of optimizations happening for non-Nvidia chips, I think the dominance of Nvidia is going to gradually go down."
> — [00:21:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_UG21_OaZLo&t=1309s)

**Context:** Answering whether new GPU generations make decentralized GPU markets a race to the bottom. Captions garble "MI300" as "Mi 350s". Note the Exo Labs mention — consumer Macs doing cluster inference supports his local-compute thesis.

### [gpu-economics] Nvidia won't stay dominant — "I don't want to live in a digital feudalism"
> "As AI gets importance, I don't think Nvidia is going to stay dominant player in terms of hardware. I think it's going to get significantly diversified — and has to, because you have one company... I don't want to live in a digital feudalism where you have one digital lord giving us what we need. Today getting chips is so hard — even the richest people in the world; Elon Musk is famous for saying getting GPUs is harder than getting drugs, even though that's a low bar in San Francisco."
> — [00:23:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_UG21_OaZLo&t=1396s)

**Context:** Concluding his hardware-diversification argument; also cites Groq ASICs and Extropic's "thermodynamic computing" as innovative chips coming to market.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash as the world's first decentralized supercloud; Nvidia its biggest user
> "Akash is the world's first decentralized supercloud, and the core essence of a supercloud is where you can decouple the control plane from resource plane, where the resource plane can consist of any cloud-capable computer, orchestrated in a decentralized and open manner."
> — [00:02:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_UG21_OaZLo&t=128s)

**Context:** Self-introduction; he adds Akash serves users "the biggest user being Nvidia, integrated directly into Nvidia products" and decentralized AI protocols like Venice AI ([00:02:52]).
