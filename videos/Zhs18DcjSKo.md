## Summary

Akash Weekly (Twitter Spaces) hosted by Nadia, with Greg Osuri giving the monthly wrap-up, followed by co-founder/CTO Adam Bosnich and head of product Anil Murty on Overclock Console, which had just debuted at Messari Mainnet and Cosmoverse 2022. Greg recounts conference travel, brand penetration (Coinbase listing Akash as a top decentralized cloud, users flocking to Akash after Hetzner banned crypto workloads), and lays out his cloud economics thesis: on average over 50% of what consumers pay online services flows to cloud providers — a "permanent invisible tax" — and companies will repatriate to their own data centers, using Akash to monetize spare capacity. He envisions Akash as a multi-cloud standard with hyperscalers eventually participating. Anil details Mainnet 4's four features: IP leases (dedicated public IPv4s enabling Solana nodes, VPN servers, and provider IP monetization), the provider-services split from monolith to microservices, IBC v3, and interchain accounts (Adam: DAOs self-managing deployments cross-chain). Adam frames Akash's origin: the internet "lost its way" with centralization (an AWS us-west-1 outage taking down most apps) and the easy/cheap/performant deployment trilemma. Greg explains Console verticalizes Akash for Cosmos RPC/validator nodes (not open source, unlike Cloudmos) because "the base layer in web3 is under assault," and predicts a prolonged, worsening bear market during which the team will hunker down on users. In the AMA he makes his boldest quantified claims: cloud is a trillion-dollar market within ~5 years, Akash is 94% (as captioned) cheaper, Netflix-type bills could halve, and Akash could plausibly reach 100% cloud penetration, with AKT's fixed 389M supply securing that value.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Cloud margins are a permanent invisible tax
> "Over 50 percent, on average, of all the money you pay to online services — like Netflix or the service fees to Uber Eats — 50% of that margin really goes to a cloud provider."
> — [00:07:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zhs18DcjSKo&t=432s)

**Context:** Citing Anil Murty's Messari Mainnet talk; Greg follows at [00:07:57] with "you end up having a permanent invisible tax for everything you do online being paid to one of these large providers."

### [cloud-decentralization] The future is bringing back your own data center, offset by Akash
> "Having their own data center, even though it may be expensive in the short term, actually is good in the long term... bringing back your data center is really where the future belongs. Akash can help you offset some of the costs... instead of needing 100 servers a company can have 80 or 70 servers and offload that unused capacity on the Akash Network, earn tokens, and use their tokens back when they want to scale."
> — [00:08:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zhs18DcjSKo&t=520s)

**Context:** Argues companies like Intuit or Walmart are realizing cloud is no longer the best cost optimizer; predicts repatriation plus marketplace monetization of idle capacity.

### [cloud-decentralization] Hyperscalers will join a multi-cloud standard
> "Akash has always been... to bring people to the table, create a multi-cloud standard. I think that's very likely the future, where you're going to have the big clouds and the small clouds and everybody at the same table, leveling the playing field and giving the consumer the ultimate benefit, which is true cost of the cloud."
> — [00:10:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zhs18DcjSKo&t=606s)

**Context:** Responding to a Coinage Media Twitter Spaces question about what happens if Amazon, Google, or Microsoft join Akash.
