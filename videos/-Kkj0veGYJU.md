## Summary

First episode of a new Akash live stream series, pairing Greg Osuri with Brian Fox — creator of GNU Bash, first employee of the Free Software Foundation, and Orchid Labs co-founder, then an Akash advisor. The first half is a values conversation: Greg (raised on a farm in India, where "open source is the only option") frames open source as collective intelligence and Akash as the first attempt at an open-source cloud, countering a cloud that is "81% controlled by four companies." Fox argues technology exists to enhance human communication and that decentralized networks let people stop trading privacy for convenience; Greg adds a panopticon argument for privacy ("people that don't move do not notice their chains") and recounts self-censorship he witnessed in China. Timely context: the WallStreetBets/Discord censorship episode had just happened, and Greg says his most satisfying moment would be banned communities redeploying on Akash.

The Q&A covers Orchid/Akash synergy (VPN as the last-mile of the decentralized stack, alongside Handshake for DNS and Filecoin/Arweave for storage), Orchid's probabilistic nanopayments (explained by Fox), multi-token settlement via liquidity pools, and machine learning as a "P1" use case — friends' ML startups keep GPU racks in their offices that sit idle 22 hours a day and could rent capacity peer-to-peer. Company updates: AKT all-time highs, mainnet 2 progress (single-command deploy, mTLS auth, inventory fixes), plans for a web-based non-custodial deploy tool with Keplr, Akash as one of seven Cosmos Stargate core developers, and the claim it will be the first chain to connect to the Cosmos Hub via IBC (~mid-Feb 2021). Also an early mention of the Supermini home device contributing low-powered nodes to the cloud.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] The cloud is closed: four companies control 81%
> "That cloud is increasingly becoming more closed, with 81 percent or so market share controlled by four companies."
> — [00:07:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Kkj0veGYJU&t=466s)

**Context:** Answering what inspired him to build Akash; he likens cloud oligopoly to what Microsoft/Apple did to open software.

### [gpu-economics] AI companies pay ~20% of margins to Amazon; data centers sit underutilized
> "Today to run an AI company on the cloud, it's literally paying 20% of your margins to Amazon... you have this enormous amount of capacity... your utilization rates are significantly low in your data center, north of 85 or so in our conservative estimates — you have underutilized capacity."
> — [00:08:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Kkj0veGYJU&t=509s)

**Context:** His core economic argument for Akash in 2021, repeated later citing an a16z report on ML cloud costs hurting startup valuations.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash's mission: society controls the system
> "The mission of Akash is to develop a modern, high performance, sovereign open cloud... where the system is not controlling the society but the other way — the society is controlling the system. It's the first attempt of creating an open source cloud."
> — [00:09:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Kkj0veGYJU&t=551s)

**Context:** Framing computing as "the fabric of human society" put at risk by a closed oligopoly; the crypto token is the sustainability mechanism open source lacked.

### [local-compute] Low-powered home devices will contribute to a larger cloud (Supermini)
> "With Supermini... there's a possibility that a low-powered home-based device can actually contribute to a larger cloud and give a very similar experience... we're seeing this democratization, distribution of these low-powered but very effective computational nodes all over the world."
> — [00:16:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Kkj0veGYJU&t=982s)

**Context:** Answering "why decentralized cloud, and what makes it possible" — cites Helium's token-incentivized home nodes as the model; an early statement of the home-compute thesis.

### [local-compute] Idle office GPU racks will rent out capacity peer-to-peer
> "The pattern is these guys use it like two hours and 22 hours it's just sitting unused. So when that's being unused they can rent out this capacity to the market and earn tokens in exchange, and when they want to use it more than they have, they can use these tokens to get more capacity. I think having a peer-to-peer machine learning cloud is an amazing use case for Akash."
> — [00:44:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Kkj0veGYJU&t=2653s)

**Context:** Machine learning support declared "a P1, priority one for us" — ML friends keep racks of 1080s/2080s in-house because cloud is too expensive; GPU roadmap promised right after mainnet 2.
