## Summary

Akash Weekly Twitter Spaces hosted by community lead Adam Wozny, introducing Chanda Dharap as Overclock Labs' new VP of Engineering, with Greg Osuri joining throughout. Chanda covers her background (StrongLoop, NodeSource, Gigster, Adobe, IBM; PhD from Penn State), her fail-fast product philosophy, and her vision: make Akash the go-to decentralized cloud and "the standard distribution mechanism for all open source services," dead-simple tenant deployments, and provider profitability dashboards. Greg explains the hire frees him and CTO Adam Bosnich from day-to-day management: he wants to return to Akash economics (unchanged since ~2020) and innovative pricing — notably compute futures, which he claims only Akash can do because its market data is open — and R&D on shrinking provider requirements down to IoT devices, plus adding GPUs, bandwidth pricing, and services to match the current cloud. Community Q&A covers the meaning of "Akash" (Sanskrit for sky/ether), AKT staking (~30% APR, higher with compounding or Osmosis LP), and diversity in web3 (Chanda on role models; Greg notes Akash's engineering leadership is all female-led). Greg closes with his competitive thesis: web2 talent is migrating to web3 ("all web 2 is, is ads and SaaS"; "Facebook gave us the 2016 election"), Akash (~600 active deployments) is no threat yet to Amazon's $500B business, but like Airbnb versus Hilton, incumbents structurally can't copy the open marketplace model, and Akash will complement big cloud until it eventually supports "everything Amazon supports and beyond."

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Only Akash can enable compute futures, because its data is open
> "We always talked about pricing, an open model enabling or unlocking futures for cloud... Akash is the only cloud network or platform that can do futures. You cannot do futures on Amazon because the data is not open. You need the data to be open, so we have an advantage."
> — [00:16:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vXOJlsI_CX0&t=991s)

**Context:** Greg's answer on what he'll focus on now that Chanda handles execution: revisiting Akash economics (designed ~2020) and pricing models impossible on centralized clouds.

### [local-compute] Shrink an Akash provider until it runs on an IoT device
> "We wanted to put gas on GPUs, and that leads into minimizing the resource requirements to run an Akash provider to a point it can be run in an IoT device. That's another area I want to start putting gas on, R&D into."
> — [00:17:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vXOJlsI_CX0&t=1033s)

**Context:** Advanced-research ambitions; early (April 2022) statement of the vision that tiny edge/home devices could sell compute on Akash.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash vs AWS is Airbnb vs Hilton
> "Akash has what, 600 active deployments, compared to a 500 billion dollar business which is Amazon Web Services — we're not a threat yet... it's going to be more of an Airbnb versus Hilton. Hilton did not deem Airbnb as a threat until they surpassed Hiltons and all the hotel chains' market cap... similarly Amazons and Googles and Microsofts can't offer what Akash can offer — their model is not set up that way."
> — [00:41:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vXOJlsI_CX0&t=2495s)

**Context:** Answering how AWS/Azure view Akash; argues open compute marketplaces are structurally impossible for incumbents to replicate.

### [cloud-decentralization] Complement big cloud now, exceed it eventually
> "Most likely we're going to not evolve as that threat, but we're going to evolve as a complement to the big cloud... eventually Akash will support everything Amazon supports and beyond that. Let the market decide."
> — [00:43:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vXOJlsI_CX0&t=2581s)

**Context:** Follows the Airbnb analogy; also notes Google hired ~140 people for a blockchain team but "you can't buy a community" — community is the competitive moat. Second sentence is at [00:43:43].

### [other] Talent is migrating from web2 to web3
> "Having someone like Chanda work at Akash is an obvious signal that the talent is migrating from web 2 to web 3... if you even open TechCrunch there's nothing else going on in web two... that's all web 2 is, it's ads and SaaS... anybody that cares about the open web will obviously jump at web 3."
> — [00:38:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vXOJlsI_CX0&t=2323s)

**Context:** Answering how outsiders view crypto; he adds at [00:39:26] that "Facebook gave us the 2016 election" as an example of web2's structural outcomes that web3 attempts to fix.
