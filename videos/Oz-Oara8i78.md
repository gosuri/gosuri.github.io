## Summary

Greg's "first comprehensive livestream of the year" (Feb 2023), hosted by an Akash Insider. Note: video 328BxcY4ec4 ("Founder School," uploaded March 2025) is a re-upload of this same stream. Greg recaps early-2023 milestones across economics, usability, capabilities, and distribution. The centerpiece is the "radically open" initiative: moving roadmap-setting and development out of Overclock Labs (~20 employees vs. ~230 network contributors) into public Special Interest Groups and working groups, with meetings recorded to Arweave — "the public is the boss." He frames decentralization as "removing systemic failure risk," peeling attack-vector layers (consensus, governance economics, company influence — citing Terra — and finally the lab itself, whose death kills most chains). Stanford professor David Tse (captioned "David Che"), who helped Ethereum's proof-of-stake transition, is contributing an economics proposal to let Akash borrow security from Bitcoin. The AKT 2.0 economics update brings stable payments (USDC/IST), governance-set take/make fees, a public goods pool, provider incentives targeted at high-end GPUs (forecast: A100s at ~$0.50/hr vs. ~$4 on cloud), market-buys converting non-AKT fees into AKT, and burns. He previews Akash AI — a verticalized, UX-first GPU product promised in 45 days ("three lines of code" for Stable Diffusion), arguing AI devs will come for cheap GPUs and developer experience, not decentralization. Distribution: ~900,000 GPUs inventoried from the Ethereum merge (top mining data centers plus mining-OS embed deals reaching "maybe even millions"), expected online through 2023. Predictions: China's ChatGPT moment within three months, AI as crypto's next narrative, GPT-4 at a trillion parameters. He also warns that studio-driven chains (naming Polygon) risk losing decentralization, and closes with his invest-in-technical-founders philosophy.
