## Summary

Delphi Digital's "DeAI month" panel pairs Greg Osuri (Akash) with Mohamed of EXO Labs (local/distributed inference on consumer hardware) and Michael of 0G Labs (decentralized AI storage/data availability). Greg introduces Akash as "the world's first supercloud" and reports demand metrics: 50% quarter-over-quarter lease growth and a 10x year-over-year jump in daily fees. He describes the GPU-scarcity windfall — researchers priced out of hyperscalers (University of Texas, Rochester Institute of Technology, a Cornell student's startup later acquired by NVIDIA) turning to Akash — and highlights NVIDIA's Brev using Akash compute to reach underutilized GPU supply. He praises EXO's distributed inference and decentralized training efforts (DiLoCo from Google DeepMind, Nous Research's DisTrO, Prime Intellect's OpenDiLoCo) as proof that decentralized networks will challenge hyperscalers. In a rapid-fire segment Greg calls dedollarization overrated (predicting a "semiconductor dollar"), calls dumb scaling overrated (favoring many small models cooperating), and says AGI by 2030 is underrated. The most notable local-compute material comes late: Greg recounts spinning up a private model on Akash for a personal health diagnosis he wouldn't share with ChatGPT, praises EXO for "sovereign AI" running on family MacBooks, says he is building a little data center in his house to run his own AI, and predicts computing will revert to the "older pattern" with more power at the edge, citing idle Tesla GPUs. Akash's stated success milestone: 1% of the cloud market. Dated markers: post-DisTrO announcement, Llama 405B era, Claude 3.5 Sonnet mentioned.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [local-compute] Computing reverts to power at the edge
> "There's a case to be made that compute will become a necessity in terms of ownership, so people will have compute — own compute... this pattern is not going to be permanent. I think we're going to go back to the older pattern where we have more power at the edge."
> — [00:39:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mODQ86rJws8&t=2341s)

**Context:** Answering what success looks like for Akash in 5-10 years; argues today's thin-client/cloud-server split will invert as AI makes owned compute a necessity.

### [local-compute] Building a home data center; idle edge compute (Teslas) will be harnessed
> "I'm building a little data center to be able to run my own AI... Elon was famously quoted about six months ago talking about how they can leverage GPUs in Teslas. There's a lot of compute on the edge that's just not being used."
> — [00:39:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mODQ86rJws8&t=2383s)

**Context:** Greg says decentralized software stacks leveraging idle edge compute are how Akash realistically reaches cloud market share.

### [local-ai] Nothing beats a private model that doesn't leave my network
> "There's nothing that beats a private AI, a private model that doesn't leave my network... for a health diagnosis I didn't want to talk to ChatGPT because I know ChatGPT is going to share my data."
> — [00:21:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mODQ86rJws8&t=1300s)

**Context:** Praising EXO Labs for bringing "sovereignty to AI"; recounts a personal medical episode where he spun up a model on Akash rather than use ChatGPT.

### [local-ai] Home models on family MacBooks; privacy is underrated
> "If I had a model in my home running on my local compute — unused compute clusters with my wife's MacBook, my MacBook, my child's MacBook — I can easily talk to the model and have the privacy guarantees. I think privacy is extremely underrated in the world of massive AI adoption."
> — [00:22:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mODQ86rJws8&t=1342s)

**Context:** Continues the health-diagnosis story; contrasts a 5-hour, costly Akash H100 spin-up with an always-available local home cluster.

### [decentralized-ai] Decentralized networks will challenge hyperscalers
> "It's only a matter of time that the decentralized networks that can leverage distributed compute will be challenging hyperscalers because of these technologies that are getting to main stage."
> — [00:09:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mODQ86rJws8&t=571s)

**Context:** After citing distributed inference (EXO running Llama 405B) and decentralized training results (DiLoCo, DisTrO, OpenDiLoCo) as converging technologies.

### [decentralized-ai] AGI more likely from many small models than one bigger model
> "It's not necessarily throwing a bigger model that may result in better intelligence... there's a strong probability it's going to be a lot of smaller models that may come together that may lead to an AGI."
> — [00:26:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mODQ86rJws8&t=1601s)

**Context:** Calls scaling laws "overrated" in rapid-fire round; draws analogy to human intelligence evolving via communication and diversity, not bigger brains.

### [cloud-decentralization] Cloud should be as free as air and water
> "Cloud, which is perhaps the most important fabric that holds a modern society together, and a very important public utility, should be as free as the air we breathe and the water we drink, and the access to which should not be gated. That was the whole premise of starting Akash."
> — [00:38:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mODQ86rJws8&t=2297s)

**Context:** His founding vision statement when asked why he focused on this problem; frames cloud as a public utility.

### [cloud-decentralization] First milestone: 1% of the cloud market
> "For us success we measure in market share, right? So our first milestone would be 1% market share of the cloud business."
> — [00:39:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mODQ86rJws8&t=2341s)

**Context:** Quantified success metric for Akash, justified by compute becoming a necessity people will own.

### [open-source-ai] Open source is the foundation of decentralization
