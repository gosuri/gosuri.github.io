## Summary

Long-form interview on the We are DePIN podcast. Greg recounts founding AngelHack (200,000 developers, 50 cities; Firebase launched there), how the deployment problem led him to containers around 2011, and his early open-source contributions to Kubernetes, Docker, and HashiCorp tooling. He indicts the cloud oligopoly on four counts: scarce GPU access (seven-figure contracts and months-long waits for H100s), nickel-and-dime pricing with obfuscated bills, opacity (using the CrowdStrike outage as evidence that centralized cloud is fragile), and moats that make competing with AWS impossible — arguing "cloud is ultimately a public utility" and everyone pays an invisible "cloud tax" of over 50% of online service spend. He gives an unusually candid tour of DePIN's friction problems: 99% drop-off getting non-crypto users through wallets/KYC, legal gray areas around token issuers fronting centralized services, notifications in a post-email world, and Akash's "trial wallet"/authorized-payments workaround (Stripe checkout, 0.5% activation rate they aim to 10x). Notable customer color: Nvidia uses Akash and settles via monthly invoices to Overclock Labs because enterprises won't hold crypto; supply comes from underutilized corporate GPU fleets (Foundry Digital, crypto miners converted to AI) — Airbnb-vs-Hilton economics. He praises Venice AI (he uploaded his own medical diagnosis, trusting its privacy because it runs on Akash), Nous Research and Prime Intellect doing distributed-training research on Akash, and closes with Akash's roadmap from single-service cloud to a "network of networks" of community-operated services.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Everyone pays a hidden cloud tax
> "We all use a cloud and we all pay a cloud tax without realizing — somewhere north of 50% of every penny we pay to an online service goes to a cloud provider."
> — [00:10:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Esawduwv7AA&t=645s)

**Context:** Arguing the AWS/Google/Microsoft oligopoly stifles competition; the quantified "north of 50%" cloud-tax figure is one of his recurring claims.

### [cloud-decentralization] Cloud is a public utility that must be decentralized
> "Cloud is ultimately a public utility that should not be closed off and they should not be this opaque, and that's the reason why you want to decentralize a cloud."
> — [00:11:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Esawduwv7AA&t=692s)

**Context:** Capping his critique of centralized providers, referencing the CrowdStrike outage that "got the planet to a standstill" as proof of centralized fragility.

### [crypto-depin] On-device keys would change the game forever
> "If Apple comes up and says we can leverage the secure enclave to store your private keys, and you can transfer those keys to other devices in an encrypted manner... and we let you use your credit card to onboard... I think that'll change the game forever."
> — [00:22:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Esawduwv7AA&t=1346s)

**Context:** Asked for the single biggest friction blocking mass DePIN adoption, he answers "lack of authentication infrastructure on device" — every phone's secure enclave becoming a wallet.

### [gpu-economics] Cheapest H100s in the industry
> "You just get cheap H100s... which are the most advanced chips for AI for buck forty-nine, which is the lowest in the industry — there's nothing cheaper than that."
> — [00:28:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Esawduwv7AA&t=1730s)

**Context:** Dated pricing claim (Sept 2024): H100s at ~$1.49/hr on Akash, with CPUs at a fraction of AWS cost.

### [gpu-economics] Akash is Airbnb economics vs. cloud's Hilton economics
> "Most compute on Akash is underutilized compute, not cloud compute... same mechanics, same economics as Airbnb versus Hilton. Hilton, they have to sell you at margin, where Airbnb... they have a room they're not using, so they'll take whatever they could get."
> — [00:35:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Esawduwv7AA&t=2117s)

**Context:** Explaining why Akash can undercut AWS: supply is idle corporate GPU fleets (ex-mining boxes, debt-financed H100 hoards) rather than margin-seeking cloud inventory.
