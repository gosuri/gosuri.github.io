## Summary

Host Alex Kahaya (an Akash investor) interviews Greg months after Akash's marketplace went live. Greg describes Akash as "the world's first marketplace for unused computer capacity" — 85%+ of data-center capacity sits idle, Akash prices at ~1/3 of Amazon (down from 1/8 at launch as arbitrageurs added efficiency) — and recounts the AngelHack-to-Overclock arc (Firebase launched at AngelHack; early Kubernetes contributions from 2014). He reports ~150 live applications with 100–150 new deploys daily, "still doubling every month," and sorts users into three cohorts: miners (Monero, Pocket — resource-hard functions clouds ban), the deplatformed (his "anti-de-platforming" cohort; full-stack censorship-resistant deployments via Handshake DNS + Akash compute + Sia Skynet backup), and innovators (Osmosis relayers/RPC, Helium validators, Althea's 5G cores with per-request dynamic pricing). Extended riffs: decentralized systems that embrace their nature build better UX than centralized ones (Uniswap vs. Coinbase onboarding), his "cognitive coefficient" framework (value must exceed cognitive load — Stripe vs. Vim), escrow accounts decoupling payment from operations to enable credit-card front ends, and disaster-recovery as an enterprise entry point. The closing segment addresses censorship vs. moderation: providers moderate themselves (cease-and-desist flows work at the IP/provider level), but the network guarantees anyone can deploy so long as one provider will serve them — "community regulated moderation." Dated specifics: WordPress ~33% of the web, Audius at 5M MAUs, IBC interoperability newly mainstream.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [crypto-depin] Web3 will replace Web2 no matter what
> "The entire stack is getting decentralized, right? So before it was just DNS, but now you have transport level and you have like the wire level... So Web3 is going to replace Web3 no matter what. I mean, it's so fast that before we realize it's going to be gone."
> — [00:16:02](https://www.podpage.com/the-index-podcast/blog/unstoppable-cloud-and-building-a/)

**Context:** Discussing Althea decentralizing the last mile; the second "Web3" is almost certainly a Whisper glitch for "Web2" — the claim is that Web3 replaces Web2 faster than people realize.

### [crypto-depin] A Cambrian explosion of experiences in 3–4 years
> "We just touched the surface when it comes to the possibilities with user experience. With the next three to four years, you're just going to see a Cambrian explosion in types of experiences... looking from the trends of it is going to be amazing."
> — [00:21:05](https://www.podpage.com/the-index-podcast/blog/unstoppable-cloud-and-building-a/)

**Context:** After arguing IBC-driven composability ("small building blocks that Akash offers or Osmosis offers... become more composable") will let permissionless systems out-design traditional ones.

### [cloud-decentralization] Four companies control the cloud — and that is scary
> "You actually end up spending a lot more money on the cloud than running a data center. And these cloud companies now have 81% market share. So if you imagine cloud is a fabric of society... Majority of this cloud is actually controlled by four companies. And that very thought is scary."
> — [00:05:30](https://www.podpage.com/the-index-podcast/blog/unstoppable-cloud-and-building-a/)

**Context:** The Akash origin story — enormous idle supply meets a cloud market "about to be a trillion dollar market," with AWS alone valued over $500B.

### [cloud-decentralization] Private deplatforming power is intolerable
> "The fact that there are platforms that can silence you that are not government-controlled, but private-controlled... considering that these Amazons and Googles and Microsoft can shut you down if they don't like you... That much power in few is not something that we can sleep with."
> — [00:09:24](https://www.podpage.com/the-index-podcast/blog/unstoppable-cloud-and-building-a/)

**Context:** Describing his second user cohort — anti-deplatforming deployments; he pairs it with the full decentralized stack (Handshake DNS + Akash + Sia Skynet) at [00:10:09].

### [crypto-depin] Personal decentralization as a coming trend
> "I use a decentralized VPN and I use all my blogs, all my websites are on a decentralized cloud. I use Handshake. I use [Sia] to store my storage. A lot of my life is actually migrated pretty successfully to decentralized. And I think that's going to be a trend."
> — [00:16:44](https://www.podpage.com/the-index-podcast/blog/unstoppable-cloud-and-building-a/)

**Context:** Offered as evidence these are "real products people are using" — predicting individual migration to decentralized infrastructure despite current friction.

### [cloud-decentralization] Decentralized systems build superior UX
> "Decentralized systems, when they embrace decentralization, end up building far superior user experiences than a centralized system. For example, Uniswap... It takes 30, 40 steps by the time you can swap an asset [on Coinbase]. Versus you come to Uniswap, you click, click, click, and you swap."
> — [00:18:54](https://www.podpage.com/the-index-podcast/blog/unstoppable-cloud-and-building-a/)

**Context:** Contrarian UX thesis — permissionless state systems allow radically simpler interfaces; he extends it to "similar experiences for something like Cloud, where you can deploy permissionlessly by using your Keplr wallet" at [00:20:20].

### [cloud-decentralization] Censorship by the few is censorship on the many
> "Censorship by the few is censorship on the many, essentially, right? So, censorship in general is a terrible thing. I don't want anybody to have the ability to silence somebody."
> — [00:32:37](https://www.podpage.com/the-index-podcast/blog/unstoppable-cloud-and-building-a/)

**Context:** Asked how Akash prevents abuse of censorship resistance; he distinguishes censorship from moderation — providers may refuse to serve, but "as long as there's one provider that's willing to serve you, you'll be served" ([00:33:59]).

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash workloads doubling every month
> "It's growing really fast, and it's still doubling every month if you think about it, the amount of workloads, active applications being deployed. And I'm so proud that we actually now have a fully, a good viable alternative to Google, Microsoft, and Amazon that is fully open."
> — [00:07:08](https://www.podpage.com/the-index-podcast/blog/unstoppable-cloud-and-building-a/)

**Context:** Growth snapshot from August 2021 — ~150 active applications, 100–150 new deploys/day, price ~1/3 of AWS (stated at [00:01:33]).
