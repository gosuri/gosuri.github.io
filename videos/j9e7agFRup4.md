## Summary

An "Akash Analysis" clip (speaker uncredited; consistent with Greg — "I'm kind of proud of our decision") on censorship resistance. Akash has become an ecosystem of independently developed protocol clients, "exactly how we anticipated," which matters because "the censorable part of the decentralized stack is not the actual protocol but the front end itself." Since neither Akash Network nor Overclock Labs develops or operates a default front-end, compliance requirements are much lower and the protocol stays credibly neutral (users still must obey OFAC sanctions and local law). He contrasts Pocket Network, which had to ban Tornado Cash because the company operates a front-end sharing the network's name — a "front-end ban" that confusingly looked like a protocol ban, which he disapproves of.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] The front-end is the censorable layer — so Akash has none
