## Summary

A four-segment radio-style interview with host Keith Koo, recorded weeks before Overclock Labs announced what would become Akash Network (here still called "Photon Network," with a first prototype ship date of March 19 and a mid-June launch). Greg tells his origin story — growing up on a farm in India, learning to code on friends' computers, founding AngelHack (150k+ developers, Firebase launched there), writing widely-used Go infrastructure libraries — and explains the genesis of Overclock Labs: cloud costs are high, data doubles every two years, and roughly 80% of data-center capacity sits unused because operators must over-provision for peak. Phase one was DISCO, an interoperability layer for moving containerized workloads across clouds; phase two is Photon, a blockchain-based open procurement marketplace connecting latent data-center capacity directly to consumers, cutting compute cost to one-half to one-fifth. Greg gives his systemic critique of cloud centralization: three companies heading toward 80% of internet-originating traffic, "burgers and fries" economics where value-added services (often white-labeled open source, e.g. Google Dataproc vs. Databricks Spark) lock users in and stifle open-source innovation ("the Amazon problem"). Side segments cover the Circle/Poloniex acquisition, Wyoming's utility-token law (Greg argues most token sales are securities and predicts 2018 will be "the year of security tokens"), the Tesla cloud cryptojacking incident (exposed API keys; he mentions Overclock itself was hacked via a vendor-leaked key), and transparency/auditability benefits of decentralized infrastructure for regulated industries. Notably dated: early ML customers "knocking on our doors" for cheaper compute, and photonnetwork.org/Telegram as the community channels.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Three companies will control 80% of internet traffic
> "In two years, majority of the internet traffic will come from cloud infrastructure providers, out of which three companies are going to have 80% market share. So, majority of the internet is going to come from three companies. That's one problem."
> — [00:24:08](https://omny.fm/shows/the-silicon-valley-insider-show/sv-insider-3-2-18-podcast)

**Context:** Asked to lay out the problems with the current cloud landscape; his core oligopoly thesis, quantified with a two-year horizon (from March 2018).

### [cloud-decentralization] 80% of data-center capacity is unused; edge cuts cost 2–5x
> "80% of the capacity sitting in data centers today is unused. So by connecting this latent capacity with the users that want to access this, we can reduce the cost of compute significantly anywhere from one half to one fifth."
> — [00:07:36](https://omny.fm/shows/the-silicon-valley-insider-show/sv-insider-3-2-18-podcast)

**Context:** The founding thesis of Overclock Labs — connecting edge nodes ("close proximity to the user") achieves higher price performance using latent data-center capacity. The 1/2–1/5 cost claim recurs as Photon's mandate at [00:15:17].

### [other] Data processed doubles every two years
> "The data, amount of data we process is doubling every two years. So that's when we're like looking at solving this problem. And we discovered there's a lot of latent capacity lying around."
> — [00:06:47](https://omny.fm/shows/the-silicon-valley-insider-show/sv-insider-3-2-18-podcast)

**Context:** His explanation for why compute cost/speed pressure keeps growing despite hardware advances; used to justify the latent-capacity marketplace.

### [cloud-decentralization] Society must take compute back
> "There's that innovation being stifled here. And it's so many things that are wrong with centralization of the cloud. And considering compute is a very, very important, you know, foundational element of our society, we should, as people, have responsibility to take it back."
> — [00:28:01](https://omny.fm/shows/the-silicon-valley-insider-show/sv-insider-3-2-18-podcast)

**Context:** Closing his argument about the "Amazon problem" — clouds white-labeling open-source projects (Google Dataproc vs. Databricks Spark) and out-competing their creators.

### [cloud-decentralization] "Burgers and fries" cloud lock-in economics
> "Cloud provides compute, raw compute, bandwidth, memory, CPU, whatnot, and storage. On top of that, they provide value-add services. And the revenue model is basically burgers and fries. You got to sell burgers and not sell fries. But you make money on fries."
> — [00:25:43](https://omny.fm/shows/the-silicon-valley-insider-show/sv-insider-3-2-18-podcast)

**Context:** One of his longest-running recurring arguments (repeated for years afterward): clouds lose money on raw compute and lock customers in via ~200 value-added services.

### [crypto-depin] 2018 will be the year of security tokens
> "I think 2018 is going to be the year of security tokens. You're going to see a lot less utility tokens. I know folks that are getting investigated... My recommendation is folks should really explore more securities than utilities and do it the right way."
> — [00:23:19](https://omny.fm/shows/the-silicon-valley-insider-show/sv-insider-3-2-18-podcast)

**Context:** Discussion of Wyoming's utility-token law; Greg argues selling tokens pre-utility with expectation of return is a security, and mentions Reg A+ as an alternative path.

### [cloud-decentralization] Photon's mandate: cut cloud cost to 1/5–1/2
> "We're calling it Photon, right, for now, but Photon's mandate is to solve the cost of cloud computing anywhere from one-fifth to one-half."
> — [00:15:17](https://omny.fm/shows/the-silicon-valley-insider-show/sv-insider-3-2-18-podcast)

**Context:** Pre-announcement of the network that became Akash — "we're going to announce this thing basically in a week or two," first prototype March 19, 2018, launch targeted for mid-June 2018.
