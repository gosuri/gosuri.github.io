## Summary

One of Greg's earliest long-form interviews (CoinBundle, Nov 2018), pre-mainnet. He recounts his origin story: learning to code at 12 in a collaborative environment in India, renting game floppies as a first business, a bioinformatics degree where compute access was scarce, moving to San Francisco, and founding AngelHack (which he describes as the world's largest hackathon ecosystem — 450,000 developers, events in 50 cities). He explains how Overclock Labs began as a traditional venture-backed, closed-source company trying to resell idle data-center capacity, and the trust problem ("what's the guarantee you're gonna be around?") that drove the pivot to a fully open-source, decentralized model — the "aha moment" coming from reading the Bitcoin and Ethereum papers. Key claims: 85% of server capacity sits unused (with the Intuit/TurboTax peak-capacity example); cloud cost is a "cloud tax"/"Amazon tax"; three companies (then ~60% share) would control 80% of cloud market share within two years, an oligopoly he compares to US Steel in the Industrial Revolution; Akash could guarantee 50% price reduction immediately and target 90% with scale. He also gives token-evaluation advice for retail investors (store/measurement/exchange value framework; bet on teams, traction, technology) and previews the roadmap: an MVP running a regular web app on the decentralized network had just shipped, testnet then mainnet targeted "by September" (2019), supply partnerships with Packet and pilot customer Life360, plus a planned machine-learning-optimized cloud. Notably dated: the mainnet timeline and the "80% in two years" forecast.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Three companies will control 80% of cloud in two years
> "It's a cloud tax — we all pay the cloud tax, we all pay the Amazon tax. More importantly, the direction cloud infrastructure is going today is scary, considering three companies will control 80% of market share in two years. When three companies, oligarchs, control foundational elements of the internet, it's a very scary situation."
> — [00:23:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Don1slbJlMQ&t=1401s)

**Context:** Asked how everyday users benefit from Akash; he frames cloud concentration (then ~60% share) as a societal danger, comparing it to US Steel's dominance in the Industrial Revolution.

### [gpu-economics] Akash can cut cloud prices 50% now, 90% at scale
> "Some of our customers are paying huge amounts to the cloud today, and we believe we can bring that price down by 90% by creating this decentralized economy. Right now with our supply we can guarantee a 50% reduction in price; with our current ecosystem we will try to get to 90% with higher demand."
> — [00:22:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Don1slbJlMQ&t=1358s)

**Context:** Explaining the AKT token's role as pure exchange of value between businesses with spare capacity and customers seeking price relief.

### [local-compute] Devices will outnumber humans; the future needs edge capacity served by people, not hyperscalers
> "We're moving to a more decentralized infrastructure where you have devices more than humans accessing the cloud, and a lot of these devices need edge capacity. Cloud is not gonna provide that because the economics doesn't really work — they're focusing on building hyperscale data centers, whereas the world and innovation is moving towards more distributed and decentralized computing. Who's gonna serve this future? It can't be the cloud — it has to be people. It's very important to take back control of a foundational element of society back from the oligarchs, and that's the mission for Akash."
> — [00:25:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Don1slbJlMQ&t=1536s)

**Context:** His closing argument on why decentralization matters long-term: IoT/edge growth makes hyperscaler economics unworkable, so compute supply must come from people. Early statement of the compute-goes-local thesis. (Captions render "edge" as "age.")

### [gpu-economics] 85% of server capacity sits unused
> "I wanted to solve the cost problem by tapping into slack capacity — the 85% of server capacity today sits unused. Extreme example would be Intuit... they run at 97 or so percent capacity; come April 16th, they drop it down to three or four percent."
> — [00:09:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Don1slbJlMQ&t=574s)

**Context:** The founding supply-side thesis for Akash: enterprises provision for peak, leaving idle capacity that a marketplace can monetize. Recurring statistic in his later talks.
