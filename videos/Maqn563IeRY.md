## Summary

An "Ask Akash" Q&A clip answering what problem Akash solves and whether it scales. The speaker (uncredited; consistent with Greg's voice and recurring arguments) frames Akash as "the first viable open cloud": cloud infrastructure is controlled by a few closed companies (Amazon, Microsoft, Alibaba, Google), yet "cloud is the fabric of human society" holding everyone's data with no transparency on handling or pricing. Akash decentralizes this "oligopolized closed system" into a non-custodial marketplace claimed to be 97% cheaper, sourcing anyone's unused (mostly data center) compute via reverse auction. Core thesis: improve the efficiency of global computation, since "conservatively over 85 percent of world's computers [are] not used." He cites a16z's Martin Casado on cloud repatriation (a company saving 40x by building an in-house GPU kit) but notes repatriation still leaves hardware idle — Akash lets owners rent it out. He closes with an energy argument: idle servers still burn power and cooling, so rather than being "expansionary to get more power," Akash looks inward to reuse what's already deployed.
