## Summary

An early community livestream with Greg Osuri and marketing manager Michael, three months after Akash's September 25, 2020 mainnet launch and five days into the Phase 3 testnet ("world's first non-custodial cloud"). Greg recaps Phase 1 (a zero-fee spam attack — 3.6M transactions, 5,000 tx/block — that taught them "free is not always free") and an AWS outage that revealed only 10-15 of Akash's validators ran on AWS, versus "60% of Ethereum's nodes." Phase 3 blew past expectations: ~800 applications deployed against a goal of 100-400, spanning DeFi front-ends, multi-tier apps, and ~140 blockchain nodes, with 250+ unique users and 135 validators.

The AMA is a time capsule of Greg's 2020 theses: Akash is 2-10x cheaper by tapping the ~85% of data-center capacity sitting unused (his Intuit/TurboTax peak-provisioning example); DeFi exchanges will "very soon" be shut down by regulators, and Akash — the "only cloud for DeFi" — lets users deploy their own DEX front-ends, shifting liability away from teams; go-to-market deliberately targets DeFi and machine learning as underserved markets before enterprise/GDPR/ISO compliance, invoking Amazon's S3-first history. He describes Solana hosting (UDP port support built for their validators), "ownerless services" where a Solana node's earnings pay Akash directly, the AKT model where stakers eventually take 20% of hosting fees, the Supermini home appliance (plug in and earn AKT, with a powerful GPU), multi-currency settlement via Kava/USDX, and Akash's relayer work as an early IBC contribution. Equinix is named as the biggest provider, Scalar Capital as a fund switching from AWS, and Brian Fox's advisor role is announced.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] 85% of global cloud capacity sits unused
> "We tap into world's unused cloud capacity — 85% of the global cloud capacity that's sitting in data centers is not used... probably even more, we're being conservative with our estimates."
> — [00:32:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=1975s)

**Context:** His supply-side thesis, illustrated with Intuit: TurboTax servers peak at 95-96% in tax season but run ~10% the other nine months, and servers have a ~3-year shelf life — "if you don't use it you lose it."

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash: 2x-10x cheaper than the incumbent cloud
> "Akash Network is the world's first non-custodial cloud that can offer anywhere up to two times to ten times lower cost than the current cloud."
> — [00:30:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=1801s)

**Context:** Rebutting a questioner calling Akash a "blockchain solution to a problem that doesn't exist"; he adds cost savings matter most for machine learning and big-data workloads.

### [local-compute] Supermini: a home appliance you plug in to earn tokens
> "We made it so that it is a home appliance and not just a server data center appliance... the idea is to have this plug-in earn mechanism where you just plug it in and just earn Akash tokens without having to do a single thing... it does have a very powerful GPU."
> — [00:55:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=3335s)

**Context:** Supermini described as Akash's reference architecture for non-technical users — frictionless passive income from home hardware; initial order of 300 units shipping, 400-500 on the waitlist. Early home-compute thesis.

### [crypto-depin] DeFi front-ends will be shut down — Akash shifts liability to users
> "Very very soon we're going to see a lot of these DeFi exchanges getting shut down because they're facilitating money transactions without proper banking licenses. What Akash gives them is a way out... they can have users use Akash to launch their own dexes by themselves... they ship the liability to the user... Akash is primed as the only cloud for DeFi that can do this — no other cloud can do this in the world."
> — [00:35:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=2105s)

**Context:** A 2020 regulatory prediction (predating the 2021-2023 DeFi front-end enforcement wave) used to position censorship-resistant hosting as Akash's moat.

### [crypto-depin] "Ownerless services": nodes that pay for their own hosting
> "Because Akash is a decentralized network you don't really have sign-up, you don't really have the concept of an owner... you earn income running the Solana node — that income can go directly from the node to Akash, paid in Solana tokens, when we interoperate. That's the future vision... a very holistic frictionless experience, and this is impossible in the cloud... a fully interoperable cloud."
> — [00:47:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=2871s)

**Context:** Explaining the Solana partnership (TCP/UDP port support built for their validators); an early sketch of autonomous, self-paying infrastructure.

### [crypto-depin] Stakers will take 20% of hosting fees — the $300B math
> "It takes about twenty five thousand dollars to run Ethereum — twenty percent of that will go to Akash stakers... the industry being 300 billion dollars, do the math: 20% of that at 100% market penetration — that's quite a lot of money."
> — [00:51:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=3081s)

**Context:** Asked about the business model; Overclock Labs takes no cut and profits by staking AKT, with inflation rewards designed to give way to a fee share.

### [other] Ethereum runs 60% on AWS — "we'll change that very soon"
> "Most of our community is not very pro-AWS, unlike Ethereum community which has 60% of the nodes on AWS. We'll change that very soon."
> — [00:05:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=343s)

**Context:** After an AWS outage during testnet revealed only 10-15 of ~100 Akash validator nodes went down; the centralization stat is his recurring critique of proof-of-stake infrastructure.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash as the gateway to the decentralized cloud stack
> "A big vision for us for Akash is to become the gateway for decentralized cloud... what we want is to enable Akash users to use key management on NuCypher and pay using Akash token... so that cross-chain interoperability is very, very important for Akash."
> — [01:00:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=3630s)

**Context:** Answering why Akash integrated Kava/USDX; multi-currency settlement so one token pays for compute, storage, DNS, and key management across networks — "just like you don't use 100 credit cards."

### [decentralized-ai] DeFi and machine learning first, enterprise later (the S3 playbook)
> "Amazon Web Services, the behemoth, started off offering one product and that is archival storage — S3... what they had is an incredibly valuable product that nobody was building, and a market for it. Our product is an unstoppable low cost high quality cloud that nobody is building, and we're serving an underserved market which is the DeFi and machine learning."
> — [01:07:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1ct2eC_Nw&t=4064s)

**Context:** Asked about enterprise compliance (GDPR/ISO); he says compliance comes "eventually," but ML users switch immediately for 50% cloud savings — an early (Dec 2020) bet on ML as Akash's core demand.
