## Summary

New to The Street TV interview with Greg roughly six months after Akash's March 2021 launch. He recaps his background ("I build things for people that build things" — 25 years programming, AngelHack, Firebase's launch) and Akash's origin: while deploying edge software for Overclock Labs, he found north of 85% of data center capacity unused, inspiring an open marketplace where developers set prices. He sizes the cloud at $370B with Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and Alibaba controlling up to 80%, cites 8.4 million data centers globally, and deploys his Airbnb-vs-Hilton analogy. He argues closed clouds lack public scrutiny (citing recent Akamai/Amazon outages), that a billion people without credit cards can now access cloud via Akash, and that permissionless systems scale like the internet did once "broken from the jails of AOL." His framing: "what Bitcoin did to money, Akash is doing to cloud computing." He walks through web1 (data centers) → web2 (cloud providers as rent-seeking middlemen) → web3 (returning to the internet's decentralized design), claiming every layer of the stack is decentralizing (Helium/Sentinel bandwidth, Handshake/ENS naming, Filecoin/Skynet/Arweave storage, Akash compute as "the glue"). Users split into arbitrageurs (miners) and innovators (NFT games like Strange Clan, Minecraft servers, DAOs like Osmosis, unstoppable WordPress, Althea's 5G dynamic bandwidth pricing). Notably, he predicts machine learning will dominate future usage, citing ubiquitous compute from gaming PCs to self-driving cars with ~6 GPUs each charging overnight. Growth: doubling monthly, ~500 live applications.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [local-compute] Future demand is machine learning on ubiquitous compute — gaming PCs to self-driving cars
> "We foresee most of our use cases coming from machine learning... there's so much ubiquitous compute available, all the way from gaming PCs to Ethereum network coming from proof of work to proof of stake, to even self-driving vehicles — each of these vehicles have about six GPUs, and we're in early conversations with some of these makers that want to repurpose some of the unused capacity because they're charging in the night. So we see mining today, but future's machine learning."
> — [00:15:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NsXA2hC9dPw&t=959s)

**Context:** 2021 prediction that ML would displace crypto mining as Akash's main use case, explicitly including consumer and edge devices (gaming PCs, parked EVs) as compute supply.

### [cloud-decentralization] What Bitcoin did to money, Akash is doing to cloud computing
> "Decentralization generally means a more equitable — it is the great equalizer... if you look at what's happening with Bitcoin or Ethereum... similarly, what Bitcoin did to money, I think Akash is doing that to cloud computing."
> — [00:10:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NsXA2hC9dPw&t=612s)

**Context:** His recurring one-line thesis, offered while explaining decentralization as repurposing existing infrastructure vs. expansion.

### [cloud-decentralization] Every layer of the cloud stack is decentralizing
> "Every stack of the cloud today is getting decentralized, all the way from bandwidth allocation — you have networks like Sentinel or Helium or Orchid... discovery layers such as Handshake or ENS domains that are decentralizing DNS... compute layer, Akash is the player there... storage layer, we have Filecoin and Skynet and Arweave... every element of the stack is decentralizing, and Akash, being the compute layer, is the glue that brings all these decentralized networks in a form that's easily available to the developer."
> — [00:12:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NsXA2hC9dPw&t=746s)

**Context:** His web3 infrastructure thesis: web3 fixes web1's inefficiency and removes web2's rent-seeking middlemen.

### [cloud-decentralization] Users pay an invisible Amazon tax that web3 will lower
> "Everyday users are actually paying invisible Amazon tax, if you know it or not, and that will be lowered without even knowing what happened. So web3 is an infrastructure layer that's optimizing and building a more equitable cloud."
> — [00:13:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NsXA2hC9dPw&t=832s)

**Context:** Predicting web3 infrastructure savings will invisibly pass through to consumers of services like Netflix.
