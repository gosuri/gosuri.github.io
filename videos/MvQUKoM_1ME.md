## Summary

A 2021 TFiR interview with host Swapnil Bhartiya, pre-GPU-era Akash. Greg lays out the founding premise: cloud is "the fabric of society," AWS alone is valued around $500 billion, yet 85-95% of data-center compute sits unused because everyone provisions for peak — Akash unlocks that capacity into an open, permissionless, self-sovereign marketplace with market-driven (reverse-auction) pricing. He contrasts Akash's prepaid, single-interface, transparent model with hyperscalers' nickel-and-dime postpaid billing, calls cloud cost savings "a fallacy" (citing Dropbox/Snapchat repatriating to data centers), and argues the data-gravity/lock-in problem is a pricing problem (egress fees), not a technological one — with a bandwidth marketplace in the works alongside providers like Equinix Metal. He defines decentralized cloud via three attributes (political decentralization, architectural decentralization, shared state), argues cloud should be scrutinized like power or water utilities and "taken back from oligopolies," and walks through the deploy flow (containers, SDL, reverse auction, peer-to-peer leases) plus composability with Sia/Skynet storage, NuCypher KMS, and Handshake DNS. Three user cohorts: blockchain infrastructure (Osmosis launched Akash-first over Amazon), anti-deplatforming/disaster-recovery users (Parler-era concerns; deplatforming "weaponized by state actors"), and compute arbitragers (Monero/Pocket miners on bare metal ~one-third cheaper than Amazon). Growth snapshot: ~10 new active applications and ~150 deploys per day. He predicts a decentralized services catalog eventually out-scaling Amazon's ~250 managed services.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] 85-95% of data-center compute sits unused
> "While the demand is there, we have enormous supply that's being unlocked in data centers, because turns out 85 to 95 percent of the compute in data centers remain unused... you normally plan for peak and you always want to keep peak capacity."
> — [00:00:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=46s)

**Context:** The founding thesis: Akash unlocks locked-up capacity into "the first open source, peer-to-peer, permissionless, self-sovereign cloud" priced by the market instead of institutions.

### [cloud-decentralization] Saving money on cloud is a fallacy — repatriation is real
> "The whole thing about you saving money on a cloud is a fallacy... it's a trend now, there's a repatriation of data centers: any high-growth Silicon Valley company like Dropbox or Snapchat, they're actually moving back to data centers for a reason."
> — [00:06:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=388s)

**Context:** Responding to the host's point that you need to be a mathematician to compute cloud pricing; Akash's answer is monetizing underutilized capacity with "sovereignty on both your performance as well as your cost."

### [cloud-decentralization] Data gravity is a pricing problem, not a technological one
> "The data gravity problem is not a technological problem, it is a pricing problem... the root of that problem is the egress costs... they want your data to stay in the cloud."
> — [00:08:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=518s)

**Context:** On vendor lock-in; says Akash is designing a bandwidth marketplace with Equinix (its largest provider at the time) to price data transfer equitably ([00:09:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=559s)).

### [cloud-decentralization] Cloud is a public utility that must be taken back from oligopolies
> "If we look at cloud as a public infrastructure, a public utility, we don't have anywhere close the level of scrutiny the other public utilities like power or water has... when we recognize cloud is important, it's important to take back the cloud from oligopolies, for lack of a better word."
> — [00:14:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=853s)

**Context:** Citing Cloudflare/Amazon outages caused by unscrutinized misconfigurations; decentralization brings the public oversight a mission-critical utility deserves. Quote spans into [00:14:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=898s). An early energy-utility analogy for compute.

### [cloud-decentralization] A mature Akash catalog will out-scale Amazon's 250 services
> "Amazon right now has about 250 services, but when Akash is fully mature and, you know, decentralized — where anybody can add any service that they want and there's a way to verify these — I don't see how we can['t] have a much more extensive catalog than any other cloud providers."
> — [00:19:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=1195s)

**Context:** After describing plans for decentralized managed services (e.g., MySQL run by low-latency providers in-datacenter); the bracketed correction reflects an apparent caption garble. Growth at the time: ~10 new active apps/day, ~150 deploys/day.

### [cloud-decentralization] Deplatforming is being weaponized by state actors
> "What's crazy is deplatforming is getting weaponized by state actors against legitimate companies... so people are using Akash as a disaster recovery mechanism."
> — [00:22:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=1323s)

**Context:** Second user cohort after the Parler takedown era — "the fact that there is a platform that has so much power is disturbing" ([00:21:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvQUKoM_1ME&t=1282s)) — with Akash as the censorship-resistant backup to primary AWS deployments.
