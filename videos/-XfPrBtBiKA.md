## Summary

An Encode Club Web3xAI Day panel on "the evolution to AGI" with Alex Page (Nillion, "the blind computer"), Mathieu (Linera founder, low-latency microchains with edge inference), a Ritual founding member (smart contracts over models), and Greg Osuri, who introduces Akash as "the world's first open source supercloud... probably the best place to get high-density GPUs, A100s and H100s, on demand."

The panel opens on Sam Altman: Greg delivers the sharpest take, claiming firsthand from his DC policy work that OpenAI actively lobbies to "make open-source AI illegal," has already won in the EU, and that most US legislators and staffers he meets agree open and decentralized AI is better for America. Ritual's rep piles on, calling Altman's China-risk narrative hypocritical since Chinese wrapper startups were proxying OpenAI's own API. On what web3 adds to the AGI pursuit, Greg argues compute is the foundation ("the spice must flow"), crypto incentives can unlock resources closed-source software can't reach, and decentralized/federated training (citing Google DeepMind's DiLoCo paper and Nous Research) is "rapidly coming" — when cracked, "we can crack AGI much sooner." Asked if that needs a token, he says "not yet," while crediting Bittensor's billions in annual emissions as a monetization mechanism for open-source AI. On AGI's shape, Ritual argues AGI will be a swarm of specialized models coordinating over a permissionless layer; Greg concurs from Akash usage data, repeating his claim that ChatGPT "forgot how to code" and betting on small task-specific models over ever-bigger ones. Timeline answers close the panel: eight years (apparently Greg), ten years (Linera), half a decade (Ritual).

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [open-source-ai] OpenAI is lobbying to make open-source AI illegal
> "I think he's just there to make money. I know this for sure because we do a lot of policy work in DC, and every time I go there, Sam Altman and the gang is trying to shut down open source and decentralized AI actively through lobbying, and they try to pass laws in the US to make open-source AI illegal, because they want to keep AI to themselves."
> — [00:07:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-XfPrBtBiKA&t=434s)

**Context:** Asked whether Sam Altman is pushing the world toward destruction; Greg adds "they're winning in EU — they did win — and we are fighting very hard so that open source and decentralized AI is going to win in the US."

### [open-source-ai] US legislators agree: open, decentralized AI is better for America
> "Most of the legislators and the staffers we speak to, all of them agree that AI being open and decentralized is better for America and the rest of the world, and unless there's concrete damage that's going to happen, they're not going to write rules."
> — [00:07:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-XfPrBtBiKA&t=477s)

**Context:** His read on US AI policy circa mid-2024; dismisses the "China will steal AI" argument as "the same argument as with guns."

### [decentralized-ai] Crack distributed training and we crack AGI much sooner
> "It begins with compute, right? Access to compute is fundamental — I mean, the spice must flow... We saw promising work by Google DeepMind, the paper called DiLoCo... heterogeneous training, federated training — a lot of work happening where you can leverage GPUs that are globally distributed and optimized for latency. So when we crack that, I think we can crack AGI much sooner."
> — [00:13:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-XfPrBtBiKA&t=825s)

**Context:** Answering what web3 adds to the pursuit of AGI; argues crypto incentivization "can go places where traditional, closed-source software cannot."

### [crypto-depin] Does open-source AGI need a token? "Not yet" — but Bittensor's emissions fund open AI
> "Does it need a token? I don't think so, not yet... Bittensor is doing interesting work — Bittensor emissions are like billions of dollars per year, right? So those incentives are going to amazing folks that are doing open source AI and giving them a monetization mechanism."
> — [00:15:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-XfPrBtBiKA&t=909s)

**Context:** Also name-checks Nous Research's decentralized training work; declines to pick a single winner because "too much work is happening."

### [open-source-ai] AGI comes from swarms of small specialized models, not bigger ones
> "I agree with the small models taking over, because we see the data in Akash as well... Have you noticed ChatGPT forgot how to code?... It's not really how big a model gets that can evolve to AGI. I think it's going to be the smaller models that are way better at coding or performing individual tasks."
> — [00:26:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-XfPrBtBiKA&t=1588s)

**Context:** Endorsing Ritual's swarm-of-specialized-models AGI thesis with Akash usage data; hedges that transformer/diffusion hybrids are evolving fast and calls GPT-4o "promising... good at overall things."
