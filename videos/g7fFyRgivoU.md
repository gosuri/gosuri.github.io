## Summary

Crypto Rich interviews Greg Osuri in a beginner-friendly deep dive on Akash Network (part 1 of 2). Greg frames the cloud as an oligopoly — four companies (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Alibaba) controlling over 80% of market share, with AWS alone valued around $500B — and argues the only route to breaking it is decentralization and open source. He explains that 85-90% of the world's data centers are unused or underutilized, and that Akash taps this idle supply via a reverse auction, delivering compute at roughly one-third the cost of Amazon. He uses his recurring "Airbnb for servers vs. Hilton" analogy: Akash offers exotic, otherwise-inaccessible options like data centers near cell towers or renewable energy sources. On the supply side he cites a ~95% conversion rate with data centers and Equinix as a major supplier; providers earn AKT, boosted by ~50% staking rewards and osmosis LP incentives, with a future take-rate model routing up to 20% of hosting fees to token holders. On demand, he identifies three cohorts: arbitrageurs/miners (who pushed Akash prices from 1/8th to 1/3rd of Amazon's), the "anti-deplatforming" cohort seeking censorship-resistant hosting (citing Parler's takedown as proof of centralized power), and innovators rebuilding open infrastructure (Cosmos validators/RPC nodes, unstoppable WordPress, decentralized docker registries) — pairing Akash compute with Skynet storage and Handshake DNS for a fully decentralized internet stack. He argues rebuilding infrastructure openly creates "the template for the future" and that Akash exists to keep power with the people.

### [gpu-economics] 85-90% of data centers are idle; Akash costs one-third of Amazon
> "It turns out 85 to 90 [percent] of data centers in the world are not used or underutilized... the result is the cost advantage you get on Akash is about one-third than Amazon. So if you're paying a dollar on Amazon, you pay 30 cents in Akash — that's just a pure free market at its best."
> — [00:05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g7fFyRgivoU&t=300s)

**Context:** Core supply-side thesis: massive idle capacity exists because data centers must provision for peak load (e.g., Intuit at ~95% utilization in tax season, 1-2% off-season).
