## Summary

Talk at deAI Summit 2024 (Token2049 side event) on "compute: past, present, and future." Greg recaps his background (25 years coding, AngelHack, Firebase's early backer, top-ranked Go developer, infrastructure code used by Nvidia and the Department of Defense) then traces compute history: mainframes (maximal centralization) → the PC ("compute came to us") → the internet as a network of networks on open protocols → cloud computing recentralizing everything, with hyperscalers now taking ~70% of IT budgets across four companies. His core arguments: centralization means hoarded resources (the Semafor story of a Columbia student who could only get A100s via Akash — and whose company was later acquired by Nvidia); a hidden "cloud tax" where roughly 50 cents of every dollar paid to online services flows to cloud providers (Asana 63%, Palantir ~40%, per an a16z S-1 analysis); fragility exemplified by the CrowdStrike outage (7,000 Delta flights); and Amazon's FTC antitrust scrutiny for stifling open source. The centerpiece warning: "digital feudalism," invoking Larry Ellison's remark about needing $100B and private nuclear reactors for AI training — digital lords who will ration who gets to do AI. Decentralized compute is framed as a return to the internet's founding principles, with compute as a public utility like air or water. He closes with Akash stats (Nvidia Brev integration, Nous Research, Prime Intellect, Venice AI; ~27,000 leases last quarter) and, in Q&A, claims Akash is ~80% cheaper than Amazon with H100s at $1.49/hour vs ~$5 on AWS.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] The cloud tax: half of every dollar goes to cloud providers
> "Today if you use an online service, 50% of every penny you pay goes to one [of] the cloud providers... Look at companies like Asana, where 63% of the fees actually goes to one of the cloud providers; companies like Palantir... about close to 40% goes to a cloud provider. All of us, no matter what sector we are in, half of every dollar we spend goes to an invisible layer — or the cloud tax, we call it."
> — [00:06:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJ-4uolkTeE&t=404s)

**Context:** Citing an a16z (captioned "hor[owitz]") study of public-company S-1 filings; a recurring quantified claim in his talks.

### [decentralized-ai] Without decentralization, we become peasants under digital lords
> "He talked about needing hundred billion dollars to do AI training and needing to build their own nuclear reactors to power AI data centers. These are the digital lords we are going to be bound to. If we need compute, we need to ask these people to do AI... we're all peasants, and this is the path we're going towards — a future where only wealthy corporations can control access to who gets to do AI. You will be rationed, you will be provisioned, and you will have [to] answer to new digital lords. That's the future we're go[ing] down if [we] don't decentralize sooner."
> — [00:10:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJ-4uolkTeE&t=622s)

**Context:** His "digital feudalism" thesis, prompted by Larry Ellison's interview the previous day about $100B AI training runs and private nuclear reactors.

### [cloud-decentralization] Compute should be a public utility like air and water
> "Imagine a world where computation is free and accessible as the air we breathe. We believe compute should be a public utility — like air, like water, like all the resources that we don't need to seek someone's permission to use."
> — [00:11:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJ-4uolkTeE&t=665s)

**Context:** Transition into the DePIN landscape slide; decentralized compute framed as "a return [to] the internet's founding principles."

### [gpu-economics] Akash is ~80% cheaper than Amazon; H100s at $1.49 vs $5
> "Apples to apples, Akash costs about 80% cheaper than Amazon... and the way you're able to get this is because Akash taps into underutilized resources, whereas [on] Amazon you're actually building new resources and new data centers. With GPUs, in fact, H100 on Akash cost[s] [$1.49] whereas the same resource would cost I think $5 on Amazon."
> — [00:15:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJ-4uolkTeE&t=929s)

**Context:** Q&A answer on why decentralized compute is cheaper; captions render the price as "$149," consistent with his usual ~$1.50/hour H100 claim.
