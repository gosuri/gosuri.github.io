## Summary

Akash Network's re-upload of Greg's October 2021 Yahoo Finance Live appearance (original on the Yahoo Finance channel: ESTCd9nb5CM). Prompted by the Trump SPAC/de-platforming news cycle and the Parler takedown, Greg gives a short history lesson: the internet was created as a decentralized network but ~80% of cloud computing is now controlled by four companies, with Amazon the biggest — and that concentration of power makes de-platforming "a threat to our civil liberties" regardless of politics. He explains Akash as a traditional-cloud-like experience sourced from a distributed network of cloud-grade data centers (naming Equinix, "the largest data center network in the world," as a big Akash provider), and argues open-source auditability makes Akash as secure or more secure than Amazon. Growth stats (dated): platform launched ~6 months prior, ~500 active applications, 30,000+ deployments, workloads doubling month over month until the prior month. On content moderation, he's a free-speech absolutist tempered by provider-level self-moderation: providers choose whom to serve, so Parler-like apps run only if some provider is willing, and universally repugnant content finds no host.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] 80% of cloud controlled by four companies
> "Internet was created as a decentralized network to fight against centralization... but over time it got increasingly centralized, and now the landscape looks like 80 or so of the cloud computing, which is a backbone for the internet, is controlled by four companies — Amazon being the biggest one."
> — [00:02:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9MCrtBKQYkw&t=130s)

**Context:** Answering how sites get de-platformed; his recurring centralization thesis.

### [other] De-platforming is a threat to civil liberties
> "We're seeing increasingly de-platforming becoming a threat to our civil liberties... it's not so much about what side of the political spectrum you fall under, but the fact that there's a single company or a single individual or entity that has so much power on civil liberties is what's concerning."
> — [00:02:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9MCrtBKQYkw&t=173s)

**Context:** Referencing the Parler/AWS takedown; core argument for censorship-resistant infrastructure.

### [cloud-decentralization] Open source makes Akash as secure as or more secure than AWS
> "It's probably more secure than your traditional Amazon because Akash being open source, all our source code is actually open for a public audit, so it offers similar levels if not better levels of security guarantees Amazon's or the big cloud offers."
> — [00:04:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9MCrtBKQYkw&t=261s)

**Context:** Rebutting host's security concern about using Akash over AWS.

### [cloud-decentralization] Early growth: 500 apps, 30k deployments, doubling monthly
> "We launched the platform about six months ago and so far we have about 500 applications live... over 30,000 deployments that happened over the last six months... until last month we were actually doubling in terms of workloads, applications coming online to Akash network."
> — [00:04:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9MCrtBKQYkw&t=261s)

**Context:** Quantified adoption snapshot ~6 months after mainnet marketplace launch (mid-2021 baseline).

### [cloud-decentralization] Provider self-moderation instead of central takedowns
> "The way Akash network operates is self-moderation. That means providers on the Akash network choose who they want to serve... as long as there's a provider that's willing to serve Parler, Parler can run on Akash. But if there are no providers... that website won't be served on Akash."
> — [00:06:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9MCrtBKQYkw&t=390s)

**Context:** His standard answer to "what if something really bad runs on Akash and can't be taken down."
