## Summary

A follow-up podcast with host Jesse ($1 To $1 Million / "Never Die" podcast) recorded on Overclock Labs' 9th anniversary. Greg gives his standard framing of Akash as "the world's first supercloud" — an open-source, permissionless aggregation layer over underutilized data-center compute — and spends the first half dismantling competitor comparisons. He calls io.net's founder Shadid Ahmed "a complete fraud" over the million-GPU claims and the assertion of having solved federated learning on heterogeneous GPUs ("mark my words, no one solved" it), and describes Render as "not fraud" but closed-source, not permissionless, hosted on Google, and unused for AI. He draws a two-ecosystem map of decentralized AI: closed/permissioned (Render, io.net, Aethir) vs. open/permissionless (Gensyn, Akash, Prime Intellect, Ritual), and cites real workloads on Akash (Venice AI, Nous Research, Prime Intellect, Flock.io, Brev.dev).

The second half is prediction-dense: compute as an abstraction stack ending in energy and sand; supply-chain shortage easing in 3-5 years; Gartner's forecast of AI compute reaching 5% of global GDP by 2030 (which he endorses); agreement with Sam Altman on universal basic compute; "separation of compute from state" as the Bitcoin analogy for intelligence; a warning that unchecked AI concentration produces a "feudalistic society"; the need to make home PCs, PlayStations/Xboxes, and Mac Studios one-click cloud-capable network participants; and a teaser that he is touring power facilities and studying a decentralized power market (especially Texas solar co-located with data centers) plus quantum ML research with an MIT researcher. Dated markers: Blackwell/B100 launch chatter, Apple's OpenAI integration announcement, io.net sybil-attack controversy, Venice AI launch.
