## Summary

Decentralized cloud panel at DCENTRAL Austin 2022, moderated by Seth "MineYour.Biz" Estrada, with Greg alongside Olly (Cudos), Patrick (Jackal, decentralized storage) and Lex (Passage, virtual experiences). Greg introduces Akash as "the world's first open market" for cloud-grade compute, citing ~85% of global data center capacity unused and costs ~95% below traditional cloud — he deployed an Osmosis validator "for seven bucks a month" the night before, pitching AKT as a bear-market friend for node operators. On scale vs. AWS, he distinguishes hyper-redundant on-chain compute from off-chain scheduling and notes Amazon's redundancy stops at the data center. He defends Akash's developer-first strategy — clean APIs, letting community clients (Akashlytics, Praetor, a Netlify-style deployer) make UX tradeoffs rather than the core team. On "web 2.5" compromises, he reframes them as "clever design": AuthZ decoupling payer from user enables legal credit-card onboarding, and he predicts web3 goes mainstream in about two years. On attack resistance he argues proof-of-stake systems are "communal organisms" — hostile token accumulation raises price and security, forks remain an exit, and the 21-day unbonding period is a circuit breaker — recounting how he, an outsider with "zero clout," passed a Terra governance proposal via Twitter to re-enable IBC. A panelist (likely Lex) notes the ETH 2.0 transition will free GPU capacity for decentralized clouds. Same panel also uploaded by the Dcentral Con channel (yfBYYjtzsX0).

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] 85% of data center capacity unused; Akash 95% cheaper
> "The idea behind [Akash] is to unlock unused capacity — about 85 or so [percent] of global data center capacity is unused. So by enabling those providers to offer their compute in an open, competitive way, we can solve efficiency problems, [and] the outcome is the cost — a cost about 95 percent lower than your traditional cloud. Like last night I was able to deploy an Osmosis validator for seven bucks a month."
> — [00:02:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EeghIeZppQI&t=172s)

**Context:** His panel introduction; adds "when the bear market hits and profitability for node operations is in question, AKT is your best friend." Quote spans into the [00:03:36] block.

### [cloud-decentralization] Amazon's redundancy ends at the data center
> "On-chain computing is hyper redundant — that's why it's expensive, that also is why it's secure. Off-chain compute, the redundancy depends on the scheduling algorithm. Amazon is not redundant... if an Amazon data center goes down, it goes down — it's redundant within the scope of the data center... the challenge is to design something that allows for cross-site, or maximum possible, redundancy while allowing for performance."
> — [00:05:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EeghIeZppQI&t=301s)

**Context:** Answering why users shouldn't just deploy on AWS; he adds he deployed a validator "within five minutes from 3,000 feet using clicks — we're getting there very very quickly" [00:05:44].

### [other] Web3 goes mainstream in about two years
> "It's not like compromises, but just clever design that evolves over time, and I think very, very soon we're going to have these systems come into play and people will figure this out, and these patterns will become the global web3 onboarding patterns — and I would give it another two years by the time web three goes mainstream."
> — [00:18:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EeghIeZppQI&t=1113s)

**Context:** On "web 2.5" onboarding: Akash's AuthZ feature decouples payer from user so a custodied, credit-card-funded wallet can legally delegate compute spend — his example of bridging legacy infrastructure (email, credit cards) rather than ignoring it.

### [crypto-depin] Proof-of-stake networks are communal organisms that resist takeover
> "What's going to prevent a bad actor, let's say Amazon, to buy up all the tokens and take over Akash Network? ... proof of stake systems are inherently communal organisms... a bad actor trying to buy up all the tokens — that's going to increase the price of the token, thereby further strengthening security... people can fork it and start a new one... the community will always come through before an attack can happen, and there are also preventive circuit breakers — we have like a 21-day unbonding period for that very reason."
> — [00:24:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EeghIeZppQI&t=1497s)

**Context:** Prompted by a question on adversarial attacks; illustrated with his Terra story — an outsider with "very little voting power, very little clout" passing a proposal via Twitter to re-enable IBC [00:26:22].
