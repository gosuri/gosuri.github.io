## Summary

Community livestream during Week 2 of the Akashian Challenge Phase 3 testnet, hosted by marketing manager Michael with Greg Osuri and CTO Adam Bozanich. Greg recaps testnet metrics: ~3,400-3,500 active leases (up from ~800 in week 1), ~440-470 unique lease owners, 100 active validators plus 48 on standby, and ~45 unique applications in the "Awesome Akash" repo — including Uniswap, SushiSwap, and PancakeSwap frontends, block explorers, WordPress/Ghost blogs, and games like Doom and Pac-Man. He then performs his first-ever live demo: deploying a Serum DEX UI end-to-end from the Akash CLI (SDL file, deployment transaction, lease status, service logs, lease close). Greg argues Akash's core value combination is usability plus censorship resistance, using ThorChain's interest in user-hosted DEX UIs as an example of non-custodial hosting shifting regulatory liability from creator to user. Q&A covers the Supermini home server (a provider device meant for home usage, limited to 300 US-only units), whether Akash could host Solana ("at least 200 Solanas" of capacity), the exclusive Equinix Metal partnership for Mainnet 2 ("the underlying cloud of the cloud"), content moderation (no governance-based takedowns since that would introduce censorship), comparisons with iExec and SKALE, and Mainnet 2 timing — Greg concedes the December 2020 target will slip ("2020 happened") with a date to be announced. Also covered: BitMart listing giving US residents access to AKT, AKT's all-time high of $0.875, a meme contest, merch raffles, and a shoutout to FTX head of engineering Nishad Singh's tweet thread about Akash being potentially transformative for the cloud.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Users will self-deploy DEX UIs signed by web wallets
> "Right now it's command line, but soon enough... you can see a world where their web interfaces are much more simple interfaces, ideally signed by web wallets, and the users themselves can deploy any DEX UI. So that's really where we're going."
> — [00:43:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TEqq1vFPus&t=2628s)

**Context:** Discussing ThorChain wanting users to host their own DEX UIs on Akash; Greg frames non-custodial hosting as shifting regulatory liability from creator to user.

### [cloud-decentralization] Usability plus censorship resistance is the winning combo
> "From one hand you have this incredible usability gains, from the other hand you have this amazing censorship resistance in a permissionless way... all this great adoption that we had is just a testament to these two incredible important features that Akash can provide."
> — [00:45:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TEqq1vFPus&t=2754s)

**Context:** After analogizing Uniswap's few-click swaps vs. a centralized exchange's 20-step flow, Greg claims Akash extends that same usability leap to cloud deployment vs. AWS.

### [local-compute] Supermini is a home provider device with home-device constraints
> "Super mini is a home device, so... the expectation is that you're not going to have a consistent network capacity, so the kind of workers you can run on super mini have to take into account these limitations of a home device versus a data center based device."
> — [00:48:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TEqq1vFPus&t=2881s)

**Context:** Answering whether the Supermini (an experimental 300-unit, US-only home server that acts as an Akash provider) can validate other chains; early articulation of home hardware supplying the network.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash could host 200 Solanas; supply is not the problem
> "100%. I mean, I think we can cover at least 200 Solanas with the current capacity that we have... we don't have a supply problem."
> — [00:49:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TEqq1vFPus&t=2967s)

**Context:** Asked whether rented Akash capacity would suffice in 2021-22 to cover a significant part of Solana's compute needs; Greg says demand, not supply, is the constraint.

### [cloud-decentralization] At maturity Akash will have thousands, if not millions, of providers
> "How do you detect this illegal content? How do you remove it? Especially considering if you're Amazon you probably have one company, but in Akash you have thousands if not millions of providers when we reach maturity."
> — [00:52:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TEqq1vFPus&t=3138s)

**Context:** Answering how Akash handles illegal content; the scale forecast of "millions of providers" is the notable quantified piece.

### [cloud-decentralization] Governance-based content takedowns would mean censorship
> "The moment you say we can introduce a governance process, that means... you're introducing censorship... we don't have the tools and technologies to even understand what the content is."
> — [00:53:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TEqq1vFPus&t=3180s)

**Context:** Greg's contrarian stance that Akash will not adopt on-chain governance for content removal, shifting moderation liability to individual providers instead.
