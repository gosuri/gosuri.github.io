## Summary

Akash Weekly Twitter Spaces hosted by Nadia Bajuelo with Greg's update segment, guest Alani Kuye (technical program manager, Overclock Labs) on a new tenant/provider referral program, and an AMA. Greg announces Mainnet 4 targeted for the first week of November 2022: IP leases (custom ports enabling Solana, Bitcoin, Handshake workloads) and interchain accounts (ICA), which he presents as removing "the human being in the middle" — a DAO or smart contract on Juno owning Akash deployments directly, plus end-to-end on-chain supply-chain verifiability from Gitopia/Radicle code to deployment. He notes 50% of hosted Ethereum nodes run on AWS, previews the custodial "Overclock Console" to onboard web2 developers, and is bullish on the upcoming GPU feature: people are buying GPUs to host at home and should lease them out when idle, and "the GPU market is much bigger than what we realized" (citing Stanford research on distributed GPU clusters). Other threads: no-token-incentives-before-product-market-fit as deliberate strategy, Spheron workshops onboarding African developers without credit cards, DoD experimenting with Akash/Secret for sovereign internal systems, and bear-market resilience. In the AMA he predicts web3 goes mainstream by 2027 (faster than Ernst & Young's 2030 cloud-trajectory estimate), argues NFTs (digitized rights/ownership) will be bigger than DeFi, and calls on crypto to move beyond finance toward "permissionless coordination at planetary scale." Alani's referral program (one wallet, one code, rewards for sustained deployments/providers) was slated to launch within about ten days.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] The only viable open source cloud
> "We're bringing that to the market with the first sort of open source cloud to do that — in fact, we are the only open source cloud that's viable, so I guess we'll be the first in anything we do."
> — [00:02:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_NZhdhw5f4s&t=179s)

**Context:** On shipping IP leases in a decentralized way for Mainnet 4.

### [crypto-depin] Smart contracts will own deployments — no humans in the middle
> "With interchain accounts, a DAO on Juno can own a deployment directly on Akash, and now you don't need a human being in the middle... as long as there's a human being, that human being becomes a weak link in the entire decentralization stack."
> — [00:04:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_NZhdhw5f4s&t=266s)

**Context:** Previewing Mainnet 4's interchain accounts; later ([00:49:14]) he extends this to full on-chain supply-chain verifiability — "nothing like this has ever happened before in crypto."

### [cloud-decentralization] Half of Ethereum's hosted nodes run on AWS
> "The base layer is where you run the actual nodes... if you're Ethereum, you're essentially running on AWS — 50% of hosted nodes on Ethereum runs on AWS... so that layer itself is another level of censorship."
> — [00:05:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_NZhdhw5f4s&t=351s)

**Context:** His three-layer censorship model of the web3 stack (access point / consensus layer / base layer); Akash targets the front-end and base layers.

### [local-compute] Buy a GPU, host it at home, lease it out when idle
> "People are realizing it's actually way cheaper to buy a GPU and host it at home, but then you're not using those GPUs most of the time, so it's even better when you can actually lease them out to recover some of your investments. So Akash GPU [is something] I'm extremely bullish on... as we get closer to releasing GPUs... we're realizing the GPU market is much bigger than what we realized."
> — [00:12:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_NZhdhw5f4s&t=745s)

**Context:** GPU support named the next big feature after IP leases; he cites unnamed large companies eager to use it for machine learning and Stanford research on using distributed GPU clusters. An early statement of the home-GPUs-earning-income thesis.

### [other] Web3 goes mainstream by 2027
> "Mainstream definition means 50% of 14,000 companies all have [a] web3 strategy — I would consider that mainstream. I think we're going to see that in five years. I mean, by 2027 at least... Ernst & Young... they think it's 2030, just by following the trajectory of cloud... but I believe as we evolve in tech stacks they tend to get faster in terms of adoption, and I believe 2027 will be the date."
> — [00:40:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_NZhdhw5f4s&t=2430s)

**Context:** AMA question on mainstream web3 adoption; he adds that regulation arriving (however harsh) gives large companies the clarity they need, and notes DoD experimentation with Akash and Secret for sovereign internal systems.

### [other] NFTs — digitized ownership — will be bigger than DeFi
> "Digitalization of ownership, I think, is going to be a lot bigger than DeFi. I think NFTs are going to be a lot bigger than DeFi... DeFi is essentially taking the old, oligopolized model of how to deal with finance and modernizing that, but NFTs unlock whole new levels of engagement with what has been traditionally very hidden and very inaccessible."
> — [00:44:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_NZhdhw5f4s&t=2654s)

**Context:** Prompted by Starbucks' NFT rewards program; he frames NFTs as composable, portable, easily custodied digital rights.

### [other] Crypto's real gift is planetary-scale coordination, not finance
> "What crypto allows us to do is coordination — permissionless coordination at planetary scale... I think it's very, very important for us to shift our focus away from finance and think about what we can do beyond finance."
> — [00:51:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_NZhdhw5f4s&t=3089s)

**Context:** Closing AMA exchange agreeing DeFi has become boring; he floats life extension as the kind of problem planetary coordination plus decentralized infrastructure could tackle. (Quote begins at the end of the [00:50:40] block.)
