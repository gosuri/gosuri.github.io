## Summary

Genzio interview with Greg at Permissionless in Salt Lake City. He recounts Akash's origins: cloud-native open-source work (early Kubernetes, Docker, Terraform), 2016 Ethereum experiments, and the 2017 "decentralized supercloud" paper premised on ~75% (conservatively; realistically ~90%) of active data center capacity being underutilized. He explains "growing up with open source" — rural India in the '90s, where Windows licenses in US dollars were unaffordable, making Linux his only path to computing — and notes 97% of web servers now run Linux. He details the difficulty of being first in decentralized compute: open-sourcing the first line of code before product-market fit, launching with no control over servers or validators (upgrades require governance votes; he once sent people to validators' houses in Europe to wake them for votes), and raising only $1.8M to launch the chain because 2017's smart-contract narrative frowned on decentralized cloud. He argues humanity's post-biological evolution rests on open access to information, credits Akash's code with enabling today's DePIN ecosystem (used by Nvidia, Microsoft, DoD), and sharply rejects closed-source "decentralized" DePIN projects — citing CrowdStrike and 23andMe's DNA database sale as closed-system failure modes: "the spectrum begins with the open source code base and ends with energy sourcing." Upcoming: credit-card payments without logins (avoiding user-data liability so "anything could happen to Overclock Labs and Akash will run as designed"), plus an end-of-year product cutting the ~6-hour, ~$30/hr cost of running Llama 3.1 405B to attract web2 developers.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [open-source-ai] Sufficiently successful software must be open source — the cloud went the other way
> "Any sufficiently successful software has to be open source — be it Linux, be it Android, anything that you would deem will have impact on a globe. Internet is open source. But we saw a direction with the cloud that was opposite: it was getting increasingly closed, increasingly oligopolized by few players, and that was a future we didn't want to accept."
> — [00:00:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pIX9lYr57fE&t=48s)

**Context:** Origin of Akash; years of work with industry and academia "to decouple control from resources."

### [crypto-depin] Decentralization's spectrum begins with open source and ends with energy sourcing
> "That's why you want open source as a foundation for decentralization, and I will reject any notion that a closed system is decentralized — it's just not a reality. And it's really sad what's happening [in] DePIN today, that there are a lot of these networks just popping out of nowhere with fully closed source code bases, and the justification is 'decentralization is a spectrum.' Yes, decentralization is a spectrum, but the spectrum begins with the open source code base and ends with energy sourcing."
> — [00:15:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pIX9lYr57fE&t=912s)

**Context:** After citing CrowdStrike's bad-code push and 23andMe's for-sale DNA database as failure modes of closed systems users don't control.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash must outlive its company — systems that run as designed
> "The whole point of building open source decentralized systems is to remove [single] centers of liability. Anything could happen [to] Overclock Labs, and Akash will run as designed — that's the future we want. So we are very reluctant to collect user information."
> — [00:18:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pIX9lYr57fE&t=1086s)

**Context:** Explaining why upcoming credit-card payments are designed without logins or stored user data.

### [gpu-economics] 75-90% of data center capacity is underutilized (2017 supercloud paper)
> "After several experiments on Ethereum in 2016, we were convinced that this is possible, so we wrote a paper and published that in 2017 called a decentralized supercloud, and we proposed that there is enormous amounts of underutilized capacity around the world — on an average about 75% of data center capacity that's active is underutilized, and that's a conservative estimate; in the reality [it's] somewhere like 90%."
> — [00:02:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pIX9lYr57fE&t=135s)

**Context:** The founding thesis, dated to the 2017 whitepaper.

### [open-source-ai] Open access to information drives human evolution
> "We humans physically stopped evolving about 10,000 years ago — our brains did not get bigger — but we got extremely smart... because we can coordinate and we can share knowledge... the most important thing for the evolution is open access to information. The more open and more frictionless there is a way to access information, the faster we can [evolve]... that's why I think open source is not just beneficial to one single entity like Akash but beneficial to the whole ecosystem."
> — [00:10:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pIX9lYr57fE&t=615s)

**Context:** His philosophical case for open source; he adds the DePIN/decentralized compute ecosystem exists because of Akash's open code.

### [other] LLM costs are limiting adoption — new product cuts time and cost
> "The challenge today is for LLM developers to deploy an LLM and use an LLM that is large, like a Llama 3.1 405B, takes about 6 hours. It's extremely expensive... it costs about $30 per hour... so we have a new product that's going to significantly reduce the cost and the time it takes to run LLMs, so that way you'll be able to attract your traditional web2 developers, and Akash will be one of the most attractive products not only in web3 but also web2."
> — [00:16:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pIX9lYr57fE&t=999s)

**Context:** Teasing an end-of-2024 launch, preceded by login-free credit card payments "next week."
