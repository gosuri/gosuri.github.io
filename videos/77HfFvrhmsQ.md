## Summary

X Spaces hosted by Nodexo with Greg and Hansel Melo ("AI Zorro") of Neural Internet, marking the Akash x Bittensor Compute Subnet 27 partnership — miners rent cheap Akash H100s and plug them into Subnet 27 for TAO rewards. Greg recaps origins (first Akash code ~10 years prior, 2017 paper) and Akash's position as "the premium marketplace for GPUs and the only place to get H100s on demand." His centerpiece answer on where decentralized compute is headed is his personal-AI ownership thesis: AI gets personal, sustainable AI means hosting your own, users should own the AI by owning the compute that runs it (electricity-grid analogy), and idle home chips should join the grid for economic reward. He riffs that GPUs are "the new spice" — the most valuable commodity before oil, now back. On adoption, he argues products win via quantifiable value ("the carrot is the GPUs, you stay for the rest"), permissionless/non-custodial rails protect platform builders from Amazon-style predation, and open systems "go far" versus vertically integrated stacks that merely go fast (his 95%-built philosophy: leave 5% for smart contributors). He details AKT 2.0 (multi-currency settlement, provider incentives, ~$20M community pool, 480 contributors, everything built in public). Notable predictions: the 2024 US election as a "deepfake election" with chaos before provenance solutions mature, and a dismissal of the Fetch/SingularityNET/Ocean ASI token merger ("there has not been a successful token merger") — open systems don't need mergers, and Bittensor is the Unix-like "orchestration layer for AI" that ASI is really competing with.

### [local-ai] The future: people that use AI own the AI
> "As AI gets more prominent in our daily lives, it's no question that AI is going to get personal... to have a sustainable AI you will have to host your own AI. I think the future is going to be: people that use AI own the AI, and the way you own the AI is to own the computer that runs the AI — just like electricity, if you have a sustainable way of producing electricity instead of depending on the grid, you have a lot more control."
> — [00:07:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=77HfFvrhmsQ&t=439s)

**Context:** Asked where decentralized compute is going; quote begins in the [00:06:35] block. He grounds it in privacy-critical AI use and the broken GPU demand/supply curve.

### [local-compute] Own the chips; idle home compute should join the grid
> "Considering AI is going to be the critical aspect of our lives every day, you have to own the chips. And when you own the chips, you're not using them most of the time, and if there's a mechanism for you to share your underutilized time in exchange for some economic reward, you can essentially create a P2P network of folks that own chips and folks that want to use chips... if there's a device in the home that's serving some desire of yours and there's underutilized compute, that should be part of the grid — and ultimately we as users of AI should own the AI. That's the real vision behind decentralized compute."
> — [00:08:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=77HfFvrhmsQ&t=484s)

**Context:** Direct continuation of the personal-AI answer; his clearest statement of the home-compute-as-grid thesis in this period. Quote spans into the [00:08:48] block.

### [gpu-economics] GPUs are the new spice
