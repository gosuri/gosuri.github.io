## Summary

Multi-guest, documentary-style episode from host Jeff Wilser about the physical footprint of the AI boom: 71% of Americans oppose new data centers near them, towns are passing bans, and hyperscalers are spending $600-700B a year on AI infrastructure they increasingly can't power. Four voices are interwoven: Andrew Chin (University of Chicago / Argonne), who predicts data centers hit at least 15% of US grid by 2030 and 50% by 2040; Lee Rainie (ex-Pew, Elon University) on public opinion; Jonathan Koomey (of "Koomey's law"), who argues the doom forecasts repeat the debunked 1990s "internet will use half our electricity" panic and that project queues are overcounted 3-5x; and Greg Osuri, framed as "the man who saw this coming."

Greg's segments bookend the episode. Up front he revisits his earlier forecast (415 TWh of data-center usage in 2024 growing to ~980 TWh by 2030) and says he *underpredicted*: Stargate, CoreWeave and other announced projects are stalled on local pushback over energy — "a crisis beyond historical proportions." His most striking stat: only ~2.4 million people actually use AI agents today, and that sliver is already causing the crunch; agentic coding alone could 10x it. In the back half he presents Akash as a decentralized supercloud pulling compute from homes and small buildings, argues latency is "a symptom, not a cause" and no longer blocks distributed training (citing Pluralis), points to Razer running an image-generation campaign on home nodes, and says home 5090 rigs on solar can earn sizable income at near-zero marginal cost. He announces Akash crossed 10 billion tokens/day. Note: Whisper renders his name "Greg Asori/Asuri"; the 50%-by-2040 grid prediction belongs to Andrew Chin, not Greg.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [energy] "We underpredicted" the AI energy crunch
> "To remind folks, 2024, I think overall data center usage was around 415 terawatt hours. And that was expected to grow to around 980 terawatt hours by 2030. It's about two and a half times growth."
> — [00:02:23](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2440361/episodes/19568316-ai-data-centers-are-eating-the-grid-is-there-another-way)

**Context:** Host asks him to score the energy prediction he made on the show a year earlier. He immediately follows with "I think we underpredicted that" [00:03:16].

### [energy] Announced data-center projects are stalling — "a crisis beyond historical proportions"
> "The reality today is most data center projects that were announced by OpenAI, as part of the Stargate program, by CoreWeave, several other players that announced building large data center projects are all stalled because of local community pushback, primarily because of energy. So we're in a crisis beyond historical proportions."
> — [00:03:16](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2440361/episodes/19568316-ai-data-centers-are-eating-the-grid-is-there-another-way)

**Context:** His update on the state of the buildout in mid-2026; frames community/energy pushback, not chips, as the binding constraint.

### [ai-agents] 2.4 million agent users are causing the crisis — what happens at 10x?
> "That 2.4 million is causing the current crisis of historic magnitude. What do you think happens when that number grows by order of magnitude? Just 24 million. And that's not even way off because 24 million is a drop in the bucket. Considering there are about 80 million people that know how to code... About 40 million professional developers. We're talking about even half the amount of professional developers using agentic coding. Alone, we'll hit that at 24 million."
> — [00:05:27](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2440361/episodes/19568316-ai-data-centers-are-eating-the-grid-is-there-another-way)

**Context:** He walks the host from ~1 billion AI users down to only 2.4 million actual agent users [00:04:42], then argues demand grows exponentially because "humans are not very good at imagining exponents."

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash: a supercloud built from places clouds don't go
> "Akash is a decentralized super cloud. What that means is it is a cloud network where the compute comes from places that normally cloud providers don't offer. Places like homes, buildings, small buildings. Pretty much anywhere there's compute can be contributed to Akash network for the greater good."
> — [00:37:18](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2440361/episodes/19568316-ai-data-centers-are-eating-the-grid-is-there-another-way)

**Context:** Host reintroduces him as the person who made "the decentralized bet long before it was fashionable"; Greg says constrained compute is driving more usage to Akash.

### [local-compute] Homes are the least understood compute resource; latency is "a symptom, not a cause"
> "Initially, Akash was mostly data center compute, but now we're going into homes with usable compute as well. Homes being one of the best resources, one of the least understood resources... Homes are extremely efficient when it comes to energy usage. Latency is a symptom, not a cause... It's not a critical component for training as well as inference."
> — [00:38:12](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2440361/episodes/19568316-ai-data-centers-are-eating-the-grid-is-there-another-way)

**Context:** His core contrarian claim against the co-location advantage of hyperscale data centers: latency constraints are an artifact of algorithm design, not physics.

### [decentralized-ai] Training now works on distributed, heterogeneous networks
> "Today, training workloads are evolving to a point that can work on distributed networks, heterogeneous networks, highly fault tolerant. We're seeing companies like Pluralis, for example, training fairly large or usable models on fully decentralized networks."
> — [00:39:02](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2440361/episodes/19568316-ai-data-centers-are-eating-the-grid-is-there-another-way)

**Context:** Adds that algorithms "can communicate less frequently in bigger batches," and that Razer (the gaming hardware company) ran a successful April 1st image-generation campaign on Akash home nodes — proof home nodes work at scale for a publicly traded company [00:39:02].

> "You can simply put a bunch of 5090s, put it on Akash right now and actually earn a sizable income. Your biggest variable will be bandwidth and your energy costs. That's what you want to watch out for. Now, if you have access to, let's say, a solar or renewable, your marginal cost is zero. That's an incredible opportunity."
> — [00:39:59](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2440361/episodes/19568316-ai-data-centers-are-eating-the-grid-is-there-another-way)

**Context:** Also notes Bitcoin miners are pivoting their setups to AI compute. (Whisper renders "put it on Akash" as "put it on a cash" and slightly garbles the surrounding sentence.)
