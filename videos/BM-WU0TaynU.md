## Summary

The final Akash Weekly Twitter Spaces of 2022, hosted by Nadia Bajuelo, recorded amid the post-FTX/BlockFi fallout. Greg's update segment argues a "war" has surfaced between privacy advocates and surveillance advocates: ConsenSys's new policy of logging IPs and transactions shows centralized entities are dangerous not from evil intent but because their databases inevitably leak — whereas protocols like Akash structurally cannot collect IPs or censor. He plugs the upcoming web console (in insider beta), Cloudmos alerts, the community Terraform provider (with HashiCorp interest), and cites Hetzner's outright web3 ban as proof that "Akash can never ban you" — predicting censorship-resistance-driven migration as the trend.

Guest Amy James (co-executive director, Web3 Working Group; Open Index Protocol / "decentralized Library of Alexandria") explains the group's mission: educating the public and regulators that web3 infrastructure (Akash, Arweave, Graph, Livepeer, Filecoin) is plumbing, not financial services, and must not be regulated like DeFi; she invokes the UK's "red flag laws" that set back its auto industry 30 years and notes declining US developer activity. Greg adds a strikingly early (ChatGPT-launch-week) warning that closed-source AI ownership and "surveillance capitalism when machines take over" make open, people-owned AI critical.

The AMA covers validator risk on centralized clouds ("Akash never went down"), FTX's fallout pushing a decoupling of infrastructure from finance narratives, Overclock's planned 2023 shift to community-driven, chain-financed development, VPN/Tailscale-style networking on the roadmap, and closes with his web3 outlook (hyperscalers on Akash in 2-3 years, web3 invisible in 5) and a macro call that November 2022 was the market bottom with a Fed pivot and recovery by mid-2023.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] A war between privacy and surveillance; centralized databases always leak
> "There is a war that's more apparent now between the advocates of privacy and the advocates of surveillance... The fact that there is a database somewhere sitting that has your IP addresses and your transaction history is fundamentally dangerous, because this database is never secure... Last thing I want is my information with my public IP address, with all the transaction history associated, by name... on the dark web — and we know that's going to happen."
> — [00:02:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BM-WU0TaynU&t=170s)

**Context:** Reacting to ConsenSys's privacy policy allowing IP/transaction logging; his argument that protocols must be built so they "cannot collect information" rather than promise not to.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash can never ban you — and that migration is the trend
> "One thing about Akash is Akash can never ban you. There's nothing that I can do or anybody can do to ban you, and that is very powerful construct... I think that's going to be the trend moving forward."
> — [00:11:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BM-WU0TaynU&t=691s)

**Context:** Prompted by Hetzner's blanket ban on web3 workloads and an NFT project (Moonwise) migrating to Akash; companies are "not evil but inherently risky" because decisions hinge on a few executives.

### [open-source-ai] AI must be owned by people, not companies
> "It's very important that we get this open source. If you deem AI as one of the most revolutionary technologies that's going to benefit humanity, it's very important that we get it right — it's very important that people own it and not owned by companies... Think about surveillance capitalism now, from Facebook and Google, to the world — think about surveillance capitalism when machines take over."
> — [00:38:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BM-WU0TaynU&t=2293s)

**Context:** Discussing the rise of generative AI art (recorded the week ChatGPT launched); he notes most AI is closed source because "incentives are misaligned," an early statement of his open-source-AI thesis.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash has never gone down
> "Guess what — Akash never went down, hasn't gone down. Only time it was taken down was by its validators during upgrade processes, and that's expected... when you upgrade it doesn't take the infrastructure down."
> — [00:47:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BM-WU0TaynU&t=2852s)

**Context:** Answering why validators should leave centralized clouds; he cites ~80 Amazon incidents in a year and frames the choice as "hyper-risky censorship at 80% higher cost" versus Akash.

### [crypto-depin] Development should be financed by the chain, not the company
> "Instead of Overclock Labs financing the development of Akash Network, I think it should be the chain and the token holders that finance the development and evolution of the network. That's how we truly build a decentralized network that's not reliant on a single company."
> — [00:53:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BM-WU0TaynU&t=3193s)

**Context:** Post-FTX transparency question; he announces Overclock's 2023 pivot to community-first roadmaps and says they'll "write a playbook" for tokenized open-source development.

### [cloud-decentralization] In 2-3 years, Amazon, Google, and Microsoft using Akash
> "If you imagine Akash in two years or three years, I would like to see an Amazon, Google, Microsoft using Akash. I mean, the whole point of web3 is to be inclusive... web3 allows competitors to collaborate... Imagine a Google offering GPUs on Akash — that way they can keep their customer, at the same time offer a competitive GPU pricing. It's good for Google, it's good for Amazon. Web3 is good for business."
> — [01:05:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BM-WU0TaynU&t=3924s)

**Context:** Asked how web3 evolves in 3-5 years; notes hyperscalers already stood up web3 teams. Also an early flag of GPU spot markets as Akash's unique value ("Akash offers a global spot market — nobody in web2 offers a global open spot market").

### [cloud-decentralization] In five years we won't be talking about web3 at all
> "I think in five years we shouldn't be talking about web3, because web3 will be so integral to our daily lives that we won't be having this discussion — it'll be just something else."
> — [01:09:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BM-WU0TaynU&t=4144s)

**Context:** His consolidation thesis: protocols with "clean, fundamental, simple value" (Arweave's permanent storage, Akash's spot market) win and fade into invisible infrastructure; most NFTs won't survive.

### [other] November 2022 was the bottom; Fed pivots and recovery by mid-2023
> "Overall I think November — we hit the bottom. We're starting to see some recovery... there's indication inflation actually reducing, so that could actually influence the Fed to pivot — a pivot means that they would start reducing interest rates sometime next year. So this is the worst part of the bear market, I think."
> — [01:11:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BM-WU0TaynU&t=4317s)

**Context:** Closing thoughts for the year; he adds at [01:14:52] "mid next year we're going to start seeing a pivot and this will be history," urging builders with conviction to stay and "tourists" to leave quietly.
