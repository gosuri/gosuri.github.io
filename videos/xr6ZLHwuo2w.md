## Summary

TOKEN2049 Singapore panel moderated by Auggie of CMT Digital, with Greg Osuri (Akash), Frank Mong (Helium COO), Mike Horton (GEODNET), and Gaurav (io.net CEO). Greg introduces Akash as fully open source, on-chain, non-custodial and permissionless, with compute ~80% cheaper than centralized cloud and documented two-minute AI deployments. His signature contribution is a framework for when DePIN works: "decentralize what is abundant and idle, centralize what is scarce and indivisible" (GPUs, rooftop solar, Wi-Fi routers vs. nuclear plants, hydro dams, submarine cables), with tokens as "the protocol layer that reduces entropy." Asked why compute centralizes, he argues inference is actually better at the edge, then explains training's three constraints (heavy communication, no fault tolerance, serialization requiring homogeneous GPUs) — and predicts those are being overcome by low-communication/asynchronous algorithms just as the energy wall hits: AI energy demand doubling every two years, needing "a nuclear reactor by 2030 to train the state-of-the-art AI" (he testified to Congress on this in May 2025). He predicts a truly decentralized-trained model within 6-12 months. On sustainability, he contrasts Akash's no-token-bootstrap approach: >70% utilization vs. AWS ~40% and Filecoin ~0.5%, a coming "utilization guarantee" incentive design, unsolved continuous non-disruptive GPU verification he claims Akash is close to cracking, and the "tokens are rocket fuel" metaphor. His closer: $3T of competing AI capex and 280% Baltimore energy price rises make decentralized coordination the only way the industry integrates. Panel peers cover Helium Mobile's 400k subscribers/AT&T offload, GEODNET's buyback-and-burn, and io.net's rebrand away from token-talk.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [gpu-economics] 80% cheaper than cloud, deployed in two minutes
> "You can purchase GPU, CPU based compute for about 80% cheaper than what you would otherwise pay on the cloud... in many documented cases, publicly, users can deploy on Akash within two minutes."
> — [00:00:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=52s)

**Context:** His panel introduction of Akash's economic proposition (GPU resources "roughly 50% or less").

### [crypto-depin] Decentralize what is abundant and idle
> "DePIN shines when it mirrors its own design — like countless small parts, like trees in a forest or neurons in a brain... fragmented assets like GPUs or rooftop solar or Wi-Fi routers or city sensors... the line is simple: decentralize what is abundant and idle, and centralize what is scarce and indivisible."
> — [00:14:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=882s)

**Context:** His answer to "when does DePIN make sense": tokens are "the protocol layer that reduces entropy"; nuclear plants, hydro dams and submarine cables should stay centralized. (Quote begins at the end of the [00:13:58] block.)

### [local-compute] Inference is better at the edge
> "Inference wasn't very centralized. Inference is a lot more flexible when it comes to deployment. I would argue that it's better when it's at the edge. So getting access to edge resources with a common set of rules in a protocol is significantly better than having a centralized coordination layer."
> — [00:16:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=973s)

**Context:** Rebutting the moderator's premise that compute inherently centralizes; he separates inference (edge-friendly) from training (communication-heavy, fault-intolerant, serialized).

### [energy] A nuclear reactor per training run by 2030
> "The amount of energy we need is doubling every two years... the problem is so pervasive now that Congress invited me to testify on energy, and I did in May. The amount of energy is doubling to a point where we'll need a nuclear reactor by 2030 to train the state-of-the-art AI."
> — [00:18:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=1106s)

**Context:** Explaining why AI training must decentralize; references his May 2025 US Congressional testimony on AI energy demand.

### [decentralized-ai] A truly decentralized-trained model within 6-12 months
> "There's a lot of work that happened in the last few years on low communication algorithms, asynchronous algorithms, fault tolerance, all coming together. I believe in the next six [months] to one year you'll actually see a truly decentralized model, or a model that's trained on truly decentralized networks... it's not decentralized yet, but it will be, very very very soon."
> — [00:19:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=1148s)

**Context:** Time-bound prediction (from Oct 2025) that AI training will decentralize, forced by the energy crisis — "we cannot build new supply of energy faster in America anymore." He notes incentives for contributing compute to training runs remain unsolved.

### [decentralized-ai] Continuous GPU verification is unsolved — and Akash is close
> "Continuous non-disruptive verification is key. That means without affecting the workload that's deployed on the GPU... you got to continuously verify, and no one has solved the problem, and I think we're close to solving the problem and doing it in fully open source and decentralized manner."
> — [00:29:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=1798s)

**Context:** Why Akash didn't bootstrap supply with token incentives: build-time verification of hardware is gameable; continuous verification is the real problem.

### [gpu-economics] 70% utilization vs. AWS's 40% and Filecoin's 0.5%
> "Our utilization right now is somewhere north of 70%. For comparison, AWS has about 40% utilization... most decentralized networks have super high supply and less utilization — I think Filecoin has 0.5% utilization."
> — [00:30:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=1840s)

**Context:** Arguing Akash's demand-first approach beat token-bootstrapped supply gluts; leads into a planned "utilization guarantee" incentive covering only the delta above ~70%.

### [crypto-depin] Tokens are rocket fuel, not cruise fuel
> "Tokens are rocket fuel: they help you launch and bootstrap a network, but once you're in orbit you have to run without the fuel, or like very less fuel... somehow we got to orbit using hot air balloons instead of rocket fuel."
> — [00:31:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=1883s)

**Context:** His summary of DePIN token-incentive sustainability, noting Akash reached "orbit" without token-bootstrapped supply.

### [energy] AI centralization is spiking energy prices; only decentralization can coordinate it
> "Three trillion dollars worth of investment went into AI, mostly buying chips and building data centers... Bloomberg literally wrote a report saying that in Baltimore, where you have data centers, the cost of energy went up by 280% in five years — that's because of AI centralization... I don't see any other way that this industry can come together without decentralization."
> — [00:39:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xr6ZLHwuo2w&t=2358s)

**Context:** Closing answer on how DePIN reaches a trillion-dollar market: "you're not building companies, you're building networks" — decentralization brings coordination against competition. (Baltimore figure falls in the [00:40:02] block.)
