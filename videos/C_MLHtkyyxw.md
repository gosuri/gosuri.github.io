## Summary

Long-form Techpost interview with host Trent Lapinsky. Note: although uploaded 2024-05-20, the content appears recorded around 2019-2020 — Greg discusses Akash's mainnet-era launch "over the last year," a forthcoming economics white paper, and Jony Ive's Apple departure as recent news. Greg frames Akash as "a new operating system for the internet" tapping "unused 85% capacity in 8.2 million data centers," a peer-to-peer network of computation clusters for "planetary scale applications." The most future-relevant material is the Cactus case study: a peer-to-peer music streaming app that cut hosting costs 4-5x with ~40x more distribution, and planned a dedicated home hardware device that joins the Akash network so rewards from hosting other workloads subsidize the device to "almost free" — an early articulation of home devices earning income from spare compute. The rest is heavily philosophical: his key insight that distributed systems fail on experience rather than technology; coined terms "cognitive coefficiency" (value delivered vs. cognitive tax) and "growth gratification"; "fail forgiveness" in the Akash CLI; open-source business models (support, open-core, and the still-unsolved tokenized model); platforms as winner-take-all markets; "specialization is a myth"; his film photography practice and the "Faces of Silicon Valley" project; and a closing warning about crypto's "gross disregard for ethics" and integrity as Silicon Valley's real currency.

## Topics

decentralized cloud, developer experience, home compute, tokenized business models, open source economics, supercloud, peer-to-peer applications, entrepreneurship, design philosophy, crypto ethics

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] A new operating system for the internet on 85% unused capacity
> "We believe in the unstoppable web, and what we're doing with Akash is essentially creating a new operating system for the internet. And how we're doing that is by tapping into unused 85% capacity in 8.2 million data centers... Akash is essentially a peer-to-peer network of computation clusters designed for global-first applications, planetary-scale applications."
> — [00:02:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C_MLHtkyyxw&t=179s)

**Context:** His standing framing of the Akash thesis, repeated twice in the episode; anchored to internet-era permissionless principles ("you don't need anybody's permission to go to a website or host a website").

### [local-compute] Home hardware device paid for by hosting Akash workloads
> "This device sits in your house and becomes part of the Akash network, by which the rewards that this particular node gains by hosting other workloads through Akash will subsidize the cost for the hardware... they can bring the cost of the hardware device to almost free — free in the sense the customer is paying for electricity."
> — [00:09:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C_MLHtkyyxw&t=582s)

**Context:** Describing music-streaming app Cactus's planned home Music Center device; Greg calls this redistribution of incentives "the true promise of blockchains" — home hardware earning its keep via a decentralized cloud. Quote spans into the [00:10:27] block.

### [other] Distributed systems' challenge is experience, not technology
> "A key insight we learned over the past year, but something that has always been behind our thoughts: the challenge for distributed systems is not technology, it's the experience... the user almost is not at the center of what we do. So by taking a different approach, an approach that involves the user, [that] solves the adoption problem."
> — [00:12:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C_MLHtkyyxw&t=757s)

**Context:** His recurring adoption thesis for decentralized tech — "we're not going to win by simply overstating the mission but by aligning our actions to our mission."

### [other] Specialization is a myth
> "Specialization is also a myth. People say that in order to be successful you've got to be doing one thing and one thing only. It's a myth, because the greatest luminaries like Leonardo da Vinci or Einstein always had creative outlets."
> — [00:30:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C_MLHtkyyxw&t=1855s)

**Context:** His answer to the Peter Thiel "unpopular belief" question, tied to his argument that art/photography makes better builders.

### [other] Platforms are winner-take-all
> "Platforms is a winner-take-all market, and there is no place for the second person — it doesn't work that way. When you enter a market you want to absolutely dominate, and you want to do it because you want your values, that align with industry, to take precedence over people with greed."
> — [00:46:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C_MLHtkyyxw&t=2775s)

**Context:** Pushing back on the host's Apple-vs-Google framing; market dominance as a values vehicle, with high margins acceptable if aligned with core values.
