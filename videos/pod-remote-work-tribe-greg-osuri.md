## Summary

Interview with host Jessica Malnik focused on how Greg runs Overclock Labs / Akash Network as a radically transparent, remote, async-first company — lighter on compute predictions than his usual appearances. He recounts the origin story: AngelHack (200,000 developers across 50 cities) exposed how hard zero-to-one infrastructure was; Overclock Labs started in 2015, published the white paper in 2017 "prophesying" that centralized cloud would lead to the reliability and cost problems visible today ("AWS going down every other month"); Cornell coined the term "supercloud," and Akash built the first one — open source and decentralized so its principles can't be changed as it grows. The network launched in 2020 and took off during the 2023 GPU crisis, when "Akash was the only one that had GPUs." Success stories include a Cornell student's Akash-powered startup acquired by NVIDIA, and Senegalese students without bank accounts using cloud for the first time via crypto.

On culture: "radical transparency" — on-chain, cryptographically verified revenue; zero private repos; all product decisions via public Akash Enhancement Proposals (AEPs); ~500 contributors; an internal AI trained on company history for onboarding; over-communication in writing so meetings are minimized (only project-based standups). He argues transparency reduces stress ("when you have nothing to hide, you have less stress"), disarms haters and short-sellers, and that the company raised only $1.8M total yet Akash was at one point valued at $3B (about $0.5B now). Lightning-round lessons: lean startup underemphasized brand ("build less, but build it well" — critical in an era of "AI slop"); big first rounds breed complacency; dinner picks are Steve Jobs and Benjamin Franklin; his desert-island item is a GPU machine with a fully loaded local AI. Whisper renders his name "Greg Osury" and Overclock Labs as "Overcloud Labs."

**Context:** Recounting Overclock Labs' founding thesis; he claims AWS "went down four times this month" (January 2026) as vindication.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash built the first-ever supercloud
> "Cornell really had a vision of creating a cloud where you can take this compute and actually deliver a similar product, if not better than what we have with Amazon. And we really took that idea and we implemented the first ever, or we built the first ever supercloud. I mean, no one actually built supercloud before us. And we took it one step further. We made it open source. We made it decentralized."
> — [00:03:10](https://www.theremoteworktribe.com/2026/01/15/the-remote-work-tribe-podcast-greg-osuri/)

**Context:** Credits Cornell University with coining "supercloud" [00:02:23]; the premise is vast underutilized compute — data centers used ~15% of the time and powerful home gaming GPUs.
