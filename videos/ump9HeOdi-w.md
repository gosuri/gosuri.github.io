## Summary

Episode 4 of Cryptocito's "Splitting ATOMs" livestream panel, recorded during ETHDenver 2022, with Greg Osuri, Zaki Manian (Sommelier/Iqlusion), and co-host Jack. The bulk of the airtime is Zaki's: liquid staking module vs. superfluid staking vs. pStake, why 150 validators is adequate for now (with a path to ~500 via Tendermint mempool/libp2p work), Babylon chain merge-mining Cosmos SDK chains with Bitcoin to shrink unbonding periods, ETH2's stake concentration (Binance/Kraken/Lido/Coinbase controlling roughly a third of block production) vs. Cosmos Hub needing top-7 validators to censor and top-22 to rewrite, and his view that outside Bitcoin, Monero, and Aleo nothing will still be proof-of-work in five years.

Greg's contributions: fresh from ETHDenver conversations, he declares "shared state doesn't scale, sovereign state is the end state"; calls ATOM the reserve currency of Cosmos while noting he's "not a big fan of shared security" (liquidity and service discovery being the Hub's real value); argues decentralized applications and validator infrastructure should leave AWS/Google for decentralized clouds (Akash being ~90% cheaper than Azure, ~80% cheaper than Amazon) because a hyperscaler shutting nodes down threatens chain security; extends the decentralization definition to community participation and "continuity of operations" (citing Secret Network's rebirth after Enigma's regulatory shutdown); warns the nearer threat than regulators is browsers and platforms censoring decentralized-web URLs (Chrome/Gmail/Twitter blocking Sia/Skynet links); and floats wallet-provisioned sovereign frontends — launching your own Osmosis UI, RPC server, even validator node straight from Keplr with Akash as the runtime. Zaki notes Greg implemented longer-bonding-for-more-voting-power in an early Akash version. Ends with Cosmos community boosterism: "we win slowly."

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [other] Shared state doesn't scale; sovereign state is the end state
> "Most of the conversations I've been having with people is about sovereignty and scalability, and I think the general conclusion is: shared state doesn't scale, sovereign state is the end state."
> — [00:03:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=213s)

**Context:** Greg reporting from ETHDenver 2022; frames app-chain sovereignty as the winning architecture ("ETH is positioning for Cosmos").

### [crypto-depin] Not a fan of shared security; the Hub's value is liquidity and service discovery
> "Atom is the reserve currency of Cosmos... I'm not a big fan of shared security in general... really for me, liquidity and service discovery is probably one of the biggest things Cosmos Hub can bring to the ecosystem."
> — [00:04:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=257s)

**Context:** Asked for his view on the Cosmos Hub's positioning; also credits sovereignty and avoiding an inherited "economic burden" as why Akash adopted the Cosmos SDK. Quote spans into [00:05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=300s).

### [cloud-decentralization] Decentralized apps must not run on Amazon
> "I truly believe all the applications — I mean especially decentralized applications — should run on a decentralized cloud. Just don't run on Amazon... if a lot of nodes are running on Amazon, Amazon shutting you down could affect the security of the network, but rather if you have nodes running elsewhere, you're okay."
> — [00:42:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=2567s)

**Context:** On validator infrastructure's web2 dependency; adds Akash is "like 90% cheaper than Azure and like 80% cheaper than Amazon — so why wouldn't you" ([00:43:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=2611s)).

### [cloud-decentralization] Browsers blocking URLs is a nearer threat than regulators
> "I think we should be more worried about browsers blocking URLs. See what's happening with Skynet, for example, Sia — Chrome blocks 80 percent of Sia URLs; most of Europe you can't even access Sia URLs... in Gmail if you put a Sia link it will go away, Twitter will block it. I think you're gonna see a lot of that come by."
> — [00:46:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=2785s)

**Context:** Responding to Zaki's point that regulators will target frontends; Greg says Akash is moving away from reused TLDs toward unique addresses to resist this ([00:47:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=2828s)).

### [cloud-decentralization] Sovereign frontends launched from your wallet
> "There's no reason why you shouldn't launch an Osmosis UI directly from Keplr that's fully dedicated to an RPC server that's fully dedicated to you — maybe even a validator node if you want that level of sovereignty, a full-stack DeFi layer... you should be able to launch that directly from your Keplr wallet... the user ultimately is responsible for the UI and the company doesn't operate [it] anyways, and Akash provides you the runtime for your container."
> — [00:47:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=2871s)

**Context:** Introduced by the co-host as "some of the cool ideas that you [Greg] thought about"; a personal-infrastructure vision of users self-hosting their own frontends/nodes on Akash. **Speaker:** the setup line may be the co-host paraphrasing Greg's idea; the elaboration is Greg's.

### [other] Decentralization is community participation and continuity of operations
> "Decentralization is just not about coin distribution, but it's also community participation... more often than not we just look at technology and number of nodes... and ultimately it comes down to continuity of operations."
> — [00:41:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=2482s)

**Context:** Extending Zaki's answer on why decentralization matters, after the Secret Network/Enigma example of a community reviving a chain shut down by regulators.

Cosmos — we win slowly."
> — [00:50:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ump9HeOdi-w&t=3043s)
