## Summary

Same Ashton Addison "Blockchain Interviews" episode as wAAHMYBOBSo (InvestmentPitch Media upload) — identical transcript, recorded the day after Akash Mainnet 2 launched. Greg introduces Akash as "the world's first open source cloud" aggregating the ~85% of data center capacity sitting unused, with 2-3x cost savings. He compares the big-four cloud oligopoly to airlines and telecom — consolidated industries that stopped innovating. Initial focus is DeFi/blockchain workloads for ideological alignment and censorship resistance, with machine learning named as the next market, citing an Andreessen Horowitz finding that ~20% of AI-company margins go to the cloud. He envisions a peer-to-peer cloud where AI companies sell their idle in-house GPU cluster time (used only 2-3 hours/day for training) for credits, becoming both providers and users. Mainnet 2 fulfills the 2017 whitepaper's reverse-auction marketplace with audited provider attributes; he promises a "Uniswap-style deployment experience" and envisions DAO-managed "ownerless web services" via IBC. On AKT, he explains staking, streaming payments, and inflation-funded subsidies per his "Bootstrapping a Free Market by Borrowing from the Future" paper, which he says can cut costs a further 10x.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [gpu-economics] AI companies' idle GPU clusters become a peer-to-peer cloud
> "The model that we envisioned for an AI company to function is more of a peer-to-peer cloud... they have a server from the offices that has GPU clusters... they only use these clusters maybe two or three hours a day while they're training these models... they can install Akash and offer this unused capacity and earn credits in exchange... and they can use these credits for when they want to use peak capacity. That's really when you see providers becoming users and users becoming providers."
> — [00:05:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0JrIenhz0fQ&t=343s)

**Context:** 2021 vision for ML as Akash's next use case — companies' own hardware, idle most of the day, feeding a shared compute market.

### [gpu-economics] 20% of AI company margins go to the cloud
> "The most attractive area for us to focus is machine learning... because that segment is currently suffering with the immense cost problem... [an Andreessen Horowitz] report recently... stated essentially 20 [percent] of margins tend to go to the cloud. It's extremely hard being a growth stage AI company that's trying to survive in this increasingly prohibitive layer called cloud."
> — [00:05:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0JrIenhz0fQ&t=343s)

**Context:** Captions garble "Andreessen Horowitz" as "and do some horrible throwing"; refers to a16z's "The Cost of Cloud" analysis. Stated as reason ML is Akash's next market.

### [cloud-decentralization] Oligopolies stop innovating — the cloud is next
> "Oligopolies tend to create products that are misaligned sometimes to the customer base, and they tend to create competitive moats that makes it prohibitive for new players to come and partake in what should be a competitive marketplace... cloud, if you think about it, is the fabric that runs the society."
> — [00:02:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0JrIenhz0fQ&t=129s)

**Context:** Comparing the big-four cloud to airlines and telecom — industries that "just stopped innovating" after consolidation.

### [cloud-decentralization] Ownerless web services owned by the world
> "I had this vision of ownerless web services, where people can actually own web services — not a single entity, not a single human being, but the world can actually own a web service that is managed by a DAO and has a threshold signature for deploying and controlling — all that can be realized now with the decentralized cloud."
> — [00:12:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0JrIenhz0fQ&t=768s)

**Context:** On IBC composability arriving; paired with his promise of a "Uniswap-style deployment experience" with no emails, passwords, or credit cards.

### [crypto-depin] Inflation subsidies can cut cloud costs another 10x
> "I wrote a paper called Bootstrapping a Free Market by Borrowing from the Future... you have this inflation and you can use this inflation to subsidize some of the costs and that in turn can actually accelerate growth... using that we can actually reduce the cost even further to 10x... it's of course an economic hack."
> — [00:14:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0JrIenhz0fQ&t=898s)

**Context:** Comparing token-inflation subsidies to US corn and Indian textile subsidies; 100M genesis supply, 389M max over ~8 years on a decaying curve.
