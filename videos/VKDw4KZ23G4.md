## Summary

A Messari Mainnet 2024 keynote — "compute: past, present and future" — that is Greg's most complete public articulation of his worldview. He traces computing's pendulum: 1960s mainframes → personal computers ("shift towards individualism") → the internet's decentralized open protocols → recentralization into cloud, with ~70% of enterprise IT equipment spend flowing to four hyperscalers. He cites a Semafor story of a student who couldn't get GPUs on AWS but got H100s/A100s on Akash and built a company later acquired by Nvidia; GPU lead times of 18 months–2 years; and a16z's "trillion dollar paradox" (half of every dollar spent on cloud services is a "cloud tax" paid to unelected entities). The CrowdStrike outage grounding the airline industry illustrates opacity and fragility; AWS antitrust scrutiny echoes 1870s railroads and early-1900s oil. The central risk: "digital feudalism" — the few who control AI compute rule the many, including the scenario of someone cracking AGI in secret and exploiting it. His counter-thesis: compute should be a public utility "as free as the air we breathe," delivered by DePIN networks (Filecoin storage, Akash compute, Livepeer rendering). Akash stats: 65 provider regions (vs. ~30 for AWS), ~27,000 leases deployed last quarter, 80% cheaper than AWS by tapping idle capacity (data centers 80–85% unused; ex-mining and upgraded ML fleets), four years with zero unplanned downtime vs. AWS "down 200 times a year," Nvidia as its biggest user, and Nous Research's DisTrO making decentralized training practical. Q&A covers reliability-vs-price marketplace granularity, pre-agreed reclamations, and a coming managed-services marketplace where open-source authors sell hosting directly ("in a year's time").

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] The risk is a return to feudalism — including secret AGI captured by the few
> "The big risk as a society as a whole we have is the return of feudalism. Feudalistic society is where few have power over the many... the few today are the ones that are running AI... There's this discussion about artificial general intelligence: what happens when somebody cracks it and no one knows that they cracked it? Because there's no incentive for them to share this AGI — they can very well go and play in the stock market or do things that are significantly advantageous to the few. So if we don't do something about this layer, we go back to feudalism... I call the term digital feudalism."
> — [00:08:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKDw4KZ23G4&t=528s)

**Context:** The keynote's central warning, framing hyperscaler concentration as a civilizational risk; "a future where corporations, or the digital lords, control humanity as a whole."

### [cloud-decentralization] Compute should be a public utility, "as free as the air we breathe"
> "Compute should be a public utility. Compute should be as free as the air we breathe and water we drink, so that innovators everywhere, from every corner of the globe, have the tools to build and create and transform without barrier... It's very important that we return to sovereignty — control over what gets built, control over what goes and touches our daily lives."
> — [00:10:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKDw4KZ23G4&t=614s)

**Context:** His positive thesis after the feudalism warning; decentralized computing as a return to the internet's 1969 founding principle.

### [gpu-economics] The "cloud tax": half of every dollar for online services goes to four providers
> "Andreessen did a study, a paper called the Trillion Dollar Paradox, where they researched S-1 filings of cloud companies, and turns out half of every dollar you spend on one of these cloud services — be it Netflix or Asana — goes to one of the four cloud providers. What this effectively is, is a cloud tax that we all pay but don't really realize we're paying, to this group of unelected individuals or entities."
> — [00:05:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKDw4KZ23G4&t=355s)

**Context:** Part of the cost argument; preceded by GPU scarcity data — 18-month-to-2-year Nvidia purchase lead times and near-impossibility of renting on centralized clouds ([00:05:09]).

### [gpu-economics] Akash delivers the same compute as AWS for 80% cheaper by reaching idle capacity
> "You can get the same amount of compute that you get from AWS for 80% cheaper, and the reason for that is decentralized systems can reach places where centralized systems cannot... closed-off networks, corporate networks, where there is heavily underutilized compute... about 80% or 85% of the time most data centers are not used, because you need to have peak capacity to address peak workloads."
> — [00:11:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKDw4KZ23G4&t=701s)

**Context:** He also cites idle GPU fleets at ex-crypto-mining companies and ML companies that upgraded their training hardware. Quote spans to [00:13:06].

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash: four years, zero unplanned downtime; AWS down ~200 times a year
> "Akash has been alive for about four years; it has not gone down a single time. Whereas if you look at networks like Amazon Web Services, it goes down about 200 times a year... The reason why Akash is incredibly reliable is because there's not a single company or a single cluster that controls the network, but rather a distributed network of nodes that interact with each other in a peer-to-peer manner without a central point of failure."
> — [00:13:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKDw4KZ23G4&t=830s)

**Context:** Reliability argument; he pairs it with the CrowdStrike incident and an anecdote of an Akash auth exploit fixed by an open-source user before anyone was impacted ("fault tolerance, not fault avoidance").

### [decentralized-ai] Nous Research's DisTrO cracked decentralized training on Akash
> "Nous recently came out with this incredible technique called DisTrO, which really is a practical way of leveraging decentralized GPUs for machine learning training. For context, training has been extremely hard to do on decentralized or distributed networks because of latency requirements, but Nous has figured out how to do that on Akash Network. So Akash is unlocking this new generation of companies that are able to get resources that they couldn't get from traditional cloud."
> — [00:18:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKDw4KZ23G4&t=1131s)

**Context:** Ecosystem examples; he also claims Nvidia is Akash's biggest user and "Akash is the only product integrated into Nvidia products" ([00:18:07]), and plugs Venice AI as a private, uncensored ChatGPT alternative running on Akash.

### [open-source-ai] A managed-services marketplace where open-source authors cut out the middleman — within a year
> "Amazon is offering these services that [are] essentially open source software white-labeled as Amazon services — that hurts the author of the open source software whose only business model is hosting... The way we're going to do that is to allow the open source author... to offer their services directly to customers, cutting out the middleman... Open source services need an open source network... databases and caches and data warehousing services — you'll probably see them in a year's time on Akash."
> — [00:25:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKDw4KZ23G4&t=1545s)

**Context:** Q&A answer on competing with AWS managed services; he cites Bittensor subnets as an early proof of incentivized service networks.
