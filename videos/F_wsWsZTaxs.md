## Summary

Fireside chat at Akash Accelerate 2025 with Greg interviewing Javier Villamizar, SoftBank operating partner involved in the $500B Stargate AI data center program. Villamizar calls AI infrastructure "the railroads of the 21st century," describes SoftBank's $1T Arizona/Illinois AI-robotics hub ambitions (robots to assemble data centers), and advocates a hybrid energy mix — solar/renewables as components but nuclear "underrated" and transformative in 4-6 years. Greg contributes his own theses throughout: training's concentrated energy need doubles every two years, so by 2030 state-of-the-art models will require gigawatt-scale supply and "nuclear will be the only way," even though America's last reactor took ~14 years and interconnect queues run ~10 years; solar works for residential but utility-scale 1.2GW is "essentially a nuclear reactor"; and Jevons paradox means efficiency gains only grow usage. He asks Villamizar about decentralized energy — microgrids producing and consuming at the edge, interacting peer-to-peer — and Villamizar responds that centralized and decentralized compute will be complementary and that SoftBank are "big believers in decentralized physical infrastructure." In rapid fire, Villamizar bets AI reaches 20-30% of US energy by 2030; Greg agrees, arguing the DOE's conservative 12%-by-2028 estimate implies 30% by 2030 in reality. Chip architecture and on-die cooling are named as underrated efficiency levers, and faster nuclear/SMR permitting as the top regulatory fix.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [energy] By 2030, training frontier models requires gigawatt supply — nuclear is the only way
> "Considering the energy need — particularly concentrated energy need for training — is doubling every two years, so the expectation by 2030 the only way to train the state-of-the-art model is with a gigawatt supply. So nuclear will be the only way to do so. Unfortunately, the last nuclear reactor we built in America took about 14 years, and the interconnect requests now average anywhere from 10 years here for new data center builds."
> — [00:07:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F_wsWsZTaxs&t=445s)

**Context:** Greg's setup to a policy question about reducing interconnect queues and regulatory burden.

### [energy] AI will consume 30% of US energy by 2030
> "I'm aligned with you there. I mean, Department of Energy report recently said 12%, and DOE is known to take a very conservative approach and they always miss their estimation. So when DOE says 12% of US energy will be consumed on AI by 2028, I think in reality it'll be 30% by 2030."
> — [00:19:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F_wsWsZTaxs&t=1151s)

**Context:** Rapid-fire round; Villamizar first bets 20-30% of total US energy by 2030 and Greg endorses the high end, discounting the DOE estimate.

### [energy] Utility-scale AI power means a nuclear reactor, not solar
> "What are your general thoughts on renewables through solar versus small modular reactors that can produce 24-hour continuous power? As I know solar is great for residential, but when you're doing utility scale — I mean 1.2 gigawatt — you're looking at essentially a nuclear reactor."
> — [00:04:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F_wsWsZTaxs&t=264s)

**Context:** Greg's question about SoftBank's 1.2GW Texas energy deal, embedding his recurring solar-vs-nuclear claim.

### [energy] Jevons paradox: efficiency will only increase AI usage
> "But also there is this Jevons paradox, right — the more efficient something gets, the more usage it gets. I mean, I always want my ChatGPT to go faster... but I'm able to do a lot more things that I couldn't."
> — [00:14:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F_wsWsZTaxs&t=890s)

**Context:** Responding to Villamizar's point that energy-efficient AI-native chip architectures (Cerebras, SambaNova) could change power projections.

### [energy] Microgrids at the edge, interacting peer-to-peer
> "What are your thoughts on decentralization in terms of energy — so small micro grids producing or capturing energy, maybe even consuming at the edge, and interacting in a peer-to-peer manner, maybe contributing back to a larger hub?"
> — [00:16:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F_wsWsZTaxs&t=1019s)

**Context:** Greg's closing question sketching his decentralized-energy vision; Villamizar answers that crowdsourced energy becomes "the flywheel for the adoption of AI" and centralized/decentralized compute will be complementary.
