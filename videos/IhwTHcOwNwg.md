## Summary

Interview with Michael (SkyInclude), aimed at the Handshake/dweb community. Greg pitches Akash as the compute leg of a decentralized stack — Akash for compute, Sia/Skynet for storage, Handshake for naming — which he calls "the holy trinity... the big foundation for an unstoppable web." He explains Akash's economics: ~85% of capacity across 8.4 million data centers sits unused, and tapping it lets Akash price at one-third of Amazon, getting cheaper as supply grows. A large segment covers the Akash Supermini, a "portable supercomputer" with a GPU for ML/AI workloads, framed as "instead of you going to the cloud, bring the cloud to you" — a home cloud that keeps keys sovereign at home and can sell idle compute on the marketplace for tokens. He reveals 300 units sold out in two weeks with a ~60,000-person waitlist, but worries most buyers want passive mining income (Helium-style expectations) rather than usage, calling that intent "fundamentally flawed" and saying he's rethinking the release. The back half discusses the Fastly outage (which took down half the internet that day), centralized platforms being "weaponized by state actors" via deplatforming (Parler/AWS), and the decentralized web as disaster recovery. He cites early traction — Akash three months post-launch, deployments doubling monthly, 50-100 deploys/day, 17x growth in two months — and plugs Handshake resolvers on Akash providers, grants for dweb builders, and the upcoming Osmosis DEX launch (June 17) as AKT's first DEX.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [local-compute] Bring the cloud to you: the Supermini home supercomputer
> "It's a small supercomputer, it's a portable supercomputer... it has a GPU system that you can run machine learning applications, AI applications... and the idea is instead of you going to the cloud, bring the cloud to you. So you want the comfort of a cloud in your home, and how can we replicate that experience."
> — [00:08:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhwTHcOwNwg&t=510s)

**Context:** Describing the Akash Supermini (2021) — an early articulation of his local-compute thesis: consumer hardware at home running AI workloads instead of renting from centralized cloud.

### [local-compute] Idle home compute earns tokens on the marketplace
> "The cool thing about Supermini is, well, when you're not using it you can give this in a market — you can sell the compute in the market and earn tokens."
> — [00:09:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhwTHcOwNwg&t=553s)

**Context:** The home-GPU-earning-income model. He then cautions that of the ~60,000 waitlist "most of them are interested in the mining part and not necessarily the usage part," an intent he calls "fundamentally flawed" [00:10:39], and explicitly warns not to expect Helium-style earnings.

### [local-compute] Home cloud with sovereignty — keys never leave your home
> "The vision is to have a home cloud — cloud in your home — and have the security and the sovereignty of the home. Like, you don't want your keys to leave your home but yet still be able to run a validator node on a remote server and do the lightweight, which is the signature part... so you can truly create distributed systems and really secure systems with a home computer."
> — [00:10:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhwTHcOwNwg&t=639s)

**Context:** Data-sovereignty framing of local compute: heavy lifting remote, sensitive operations (key signing) local.

### [cloud-decentralization] 85% of data center capacity is unused; Akash is one-third Amazon's price
> "We make a better cloud by tapping into unused capacity, and turns out about 85 or so [percent of] capacity in 8.4 million data centers is unused... we provide a much similar capability but much better user experience at a price point that is one-third... what Amazon would charge... because we tap into servers that are not used, and as we get more supply it's only going to get cheaper."
> — [00:02:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhwTHcOwNwg&t=127s)

**Context:** His recurring supply-side argument against the four companies controlling cloud; quote spans into the [00:02:51] block for the one-third pricing claim.

### [cloud-decentralization] The holy trinity for an unstoppable web
> "You have three protocols that are decentralized... working with each other, interoperating... I call it the holy trinity — Skynet, Akash and Handshake — because that is the big foundation for an unstoppable web."
> — [00:05:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhwTHcOwNwg&t=339s)

**Context:** On users organically combining Akash (compute), Sia/Skynet (storage), Handshake (naming) into full decentralized deployments.

### [cloud-decentralization] Centralized platforms are being weaponized; dweb is disaster recovery
> "Even the lower-level services such as Amazon Web Services deplatforming Parler... the point is these companies have the power, and that very weakness is being weaponized by state actors... in a case like that you want to have a disaster recovery platform that has much lower chances of failure... if you look at decentralized web, I think it is a lot more crucial than we originally thought, and 2020 proved that to be the case."
> — [00:20:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhwTHcOwNwg&t=1232s)

**Context:** Prompted by the Fastly outage that morning and deplatforming discussion; he argues protocol changes get more scrutiny so "system-wide failures don't happen that very often in decentralized networks" [00:19:50].

### [other] Early traction: deployments doubling monthly, 17x in two months
> "Akash just literally launched three months ago and we are more than doubling every month in terms of applications we deployed... right now, I believe, about 50 application deploys a day... sometimes we range from 50 to 100 deployments a day, and we're three months old. We grew 17x in the last two months in terms of deployments."
> — [00:26:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhwTHcOwNwg&t=1569s)

**Context:** Countering "dweb is early" skepticism; he adds "growth is coming from usage of these decentralized networks, and I think this is a good time to invest your time and energy" [00:26:51].
