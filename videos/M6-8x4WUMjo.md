## Summary

Weekly Akash community Spaces hosted by Nadia Bajuelo, with Greg Osuri giving ecosystem updates and Jigar Patel of Praetor (provider-onboarding tooling that cut setup time by 83%, to under 20 minutes) as guest. Greg's updates: the Paloma chain running ~7% of its (testnet) validator nodes on Akash — framed as the first "majority penetration" moment for decentralized cloud in a blockchain's validator set; anticipation of the Ethereum Merge freeing an enormous amount of GPU capacity that Akash's upcoming GPU support could absorb, with Akash GPU pricing predicted 20-50% below market; the Cloudflare outage as proof that Web3 infrastructure is unacceptably dependent on Web2; ethernodes.org data showing 40%+ of Ethereum nodes on Amazon (54%+ on big cloud), with Amazon cast as crypto's hidden middleman; Solana's mobile phone announcement, which Greg turns into a sketch of lightweight processing on phones paired with heavyweight processing on Akash (games as example), potentially combined with Helium 5G; and a macro take that 2022-23 would mirror the 2018-19 cycle with a year-long downturn followed by a recovery rooted in real growth, with Cosmos-style sovereign chains at center stage. He also discusses hiring (VP marketing, senior staff engineers) and answers an AMA question on why staking take-income is gated on SEC regulatory clarity (securities risk; Gensler calling only Bitcoin a commodity), noting AKT is a utility token for paying for compute. Praetor previews persistent-storage support and node-membership management via web UI.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [gpu-economics] Merge will strand enormous GPU capacity
> "As we all know, post-Merge, when it moves from proof of work to proof of stake, there's going to be an enormous amount of GPU capacity that will be unutilized... the fact that we will be able to leverage GPUs on Akash very soon, it's a great way for ETH miners to earn additional income on their existing capital."
> — [00:05:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6-8x4WUMjo&t=302s)

**Context:** Discussing a press piece on Akash as an option for ETH miners after the proof-of-stake transition (which happened September 2022); GPU support on Akash was still pre-launch ("can't wait to show you a demo very soon").

### [gpu-economics] Akash GPUs 20-50% cheaper than market
> "Currently GPUs are very expensive on the cloud. We predict Akash's GPUs to be anywhere from 20 to 50 percent lower than what the market offers — that's an enormous cost savings for highly compute-intensive applications."
> — [00:05:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6-8x4WUMjo&t=345s)

**Context:** Quantified price forecast for the then-unreleased Akash GPU marketplace ("a car's GPU" in the auto-captions = "Akash's GPUs").

### [cloud-decentralization] Web3's dependence on Web2 must end
> "The whole purpose of building decentralized cloud infrastructure is to build a reliable infrastructure... the fact that a lot of dapps and nodes and our entire infrastructure depends on Web2 is not acceptable in my book, and the Cloudflare outage clearly showed that our decentralized infrastructure is very, very reliant on Web2 — and that needs to change now."
> — [00:06:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6-8x4WUMjo&t=389s)

**Context:** Reacting to the June 2022 Cloudflare outage that took down much of the crypto ecosystem; he adds "we have a lot of work to do and we have to do it quicker."

### [cloud-decentralization] Amazon is crypto's middleman — 40% of Ethereum nodes
> "Over 40 [percent] of Ethereum nodes currently run on Amazon alone, and over 54 [percent] of the nodes are running on the big cloud... that exposes the vulnerability... is Ethereum really as decentralized as it claims to be when one company has over forty percent of the network processing power?"
> — [00:07:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6-8x4WUMjo&t=475s)

**Context:** Citing ethernodes.org real-time data; he follows up that "the whole notion of not having a middleman — but actually we're having middlemen in crypto, and that middleman happens to be Amazon."

### [local-compute] Phones do light compute, decentralized cloud does the heavy lifting
> "Mobile phones... are lightweight devices... a use case for Akash there would be lightweight processing on the mobile phone and heavyweight processing on Akash, on a decentralized cloud. So you can see how the mobile operating system can work in tandem with the cloud operating system... in a highly composable way, without the user even knowing about it."
> — [00:12:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6-8x4WUMjo&t=733s)

**Context:** Reacting to the Solana phone announcement; his example is games with high-end graphics rendered on Akash and streamed to the device. He extends it: "you can see a future where Akash, Helium and Solana working in tandem... deliver the user experience on a mobile device that has never been done before" [00:12:56], possible "in a year's time" once IBC connects them [00:13:38].

### [crypto-depin] Bear-market costs drive validators to decentralized cloud
> "We predicted that in a bear market the costs are going to be an issue, especially for validators trying to scaffold, and Akash providing a cost-efficient solution that's self-sovereign is a very attractive value proposition... really excited to see a majority penetration in a blockchain for the first time by a decentralized cloud."
> — [00:04:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6-8x4WUMjo&t=259s)

**Context:** On Paloma (Cosmos SDK messaging chain) running ~7% of its testnet validator nodes on Akash.

### [other] 2022-23 downturn will end in a recovery rooted in real growth, centered on Cosmos
> "I think we're going to see a similar pattern in 2022 and 2023: I think we're going to see a year-long crisis and then it's going to be an upturn, and I think that will be rooted in real growth... last time it was about product-market fit, this time it's going to be about growth and people using real products."
> — [00:15:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6-8x4WUMjo&t=904s)

**Context:** Comparing to the 2018 peak-to-trough and 2019 DeFi-led recovery; he adds "Ethereum and none of the actual shared-state chains can deliver that level of user experience — that's why I'm extremely bullish on the Cosmos model, the sovereign model" [00:15:47].
