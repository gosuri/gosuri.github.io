## Summary

Wide-ranging podcast with Proof of Coverage Media recorded just after the 2024 US election. Greg covers his Washington work (Tom Emmer's team, FIT21 classifying sufficiently decentralized tokens as commodities under CFTC), argues the incoming administration marks "a generational shift in American governance," and speculates DOGE could push decentralized tech (public records on immutable ledgers) into government — noting a DoD hackathon where agencies pooled idle data-center capacity on Akash.

On AI policy he's maximalist: regulate AI's outputs (weapons via ATF, medicine via FDA), not the technology — "the best regulation is no regulation for AI" — and accuses OpenAI/Microsoft/Google of lobbying to ban open-source AI via national-security scare tactics.

The centerpiece is his energy-bottleneck thesis: frontier-model energy needs grow ~half an order of magnitude (5x) per year; GPT-4-class models used ~10MW data centers, the next generation needs 250MW, then 1.2GW — nuclear-reactor territory the US can't build in under ~7 years (Georgia's Vogtle: ~$32B, ~14 years). The escape valve is federated training over the internet: Nous Research's DisTrO and Prime Intellect's OpenDiLoCo enable heterogeneous GPUs — "a home... a garage for all I care" — to join training runs, with crypto incentives and tokenized model ownership (open weights earned by compute contributors, paid inference). He predicts a 100B-parameter distributed training run before end of 2024 and a "DeAI summer" going mainstream by mid-2025 after ICML.

On Akash: GPU revenue up 1700% YoY since GPUs launched August 2023, 80-95% utilization, credit-card payments boosting activation from 0.5% to 75%, and users including Venice.ai, Nous Research, Prime Intellect, universities, and rendering/gaming workloads.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [energy] AI energy needs grow 5x per year with model size
> "As LLMs get larger and larger and larger, the amount of energy they consume also goes in proportion to the size of the LLM that's being trained... the growth of energy needed every year is about half an order magnitude — that means every year we are [5]x-ing the amount of energy we need to develop the state-of-the-art models for that year."
> — [00:21:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rp_LL6tPDFU&t=1261s)

**Context:** Asked what major trends will shape the world; opening of his AI energy-bottleneck thesis.

### [energy] In two years, training a frontier model will need a nuclear reactor
> "The next big LLM will need [a] 250 megawatt data center, and a year after that is going to be 1.2 gigawatt data center, and the only place to get 1.2 gigawatts of capacity is a nuclear reactor. So in two years we're going to need a new nuclear reactor to train the most powerful state-of-the-art model, and I think that is a huge bottleneck we don't talk much about."
> — [00:22:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rp_LL6tPDFU&t=1349s)

**Context:** Extrapolating from GPT-4's ~10MW training data center; he notes the last US reactor (Georgia) cost ~$32B and took ~14 years, so optimistically seven years to build.

### [energy] Only China can build reactors fast enough; energy is the ignored bottleneck
> "We don't pay enough attention to the energy bottlenecks. We just keep assuming these models are going to get larger and larger, but where do you get the power from? The only place you can get the power is China... only China can build a nuclear reactor in three years — they've done it — and the US is not that competitive anymore, sadly."
> — [00:28:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rp_LL6tPDFU&t=1735s)

**Context:** Wrapping his energy thesis; argues the US can't out-build the power curve, motivating distributed alternatives.
