## Summary

Interview with crypto analyst Michaël van de Poppe recorded roughly a week after Akash mainnet 2 launched (the "completion of our white paper"). Greg recounts his background — growing up on a farm in India, learning to code from books at 12, founding AngelHack (150K developers; Firebase launched there), contributing to Kubernetes — and Akash's origin: discovering ~85% of deployed data-center capacity across ~8.2 million data centers sits unused. He frames Akash as solving cost (2-3x cheaper than AWS; mainnet-2 providers "at least half" of Amazon pricing), developer productivity (5 hours on AWS vs. 10 minutes on Akash, no signups — the Uniswap-vs-Binance UX analogy), and censorship resistance — calling Akash "the first open source cloud" and a "super cloud," complementary rather than competitive to Amazon/Google (Equinix, which supplies the hyperscalers, was Akash's first provider). Notable 2021-era artifacts: the Supermini plug-and-earn home compute device planned for end of year (300 units, 15,000-person waitlist), the Ethereum-is-a-proof-of-concept take, IBC as "the TCP/IP of blockchains" (Akash the second chain to connect), a four-to-six-month prediction for breakout Cosmos DeFi products, THORChain nodes as the anonymity use case, and the roadmap: persistent storage, a peer-to-peer machine-learning cloud with GPU compute (ML companies earning tokens from idle office GPUs), and inflation-to-take-income economic transition. He closes with his life goal: making cloud computing affordable enough that 10-year-olds anywhere can access it — within four years.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] 85% of data-center capacity is underutilized
> "The customers running their own data centers are sitting on humongous underutilized resources — 85% or so of data center capacity that's deployed is underutilized, and we're talking about 8.2 million data centers."
> — [00:04:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=264s)

**Context:** The founding insight behind Akash, discovered while deploying Kubernetes-based solutions to enterprise customers at Overclock Labs.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash is the first open source cloud
> "The only way to do that is to remove the sense of control and centralization, to really go with an open model both for the source code... and provide a platform that's permissionless, self-sovereign and non-custodial — and that's what Akash is. Akash is, in a sense, the first open source cloud."
> — [00:13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=780s)

**Context:** Summing up the three problems Akash targets — cost, developer productivity, and censorship (he cites deplatforming risk even for "small misalignment with the company's policy").

### [local-compute] Supermini: a plug-and-earn home data center device
> "You can buy one of the devices that we will be shipping end of the year, called Supermini, which is essentially a very tiny, portable version of what a data center computer would look like... it's designed for home users, designed for plug-in-earn... we're only making like 300 of these, and we have like 15,000 people in the waitlist."
> — [00:18:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=1122s)

**Context:** Early (2021) concrete product for home compute earning income on Akash; he notes it was deliberately delayed until "the economics and the network is primed to even accept something like a Supermini." (Waitlist detail in the [00:19:25] block.)

### [other] Ethereum is a proof of concept, not a scalable architecture
> "I think Ethereum is a proof of concept more than a real, scalable architecture, and it's obvious with the gas fees that we are paying. The shared fee model — all that is just not scalable."
> — [00:27:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=1637s)

**Context:** Explaining why Akash built on Tendermint/Cosmos since 2017; he credits Ethereum with proving product-market fit for decentralized applications.

### [crypto-depin] IBC is the TCP/IP of blockchains
> "The way to scale massive systems is through interoperability... if you think about that, IBC is the TCP/IP of blockchains. It's [an] interconnectivity system, and Akash now is the second chain to connect to IBC."
> — [00:28:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=1721s)

**Context:** On IBC's launch; he predicts cross-chain atomic composability (borrow on one chain, deposit on another) and an "interoperable de-cloud" where one token pays for compute, storage, and DNS across networks.

### [cloud-decentralization] Hyperscalers should offload compute onto Akash
> "You don't see them as competition, you see them as complement... it's actually better for these centralized cloud providers — Amazons and Googles and Microsofts — to use Akash to offload compute. In fact... we've seen some amazing interest in a lot of the big guys."
> — [00:35:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=2154s)

**Context:** Akash as "the cloud of clouds" (Akash = Sanskrit for sky, "where the clouds are formed"); Equinix — ~220 data centers in ~25 countries, supplier to the hyperscalers — had just become a provider.

### [crypto-depin] Cosmos DeFi breakout within four to six months
> "It's only a matter of time — I would give it like maybe four months, maybe six months at tops — that you're going to see incredible products built in Cosmos... you don't have to pay like $1,000 in gas fees to do a lot of the operations."
> — [00:41:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=2500s)

**Context:** Citing Sommelier and Osmosis (pre-launch) and an Emeris/Gravity AMM demo over IBC; Osmosis launched in June 2021, roughly on his timeline.

### [gpu-economics] The peer-to-peer machine learning cloud
> "20% of revenues for these machine learning companies is going to the cloud... We'd love for these companies to install Akash and earn the tokens when they're not using [their servers], and when they want to peak they can use these tokens to actually get compute. This is the use case where the provider becomes a tenant and vice versa, creating a truly peer-to-peer cloud."
> — [00:46:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=2797s)

**Context:** The 2021 GPU roadmap item (a16z report on ML cloud spend cited): ML companies' office GPU servers idle ~21 hours a day; enabling this required solving GPU multi-tenancy. GPUs eventually shipped on Akash in 2023.

### [cloud-decentralization] Goal: cloud power affordable to 10-year-olds within four years
> "I learned how to code when I was 12... and I had to borrow computing. I want to make sure the next generation is not going to find it hard... I want to make it affordable to a degree that 10-year-olds can get cloud power if they want. That's — in four years I'd like to see that."
> — [00:51:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XY33CFNI-OE&t=3102s)

**Context:** His answer to the host's "life goal" question, rooted in growing up on a farm in India without a computer; the quote begins in the [00:50:58] block.
