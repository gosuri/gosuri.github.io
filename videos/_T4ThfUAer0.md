## Summary

A clip from a community discussion (Akash Analysis series) on whether web3 teams should preach decentralization while running on web2 infrastructure. Another speaker (apparently from a validator/project team) opens by saying the web3 tech stack "hasn't caught up" and web2 is a stepping stone, with plans to eventually move on-chain as their own layer-1. Greg responds that his concern isn't web2 infrastructure per se but over-reliance on a single host: he'd rather teams run bare metal they control than Amazon, calls using Amazon for a web3 project "fundamentally wrong" due to over-concentration of power, and reframes Akash as "not really a cloud, it's a market" — deploying to Equinix, OVH, or even someone's home server. He concedes Akash is harder to use and that startups reasonably choose AWS iteration speed, putting the onus on Akash to improve UX; he touts single-command deploys "significantly better than Amazon... even better than the Vercels or the Netlifys," and argues Akash's design (open source, remote signers, developer-chosen security architecture, fewer automated bells and whistles) is more secure than Amazon's, noting Akash had no security events to date.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] Web3 on Amazon is fundamentally wrong
> "If you were to use cloud infrastructure, I'd rather prefer putting your infrastructure in a bare metal server sitting in a secure data center that you have complete control over, and not Amazon. I just think using Amazon for a web three project is just fundamentally wrong. Over-concentration of power."
> — [00:01:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_T4ThfUAer0&t=84s)

**Context:** Responding to a validator team defending web2 infrastructure as a stepping stone; he later repeats "web3 projects should not use Amazon... can use anything else."

### [local-compute] Akash is a market — including someone's home server
> "Akash is not really a cloud, it's a market, right? If you're using Akash you're actually deploying on an Equinix or an OVH or, you know, someone's home server if you choose to. The point is it's not really a single provider but rather you're going to a bunch of bare metal providers."
> — [00:01:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_T4ThfUAer0&t=84s)

**Context:** Recurring framing of Akash as a marketplace spanning tier-1 data centers down to home hardware.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash has a more secure design than Amazon
> "Fortunately we don't have any — haven't had any security event, and I want to keep it that way. I think Akash itself has a much more secure design than Amazon — being open, being hyper focused on selecting the best infrastructures, and also not automating a lot of things people expect but rather giving the flexibility to the developer to choose the security architecture they please."
> — [00:02:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_T4ThfUAer0&t=128s)

**Context:** Answering security concerns about decentralized infrastructure; cites remote signers for validators as an example of forced-secure trade-offs.

### [cloud-decentralization] Single-command deploys better than Amazon, Vercel, Netlify
> "If you look at some of the repos we have, it's a single command deploys, right — from your vanilla repos to getting highly productive and into production within a single command on Akash, which is significantly better than Amazon... in fact even better than like Vercels or the Netlifys."
> — [00:04:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_T4ThfUAer0&t=298s)

**Context:** Conceding AWS wins on iteration speed today, he claims Akash tooling under development will exceed mainstream PaaS UX and "shows you the power of non-custodial systems."
