## Summary

An early (October 2021) long-form podcast aimed at data scientists. Greg explains Akash as the Airbnb of cloud — a marketplace tapping unused data-center capacity versus the "Hilton" consistency of AWS/Google/Microsoft — and pitches its lower prices for ML workloads. Notably dated: Akash was CPU-only at the time, and he says GPU support is about six months out. He sketches a future supply base of edge devices — Ethereum miners freed by proof-of-stake, electric vehicles as "data centers on wheels" with 6-7 high-end GPUs, and the Tesla bot's idle GPUs — all joining Akash permissionlessly.

Much of the episode is career-oriented: data-science use cases on Akash's public chain data (pricing dynamics, deployment lifecycles, staking/escrow analytics, protocol-mining profitability calculators, the community-built Akashalytics dashboard, $100k grants program). He then recounts his path: programming at 12 on a farm in India (splitting DOS games across floppy disks as his "first distributed computer"), IBM consulting, building Kaiser Permanente's first cloud on VMware in 2008 (when using AWS "got you fired" — a pattern he says Akash is now repeating), founding AngelHack (where Firebase launched), discovering containers and Kubernetes, and how Overclock Labs' edge-computing work exposed the unused-capacity problem that became the Akash marketplace — "we accidentally created a blockchain."

Extended advice segments cover optimizing for failure over early winning ("winning is the worst teacher"), startup risk-reward, burnout ("burnout happens when you are working a lot but the outcome is not ideal"), Twitter as the best networking tool, photography as recovery, and sustainability through consistent small wins. He closes calling the moment "a big paradigm shift from centralized architectures to decentralized architectures."

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [gpu-economics] GPU support on Akash coming in about six months
> "Right now Akash supports CPUs only, but in about six months' time you'll start supporting GPUs — the work is underway. What that means is a lower cost GPU-based compute. As you know, GPU is very, very expensive on the cloud, so any cost reduction I think is a big, big win for the data science community."
> — [00:03:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ejhD_lNs4s&t=216s)

**Context:** Answering what Akash offers ML practitioners; dates the GPU marketplace roadmap to roughly spring 2022.

### [local-compute] Cars, robots, and planes as edge cloud supply on Akash
> "If you think of electric vehicles that are essentially data centers on wheels — some of these cars have about six to seven high-end GPUs that could potentially come on Akash. Elon Musk introduced this whole notion of the Tesla bot, and that's sitting on at least four to five GPUs that'll be charged in the evening and has really not using those GPUs — those could be on Akash. So I think we have a great future of unused capacity. The cloud capacity is on the edge, whether it's a robot or a car or a plane — that can all come onto Akash in a permissionless manner."
> — [00:04:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ejhD_lNs4s&t=259s)

**Context:** Early articulation of his edge/idle-device supply thesis; also cites Ethereum's move from proof-of-work to proof-of-stake "unlocking all this excess supply for GPUs."

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash today is like AWS in 2008 — adoption takes time

Not too many people know about Akash, but it's one of those things that takes time for technologies to really show their power."
