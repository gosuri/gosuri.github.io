## Summary

Akash-hosted Twitter Spaces (recorded to YouTube) with host Adam Wozny, Greg, and Overclock Labs CFO Cheng Wang, timed to a genesis token unlock day. Greg explains the two whitepapers (2017 technical, plus a ~4-month AKT economics paper), the fixed 389M supply with 100M at genesis and the rest emitted per-block on a decay curve over decades, and admits the 6-month genesis unlock schedule was "dictated" by an unnamed exchange partner in the 2019 bear market — a design he would replace with continuous release today. He and Cheng rebut unlock FUD at length (naming Flux as paying people to spread it), attribute the earlier $6 AKT price spike to the Osmosis launch rather than marketing, and note the final genesis unlock (March 2023) is team/foundation tokens. Greg lays out AKT's three utilities — governance, bootstrapping/rewards, and reserve — and describes the then-upcoming design where a cloud-specific stablecoin for settlement is minted by burning AKT (inspired by Helium, "very similar to Terra" in mechanism), calling AKT becoming the reserve currency of a $1.1T-by-2027 cloud economy "a bet I'm willing to make." An audience question revives the "Supermini" home supercomputer: Greg says it was postponed a few years because Akash lacked GPU support and home computers couldn't yet earn income against data-center-grade supply, but machine learning workloads will change that — "Supermini is not dead." He closes on Akash as web3's infrastructure layer: Juno, Osmosis, Stargaze validators/RPC on Akash, Cosmos Omnibus surviving a DDoS test, and Akash as data-services provider for DAOs. Dated artifacts: Terra referenced approvingly weeks before its collapse; Testnet 3 just completed, Mainnet 3 ~5 weeks out.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [crypto-depin] AKT as reserve currency of a $1.1T cloud economy
> "Cloud computing market is going to be 1.1 trillion by 2027... if you get one percent market share, which is humongous, Akash token becoming the reserve currency for this global cloud economy is a bet that I'm willing to make."
> — [00:24:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G_pXEAo-ZsU&t=1498s)

**Context:** Closing his explanation of AKT's three utilities (governance, bootstrapping rewards, reserve) with the market-size thesis he repeats across appearances.

### [crypto-depin] Stablecoin settlement minted by burning AKT
> "Settlement will move to a stable coin, and when that happens the way you mint the stable coin is by burning AKT... the more usage, the more adoption Akash gets, the more burning of AKT happens."
> — [00:29:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G_pXEAo-ZsU&t=1798s)

**Context:** Forecasting the burn design later realized as Akash's take-and-burn/USDC settlement; he calls it "the first cloud-specific stable coin to be ever introduced in the market" and cites Helium (and, awkwardly in hindsight, Terra) as inspiration, promising "you're going to see burning... very very soon."

### [local-compute] Home computers will earn income once ML workloads arrive
> "A home computer that's powerful enough can earn income on Akash when we have applications that would leverage such a computer. So machine learning would be the type of application... so we kind of postponed the Supermini to a few years down the line."
> — [00:28:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G_pXEAo-ZsU&t=1713s)

**Context:** Answering why the Supermini home supercomputer was shelved: no GPU support yet and demand skewed to data-center compute; an early (2022) version of the home-GPU-income thesis he later ships as Akash Home Node.

### [local-compute] Supermini is not dead
> "Supermini is not dead. It's just living and waiting for its day to come back."
> — [00:37:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G_pXEAo-ZsU&t=2221s)

**Context:** After riffing on interim uses for a home device — remote signer/"entropy source" for validators, sovereign key custody for cloud data — he keeps the home-compute vision alive pending GPU support and favorable economics.

### [cloud-decentralization] Akash as the de facto standard for web3 infrastructure

**Context:** Citing Juno's website, Osmosis RPC/API servers, and Stargaze validators running on Akash; also predicts Akash becomes "the data services provider for DAOs" since multisig-controlled deployments remove key-man risk.
