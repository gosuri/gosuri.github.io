## Summary

One of Greg's earliest recorded public pitches of Akash (then under Overclock Labs). He frames Akash as "a decentralized and open marketplace for cloud computing resources" offering up to 80% cost reduction versus incumbent clouds, sourced from the ~500,000 data centers worldwide running at ~85% underutilization (caused by planning for peak — his Intuit/Tax Day example). He argues cloud centralization is a societal threat: within two years the majority of internet traffic would come from Amazon, Google, and Microsoft; oligarch cloud providers lock customers in via proprietary "codification" of services and stifle innovation by white-labeling open-source software (his Databricks/Spark vs. Google Dataproc example — "cloud copied this offer" and shut down friends' open-source companies). He explains why Overclock chose the decentralized route over being a centralized broker (legal liability, capital requirements, risk of Amazon suing them out of existence), and describes the architecture: blockchain only for procurement and settlement, actual deployments off-chain in a private peer-to-peer network using containers, with a staking token to align incentives on a permissionless network. Dated details: team "been around for three years," working on the tech four years; goal of an ecosystem where services like Spark or Redis plug in as providers.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [cloud-decentralization] 80% cost reduction vs. incumbent cloud
> "Akash is a decentralized and open marketplace for cloud computing resources, and the value proposition to the customer is to 80% cost reduction than what you would pay for the cloud today."
> — [00:00:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fVVvEFkIaUU&t=45s)

**Context:** Opening pitch of the talk; the cost claim is grounded in tapping idle data center capacity.

### [cloud-decentralization] 500,000 data centers at ~85% underutilization
> "Connecting data centers around the world, underutilized server capacity in data centers around the world — and there're about 500,000 data centers which have about 85 percent of underutilization... the reason for this underutilization is primarily because of planning for peak."
> — [00:01:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fVVvEFkIaUU&t=87s)

**Context:** Quantified supply-side thesis; illustrated with Intuit needing ~95% capacity on Tax Day and ~5% on April 16th.

### [cloud-decentralization] Majority of internet traffic from three providers within two years
> "Effectively in two years majority the internet traffic will come from three providers, and they happen to be Amazon, Google, Microsoft — and this is effectively very threatening to our society because we all know what happens when you have oligarchs control the foundational elements."
> — [00:02:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fVVvEFkIaUU&t=132s)

**Context:** Timed prediction (from April 2018, so by ~2020) used to justify building a decentralized alternative.

### [cloud-decentralization] Clouds stifle open-source innovation
> "Google effectively took what is open source and white-labeled and called their own, and a company that actually developed Spark doesn't get to take the market... I have friends of mine whose companies were shut down — open source companies — because cloud copied this offer."
> — [00:03:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fVVvEFkIaUU&t=220s)

**Context:** His "burgers and fries" argument that clouds monetize open-source services; Databricks/Spark vs. Google Dataproc as the example.
