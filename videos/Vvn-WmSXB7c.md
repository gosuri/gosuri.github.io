## Summary

A Messari Mainnet 2022 panel on getting mainstream corporations into crypto, with Greg Osuri, Mary Gooneratne (Bridgesplit), Rachel Saunders (Apex Crypto), and Paul Brody (EY). Greg recounts the Akash origin: Overclock Labs was founded in 2015 to take Kubernetes to market, and deploying to the edge revealed most edge/data-center compute sat unused, spawning "the world's first open market for unused capacity." Headline claims: Akash costs "about 95% cheaper than your traditional Amazon"; ~20% penetration among Cosmos projects "tracking to about 80% penetration in next 12 months"; and users ranging from the Department of Defense to drug design. On enterprise obstacles, he says non-financial crypto use cases lack web3-native engagement infrastructure — no non-custodial notifications, on-ramps/off-ramps, or even email — causing most churn. His biggest lesson: enterprise sales "has to be bottoms up" — every enterprise deployment started with a curious developer or hackathon participant who championed Akash internally; a top-down pitch of "95% cheaper than Amazon" gets you thrown out as unbelievable (and he concedes Akash lacks capabilities Amazon has). Other panelists: Brody (EY) on risk-averse CFOs ("not many of them will do well in prison"), the failure of private blockchains ("if you want to be on public you've got to start on public"), and EY's ETH-maxi strategy; Saunders on regulated trading infrastructure; Gooneratne on abstracting blockchain value props for institutions. Lightning round on when 50% of the Global 1000 will use blockchain/crypto: Greg says 2027 (most optimistic on the panel); Brody argues 2030 by analogy to cloud adoption curves.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [gpu-economics] "Akash costs about 95% cheaper than your traditional Amazon"
> "Akash as it stands today is the world's first open market for unused capacity... where the benefit is really the first most visible benefit is the cost — I mean, Akash costs about 95% cheaper than your traditional Amazon, and the consumers of this compute interact with Akash in a fully non-custodial and fully permissionless way."
> — [00:03:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vvn-WmSXB7c&t=221s)

**Context:** Self-introduction; the moderator notes he "buried the lede." His most aggressive public cost claim (earlier appearances said 1/3–1/5).

### [crypto-depin] From 20% to 80% Cosmos penetration within 12 months; DoD and drug-design users
> "Right now we have about 20% penetration in Cosmos, tracking to about 80% penetration in next 12 months. We've seen several use cases, [from] Department of Defense to drug design — so we have a wide variety of users right now using Akash."
> — [00:03:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vvn-WmSXB7c&t=221s)

**Context:** Quantified adoption forecast for Cosmos-ecosystem projects hosting on Akash (Akash being a Cosmos SDK chain naturally attracted them).

### [other] Enterprise adoption only works bottom-up, through internal champions
> "You can't have a top-down approach... it really has to be a bottoms-up. It's not a company, it's an ecosystem... Every enterprise we are in right now happened because there was a developer, a hacker, or somebody curious... used the Akash network, participated in one of our hackathons, and took that back to the enterprise and gathered support... If you go top-down and be like 'hey, we're 95% cheaper than Amazon,' people throw you out, because it's unbelievable."
> — [00:13:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vvn-WmSXB7c&t=811s)

**Context:** His "biggest lesson learned" answer; he stresses grassroots community building and consistent communications as the only scalable enterprise channel, and admits Akash can't match all of Amazon's capabilities.

### [crypto-depin] Enterprise churn comes from missing web3-native, non-custodial infrastructure
> "The real challenge is really engaging with enterprise customers in a fully non-custodial way. In a non-custodial system you do not have the observability infrastructure, you do not have the engagement infrastructure... you don't have notifications, for example — how do you really engage a user without an email? There's just a lack of traditional on-ramp, off-ramp, notifications infrastructure to engage with the typical enterprise customer... most of the churn comes from this lack of web3-native infrastructure."
> — [00:07:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vvn-WmSXB7c&t=433s)

**Context:** Answering the biggest-obstacle question for non-financial crypto use cases like cloud computing.

### [crypto-depin] 50% of the Global 1000 on blockchain/crypto by 2027
> "2027."
> — [00:18:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vvn-WmSXB7c&t=1111s)

**Context:** Lightning-round answer to "when will 50% of the world's Global 1000 corporations be using blockchain or crypto?" The most optimistic on the panel — others said five years or 2030 (Paul Brody's cloud-adoption analogy).
