## Summary

Zach from Coinage interviews Greg at the Mainnet conference, a year after naming Akash a crypto project of the year. Greg cites 50% quarter-over-quarter growth in deployed applications, daily spend up 10x from ~$500 to ~$5,000, Nvidia as the only decentralized protocol it uses, and legislators on Capitol Hill recognizing Akash as "the intersection of DePIN and AI." His central thesis: the biggest societal risk is "digital feudalism" — AI, the technology that buys humans time, controlled by a few unelected people (he cites the OpenAI board episode where five people controlled the most powerful AI), with zero transparency into training. He warns whoever discovers AGI has no incentive to share it, and argues sovereignty must return to AI users. On policy, he opposes premature regulation (it breeds regulatory arbitrage favoring capital-rich incumbents) and reports consensus among policymakers for low-touch, damage-triggered response plus scenario-planning risk mitigation. On scale, he's "anxious and excited" about Apple Intelligence bringing a billion AI phones online: Nvidia makes ~750,000 H100s/year but ~8 billion would be needed for per-person AI, with chips obsoleting every two years. He predicts AI will consume 20% of US electricity by 2030, that training clusters scaling from ~100MW (GPT-4) toward 1.2GW mean nuclear reactors (solar can't work due to storage limits; Microsoft's Three Mile Island deal cited), and envisions a world of decentralized data centers, energy grids, and wireless/bandwidth marketplaces. A new product will cut Llama 405B setup from ~5 hours to 15 seconds; he wants his own AI usage to go from every 3-4 hours to every minute, which requires solving inference cost.

## Predictions & Notable Claims

### [decentralized-ai] The biggest risk society faces is digital feudalism
> "The biggest risk as a society we face is the risk of digital feudalism... AI is augmenting intelligence, is really extending how we think and how we work. AI is a way to buy time so we can do more as humans. So we have such an important technology piece that is controlled by so few."
> — [00:05:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P8F-Ac0mitI&t=305s)

**Context:** His flagship framing (also the video's cold open): like feudal lords hoarding critical resources from peasants, a handful of AI companies control access to intelligence.

### [decentralized-ai] Five unelected people controlled the world's most powerful AI
> "We all saw what happened when five people on OpenAI board can effectively control the most powerful AI in the world for however long — for a few days. That is scary because these are not elected officials, these are relatively unknown members. A private corporation can control such a powerful AI — imagine how fragile we are."
> — [00:06:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P8F-Ac0mitI&t=393s)

**Context:** Referencing the November 2023 OpenAI board crisis; he adds there's zero transparency into how models are trained or who benefits.

### [decentralized-ai] Whoever gets AGI first will keep it
> "A bigger question is what happens when we finally discover AGI — there's no incentive for the individual that has AI to share it with the world, because what happens when you have the most powerful AI, natural tendency is for you to keep it to yourself and benefit... versus sharing the technology. That's something I think is super risky — that's the future we have if we don't look at how to decentralize or bring the sovereignty to the people that actually use AI."
> — [00:07:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P8F-Ac0mitI&t=436s)

**Context:** Argument for decentralizing the full AI stack, starting with compute ("come and take it" GPU shirt referenced by host).

### [local-ai] A billion AI phones are coming and the world lacks infrastructure — 8 billion H100s needed
> "I'm anxious about the Apple's AI production and also excited — anxious because a billion phones coming online with AI. I don't think we have the infrastructure to scale... the globe doesn't have infrastructure to scale. For context, Nvidia makes about 750,000 H100s a year. We need one H100 per person — that's about 8 billion H100s to have every second connectivity... and this technology evolves so fast that H100 now will go out of fashion in two years."
> — [00:15:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P8F-Ac0mitI&t=913s)

**Context:** On Apple Intelligence's launch; he frames global consumer AI demand as an enormous compute-supply opportunity that requires solving inference cost.

### [energy] By 2030, 20% of US electricity will be consumed by AI
> "The energy needs to catch up... I think the prediction now is by 2030, 20% of US electricity production will be consumed by AI. So we don't have infrastructure to even power that power requirement — we need to build nuclear reactors."
> — [00:15:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P8F-Ac0mitI&t=959s)

**Context:** Immediately followed by his vision of decentralized data centers (Akash), decentralized energy grids that trade power, and decentralized wireless/bandwidth marketplaces.

### [energy] Training compute is scaling to gigawatt clusters — it is going to be nuclear
> "GPT-4 was trained I believe on a 100 megawatt capacity data center, and that grows to about [2]50 megawatt capacity for the newer model... and the next tier is 1.2 gigawatt. When you get to 1.2 gigawatt, that means it's a nuclear reactor — nuclear reactor produces 1 gigawatt. We can't do solar... we just don't have enough storage capacity for solar. So it is going to be nuclear, and Microsoft just bought a nuclear reactor in Pennsylvania."
> — [00:17:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P8F-Ac0mitI&t=1043s)

**Context:** He says power needed for AI grows by roughly half an order of magnitude per year, and notes Oracle's reactor plans and banks financing nuclear — "such an opportunity right now to decentralize power."
