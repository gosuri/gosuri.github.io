## Summary

Long-form interview with host Ruper Pickering on the All In Crypto podcast. Greg recounts Akash's origin: the 2013 "renaissance of cloud," his low-latency HPC background at Demandbase, and the Cornell "supercloud" paper he turned into a 2017 whitepaper before launching in 2020. He credits Akash with bootstrapping Venice AI, Nous Research's Hermes agent, Prime Intellect, and Brev (acquired by Nvidia). He frames Big Cloud as "techno-feudalism" — cloud companies as kingmakers (Amazon choosing Anthropic), an "invisible tax" where half of every dollar paid to an online service goes to a cloud provider — and positions Akash not as a competitor but as a distribution channel ("Airbnb for compute") that Google/Amazon/Azure will eventually join. The middle section is a detailed walk through the AI supply-chain crisis: only ~2.4M people use agents (vs ~1B AI users, 80M programmers), agents make ~1000x the inference calls of chat, used H100s have doubled in price, HBM memory costs 4x, turbine lead times are 14 years and transformers 4 years, and by 2028-29 training frontier models will need gigawatt (nuclear-reactor-scale) data centers. His answer: distributed training on heterogeneous compute, taking compute to where power is, and moving inference into homes — his own solar can host "eight H100 nodes" and the waste heat warms the house. He pitches Akash Home Node (home.node.akash.network, Razer partnership, ~$0.20/hr per 5090, ~8-month payback), AKT's burn-mint mechanics, and Overclock Labs' pivot to a profitable enterprise business with multi-million-dollar contracts so the network doesn't rely on token sales. Notably dated: references to GLM 5.2 rivaling Opus 4.6, a US government "shutdown" of an AI model, and AkashML doing ~10B tokens/day.
