## Summary

Akash Weekly Twitter Spaces hosted by Nadia Bajuelo with Greg Osuri and guest Kaori Hero (co-founder, MASQ Network). Greg's updates lead with a U.S. Department of Defense website running on Akash — brought in by community member Anthony Rosa with Space Force airmen/guardians, winning a "most tactically relevant for cyber operations" category alongside Secret Network — which Greg frames as validation that governments must reduce reliance on centralized "choke points" like Amazon. Mainnet 4 is coming in ~3 weeks with interchain accounts (enabling private deployments from Secret Network), IP leasing, and provider client 2.0. Other updates: validator by23 and Leap wallet joining the ecosystem, Chia's blog on using Akash (a community member paid $41 for 430 Chia plots), Spheron running Akash workshops across India, and a free-hosting program for projects banned by Hetzner. Previewing a Messari spaces talk, Greg argues the centralized cloud is "a kill switch," citing a16z's "cost of cloud" analysis: ~50% of what we pay for online services flows to cloud providers — "half of every dollar... goes to Uncle Jeff" — an invisible rent-seeker, and warns the centralized cloud could "ban decentralization." Kaori presents MASQ ("if VPN and Tor had a baby"), a web3 privacy browser whose users earn by sharing bandwidth, deployed on Akash via Cloudmos; they compare MASQ to Sentinel and Brave. Greg riffs on web3 developer experience (Twitter shutting down its APIs vs. permissionless building), envisions a fully verifiable software supply chain from code to Akash deployment, and answers token-unlock questions: 20M AKT unlocking Sept 25 (8.6M to seed investors who bought at $0.15), the last investor unlock, with Overclock willing to buy back tokens at "ridiculously low" $0.30.

> "We anticipated this level of involvement long time ago. We've been arguing the fact that open technologies are the future because they reduce reliance... think about how much money and time investment goes into Amazon's really centralized systems that have a lot of control and more often than not become choke points for combat operations or anything strategically important. It's important for our government to operate without reliance on closed technologies."
> — [00:02:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ckp4eQ94ojM&t=174s)

**Context:** On the U.S. DoD website deployed to Akash via a community member. Greg adds later ([00:14:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ckp4eQ94ojM&t=867s)): "if Akash is good enough for a U.S. Department [of] Defense website, I think Akash is good enough for a lot of folks."

### [cloud-decentralization] Decentralization will take center stage
> "The most important thing from a project standpoint is for us to keep building, keep shipping features, keep improving the system in a manner that it opens up a lot more use cases... and before you know it, the decentralization is going to be in the center stage."

**Context:** Describing web3 network effects "that are bound to go up" after the DoD win.

### [cloud-decentralization] The centralized cloud is a kill switch
> "As we know, crypto right now is on a major assault by censorship as well as outright banning... I'm going to go on talking about why decentralization is important — [Hetzner] in the cloud basically is a kill switch."
> — [00:12:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ckp4eQ94ojM&t=779s)

**Context:** Previewing his Messari spaces talk on base-layer decentralization, after Hetzner banned crypto workloads (captions garble "Hetzner"); Akash offered free hosting to affected projects.

### [cloud-decentralization] Half of every dollar paid for cloud services goes to "Uncle Jeff"
> "50 [percent] on average [of] all the costs — the margins — actually goes to the big cloud... to the cloud providers. It's incredible. So half of every dollar we pay to cloud services, all of us, goes to Uncle Jeff... the rent seeking model that was created, the invisible rent seeker."
> — [00:13:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ckp4eQ94ojM&t=824s)

**Context:** Citing a16z's "cost of cloud" post; services like Notion, Asana, and Netflix pass ~50% of costs through to hyperscalers. Decentralization, he argues, also "reduce[s] the cost of the globe... in these high inflation times."

### [cloud-decentralization] The centralized cloud could decide to ban decentralization
> "Then we have this bigger problem, which is the centralization of the cloud that... can decide one day to ban decentralization, right? And it's [at] a very critical juncture right now."
> — [00:14:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ckp4eQ94ojM&t=867s)

**Context:** Framing censorship risk to crypto's base layer — the argument for sovereign infrastructure, made days after Hetzner's crypto ban.

### [other] Web3's edge: open source software that ships with its own network
> "The big difference between the open systems that are prominent so far versus web3 is that web3 comes with functionality, whereas open systems don't come with functionality... with something like web3, you use an open source software, it comes [with] the network capability where you can fully realize the power of the software."
> — [00:37:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ckp4eQ94ojM&t=2279s)

**Context:** Contrasting Docker (needs a cloud to be useful) with web3 protocols; part of his answer on how developer experience drives web3 adoption.

### [other] Web3 will go places web2 can't even come close to
> "If we can improve the developer experience, especially around interoperability, I think this is going to go places where web 2... can't even come close. So I'm very, very confident on where we're going."
