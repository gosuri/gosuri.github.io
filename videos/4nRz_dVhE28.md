## Summary

Fireside chat at Akash Accelerate 2024 with Greg as interviewer and Erik Voorhees as guest. Greg frames Voorhees's arc as extending "separation of money and state" to "separation of state and intelligence." Voorhees recounts his history (SatoshiDice in 2012, ShapeShift after Mt. Gox) and explains why he built Venice AI on top of open-source models — much of its compute runs on Akash — arguing centralized AI subjects users to both corporate whims and regulators, and imagining a world 5-15 years out where governments use AI systems to control how people think. Voorhees's central thesis: open source (Llama 3) has caught up to centralized models, but the ecosystem faces a "honeymoon period" risk — if Meta and others stop releasing frontier open models before efficient decentralized training is solved, "we have a possibly very dystopian future"; solving decentralized training is the most important problem in the space. Greg responds that he is very positive on decentralized training progress, citing research papers (DiLoCo) proposing training on heterogeneous, heavily distributed compute. In audience Q&A on the biggest risk Akash faces, the answer given is regulation: Akash is still early enough to be shut down by regulation, but if regulation stays out of the way, "Akash is unstoppable."
